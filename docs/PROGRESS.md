# digitus2 — status

This file holds the project's current state: where things are, what lives outside the repository, the owner's standing rules, the decisions that constrain future work, known limitations and what is next. The owner's requests and planned work, open and closed, are [GitHub issues](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues). Keep this file current when work lands.

The app is public at https://marchalmaxim.github.io/digitus2/ (GitHub Pages, deployed from `main` on every push by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`). The repository is https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2 (MIT).

## Where things are

The phone score camera works like Google PhotoScan, rebuilt for [#143](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/143): live advice on every frame (dark, blurry), a shutter that always answers, the phone's light where the browser offers it, five photographs taken whenever the phone pauses somewhere new while the reader sweeps it over the page ([#149](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/149)), each one shown by a dot, a flash and a pop ([#156](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/156)), tracking in a worker that keeps up on slow phones, and a precision-weighted fusion that checks each view's registration before it may add detail. Behaviour, measurements and limits are in `docs/scanning.md`; real optics on Android and iPhone remain the owner's device check.

A beginner can play along on their own digital piano, USB or Bluetooth, over Web MIDI ([#182](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/182)): one quiet line under `Open your own score` turns it on (only where the browser has Web MIDI), and in the score view the red waits on the next group of notes and moves on at the press of all of them, as a partner would ([#204](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/204)); when the next note is more than a beat away, a thin ring around it closes in over the time to it; the reader's held keys show on the on-screen keyboard, filling over each note's length ([#185](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/185)), and no sound is made for them. Playing along makes no sound: the reader's piano is the only instrument, and with one hand chosen in the Practice panel the walk asks for that hand's notes alone. Behaviour, budgets and limits are in `docs/app.md` (MIDI input, Library, Playing along on a piano, One hand); the plans are `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-23-play-along.md`, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-23-hold-cue.md` and `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-25-play-along-halo.md`. A `Practice mode` switch at the head of the Practice panel, shown only while a piano is connected and on by default, keeps the piano connected and lets Play play as normal when it is off ([#191](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/191)). While the reader walks a piece on the piano, digitus compares their rhythm with the written one against their own pulse, and after each bar a thin gray ring marks the note that came most clearly early (before it) or late (after it); a hesitation is not judged, and Play only plays ([#192](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/192); `docs/app.md`, Timing in the walk; the plan is `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-25-walk-timing.md`). The windows wait for the owner's piano (`docs/research/play-along-timing.md`). A whole piece on the owner's Roland GO:PIANO over Bluetooth, with the Windows flag set, remains the owner's device check on the issue.

**PIG, in plain words.** The Piano Fingering Dataset (Nakamura, Saito and Yoshii, Kyoto University): 150 classical piano excerpts where pianists wrote the fingering of every note, several pianists per piece; the standard benchmark of the fingering literature. The engine's main scorecard is how often its finger matches the human one on PIG's 30 test pieces. Its licence allows non-profit research use but not redistribution, so it is never committed and nothing fitted on it ships. Without it, `pnpm verify` reports the benchmark and hands steps as SKIP ("dataset missing") and the floors go unchecked: a green run without the data is not a full verification.

**The verification loop, in plain words.** `pnpm verify` is the definition of done: one command, fifteen steps in a row, and a change counts only when all pass. The ordinary steps are typecheck, lint, unit tests, a production build, a bundle-size budget and Playwright end-to-end tests at desktop and phone widths. The project-specific ones are a repository-size check, a colour guard (black, white, one red), the scanning harness tests, the scan service's Python tests (`omr:service`, SKIP without Python), the scanning acceptance gate (`omr:acceptance`, SKIP without the recognisers), and three benchmark gates that score the engine against the floors in `benchmarks/floor.json`: a change that makes the fingerings worse than the floor fails even when every test passes. CI runs most of the same checks on GitHub. Details: `docs/verification-loop.md`.

## Standing rules from the owner

All scanner implementation paths have returned to the coordinator; no concurrent source edits are active. Private photographs and generated acceptance evidence stay under ignored `build/`. Only the coordinator runs git.

`CLAUDE.md` carries the fixed decisions and the full wording of the UI rules.

- **Models.** Every coding stream runs plan, build, review, fix: Opus 5.5 at medium effort writes the plan, Sonnet 5.5 builds and fixes, Opus 5.5 reviews adversarially before a merge; Sonnet 5.5 also does the mechanical work (web searches, `pnpm verify` and Playwright runs that only report results, screenshots); Fable coordinates, diagnoses and does the final review. Use workflows where parallel work helps. In Codex sessions: gpt-6.1-sol agents on bounded, independent tasks, with independent verification of the actual output.
- **Verification.** Iterate with `pnpm verify --changed` (only the steps the diff against origin/main touches); one full `pnpm verify` before the pull request, and green CI, is the definition of done. Readability for intermediate players is a first-class metric. A qualitative UI review by an Opus subagent (verification spec 3.10) accompanies UI work.
- **UI minimalism.** "Make sure that the UI stays very pristine and not cluttered. I very much like it right now. Do not overclutter it with features and be brutal with this." Controls appear only in the context that needs them (progressive disclosure).
- **Merging.** Never merge into `main` without the owner's explicit confirmation. Work goes on a branch and a pull request; the owner merges.
- **Requests.** Every request or bug report the owner posts becomes a GitHub issue with the owner's words, a judgement, the agreed approach and a checklist. The pull request that does it closes the issue; a request the owner rejects is closed with the reason.
- **This file.** Keep it current; it is what a new session reads first.

## Decisions

### Licensing and data

- **PIG never ships.** Its licence permits non-profit academic use only and forbids redistribution. The dataset, PIG-fitted weights (`research/weights/`) and PIG-derived hand statistics stay research-only. Asking the PIG maintainers for permission is the owner's decision; without it the shipped weights cannot reach FingerFlow's 68.6.
- **Shipped weights come from open data only:** the textbook oracle, Beringer (via pydactyl, MIT) and the train half of the Mutopia editorial corpus. Mutopia is split 70/30 by piece (seed 20261013); the test half is a held-out oracle.
- **Datasets whose licence forbids redistribution are never committed** (MIT, non-commercial project). Per-dataset licences are in `research/data/README.md`.

### Engine and weights

- **Training runs in TypeScript** through the engine CLI with the engine's own decoder and term functions, so features exist once; Python keeps data, splits and statistics.
- **The CLI and the app solve with `RECOMMENDED_WEIGHT_SET` (`learned-open`);** the solver's own default stays `parncutt`, which the textbook floors are measured with. The benchmark gate runs the set named in `benchmarks/floor.json` (`weightSet`, `weightSetHash`): another set, or a retrained set whose floors were not re-measured, is a breach.
- **Calibration correctness is per annotator** (soft labels): PIG validation has 1.2 annotators per piece and test 5, and the any-annotator measure did not transfer (ECE 0.063 on validation, 0.243 on test).
- **The consistent-repeats pass is on by default:** identical passages get the same fingering (repeated bars fingered differently was a teacher objection in the first UI review). It was accepted although it changed 25 of the 30 core pieces under `parncutt`.
- **Hand separation priors** come from the library and the Mutopia train half with equal weight per corpus: pooled counts let the larger Mutopia corpus dominate and mis-split Twinkle. A PIG-fitted variant scored 91.76 against the shipped 92.05, so nothing PIG-derived is needed.
- **Ornaments are a practice default, not a historical interpretation:** trills alternate with the upper neighbour, short and long mordents take 3 and 5 attacks within the written duration, respecting explicit markings and the key; the keyboard follows the realised pitches; auxiliary notes get no invented fingering.

### Benchmarks and floors

- **Floors** (`benchmarks/floor.json`): a milestone's measurement minus 0.5, rounded down, raised in the same commit as the improvement with a row in `benchmarks/history.md`. The ECE maximum is the measurement plus 0.02, capped at 0.10. The lint maximum may loosen only on forced-error evidence (extra errors only in hands with no legal fingering). A floor is never lowered without a recorded reason here and in floor.json's note.
- **`pnpm verify` never rewrites a tracked file;** only labelled milestone runs replace `benchmarks/latest*.json` (`docs/verification-loop.md`).
- **The `core30` benchmark set:** `content/manifest.json` entries carry `benchmarkSet` (`core30` on the first 30 pieces, null on later ones; a missing field means core30), selected by `benchmarkEntries()` in `packages/engine/src/library/benchmarkSet.ts`. Every library-based number (hands floor, repeat consistency, the engine's library tests, `make_hand_motion`, teacher-review round-trips) measures core30, so growing the library never moves a floor.
- **The PIG test split (pieces 001–030) is scored only for the record.** Hypotheses are tested on train and validation; the teacher review's hard-error passages come from validation pieces only.
- **The teacher review is never a gate or a tuning target;** its hypotheses go to `docs/experiments.md`.
- **Readability** (`docs/research/readability.md`, verification spec 3.6): teaching editions print 32% of digits, library pieces a median 18%. The engine's reduction (`packages/engine/src/readability/`, [#94](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/94)) is the greedy forward pass over the player model R0–R5 plus first notes and pins, cut to a budget: the level target (20, 25 or 30 percent of notes) and never more digits than the editor prints on the same piece (`editorDigits` in the manifest), removing the digit whose absence costs the reader least. The confidence trigger is off by default (the engine's confidence is below 0.6 on most notes). The gate holds the reader's reconstruction of the engine's fingering per level group (`benchmarks/floor.json`, `readability`); numbers in `docs/research/readability.md` section 7.4.
- **Metrics are cross-checked:** the Python scorer in `research/fingering_eval/` matches the official C++ evaluator (1,478 pairs), and the TypeScript metrics match the Python scorer.

Engravings and arrangements are public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA; CC BY and CC BY-SA pieces are credited in the app (typesetter, licence, Mutopia id from `content/manifest.json`). Scarborough Fair uses the traditional Dorian tune.
- **Archive lanes (PDMX), the owner's broad copyright reading:** a score is admitted on the uploader's declaration of public domain or CC0 with PDMX's `license_conflict` false; there is no author-date check. The songs lane (pop, rock, soundtrack, jazz, blues) therefore ships modern arrangements in a public repository on that declaration: a stated risk the owner accepted. Only content exclusions apply (junk, Nazi-era and propaganda pieces; `content/tools/pdmx-excluded.json`); any single reviewer's flag removes a candidate.
- **Non-public-domain scores copied from the web are refused** (unauthorised copies, public repository). The legitimate routes are a private local folder and a CC-licensed lane (#37).
- **Gates:** every piece passes the XSD, the span check, notes on both staves and zero lint law errors at the medium and large profiles. The core 30 are exempt from the law check. Level 6 may relax the span and lint gates within a ceiling (40 law errors per 1,000 notes at each profile, 20 unplayable slices per 1,000), with the flags recorded (`relaxedGates` in the catalog, a sentence in the piece's notes, checked by `gates.py`), because the app's product is the fingering. Bulk Mutopia editions take the same relaxed gates and ceiling at grade 6 by a stated rule (`content/tools/mutopia_advanced.py`: concert repertoire) and keep both MIDI checks.
- **Levels:** the frozen heuristic proposes grades 1–5; grade 6 comes from a stated rule (`content/tools/pdmx_advanced.py`), which both archive lanes take, or, for a bulk Mutopia edition released under the relaxed gates, from the Mutopia rule (`content/tools/mutopia_advanced.py`); a grade-6 song stays on its Songs or Modern shelf. The UI shows four levels (grades 1–2, 3, 4, 5–6); the manifest keeps the grade.
- **Scoped relaxation for four composers (issue #80, owner, 2026-10-19):** "relax the ceiling", "loosen the advanced rule, but no more beginner songs". For archive pieces credited to Liszt, Tchaikovsky, Rachmaninoff or Prokofiev, a proposal of 4 is advanced at any score with fast notes from 4 a second, under a ceiling of 60 law errors and 30 slices per 1,000 notes; a proposal of 3 or below is never advanced. Every other composer keeps the archive rule and ceiling. Pieces credited "NA" are not covered (#48).
- **Archive caps and the note ceiling:** the caps by rating (1,300 classical pieces, 1,000 songs) hold grades 1–4 only; a passing piece at grade 5 or 6 always ships, and within a cap a shipped piece keeps its place, so a larger pool never removes one. A score may have up to 25,000 notes (`pdmx_screen.MAX_NOTES`), the longest tested in the app: the complete Chopin Études Op. 25 opens in 16 s at phone width and plays. The 1.5 MB score limit applies to the stored gzip file.
- **Dedupe compares notes, not titles** (`content/tools/dedupe.py`); a curated piece is never removed, and the same tune at another level stays.
- **Batch harvesting and automated checks come before per-piece agent investigation** (the owner's correction on throughput and cost). Downloaded candidates are not library entries until they pass the gates.
- **Storage:** archive scores are gzip (`scores/<slug>.musicxml.gz`, byte-identical on a rebuild). The app fetches the compact `content/library-index.json` before the first render, loads the full manifest and the score-file map (`apps/web/src/library/scoreFiles.ts`) lazily, so the start-up bundle does not grow with the library. `readScoreFile` detects gzip by its signature bytes, because servers may already have decoded a `.gz` file sent with `Content-Encoding: gzip`.
- **Repository budgets** (`scripts/check-repo-size.mjs`): 120 MB of trackable files (100 MB until the note ceiling was lifted), 1 MB per file and 1.5 MB per score, with explicit allowances for the manifest, verification records and archive catalog fragments.

### App and UI

- **GitHub Pages only:** public, static, no VPS, no login. The one server is the scan recognition service on Cloud Run (`docs/scan-service.md`); the shared library's store is Firestore, which runs no code of ours (`docs/shared-library.md`); the app is deployed nowhere else. The auth study stays as reference (`docs/research/auth-options.md`). Mobile is the responsive web app, installable as a PWA; no native app.
- **One red element on screen;** it may scroll out of view. The red travelling between Play and the cursor counts as the one red; the note burst's double-click accent is a deliberate exception.
- **Pause keeps the resume point red;** Play is plain while paused away from the start.
- **Hand letters on the keyboard only in bars where the hands come within a fifth** (a per-onset octave rule would toggle 41 times in the Minuet), with no phone exception.
- **Seeking (T13): the score is the timeline.** No scrub bar or progress line. A click or tap anywhere in a bar, notes and digits included, goes to its start; Left and Right step a bar, Shift four, Home rewinds; seeks stay inside a loop. Stopped, the bar is cued: its first note is red and Play starts there. The red hops to the bar, and while playing the music waits for it to land (220 to 340 ms). When note editing (M5) arrives it must take clicks on notes over from the seek.
- **Disclosure thresholds** live in `apps/web/src/library/disclosure.ts` (level pills and shelves above 40 pieces, search from 100, collection headings above 12, length pills above 40).
- **Motion durations come from the tokens** and reduced motion turns them off. The theme switch spreads the new theme as a circle from the switch (View Transitions API, 400 ms); browsers without view transitions change instantly.
- **Dark mode** follows `prefers-color-scheme` until the switch overrides it. Score ink is `#d4d4d4`: the owner found near-white notes too bright and `#c4c4c4` too grey.
- **Favourites (#78)** are library slugs, newest first, in the settings in `localStorage`; a piece is marked with the heart on its score page (the style shelves' rows have none) and taken off there or with the filled heart on its Favourites row, and the `Favourites` shelf stands on the arrival page only (a search or an opened shelf takes it away). A favourite stays among a shelf's samples too: leaving it out of the draw would change the samples of the shelf the reader came from the moment a heart is pressed. Back restores the list by its anchor: the remembered offset moves by as much as `.library-list` moved, so a change above the list (Favourites, My scores) is compensated, one inside it is not.
- **The score view widens with the window** (`clamp(72rem, 100% - 8rem, 120rem)`): nothing moves up to 1,280 px, and the keyboard fits a five-octave piece on a wide screen.
- **Bundle budget:** the library page's initial JavaScript and CSS stay under 150 KB gzip; Verovio stays a lazy chunk.
- **Digitus does not play the hand left out.** Playing along makes no sound; Play plays the whole piece. Measured with a microphone on the owner's GO:PIANO over Bluetooth MIDI, a note the app sounded at the reader's press reached the ear about 89 ms after the reader's own (`docs/app.md`, One hand, has the parts), a flam on every shared beat that nothing in the app removes short of guessing the next press. The owner: "89 ms is not worth fighting; let's drop the other hand from play-along." The test hooks also install with `?hooks=1`, with the real instrument.
- **The piano line says it is looking for the piano** when a granted request stays pending for 2 s (Chrome's WinRT backend with a Bluetooth piano off, [#182](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/182)).
- **Playing along on the reader's own piano ([#182](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/182)) is Web MIDI input only, and the app makes no sound for the reader's notes.** The red waits as a cue in performance seconds (`Playback.cueAt` and `finish`), never as a written seek: a written second enters a bar going back at its last hearing, so a walk made of seeks would loop a repeat's first pass for ever. The existing cue look, page turn, finish and flight home are reused unchanged, and the one control is the Practice mode switch in the Practice panel ([#191](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/191)), which gates the walk alone: off, the walk takes its inactive path as with no piano, and the piano stays connected and its keys still show. A step passes at the press of all its keys ([#204](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/204)). The reader's held keys fill to show each note's length ([#185](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/185), `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-23-hold-cue.md`), in the bar's own gray, with the number on it in `--key-on-level`. A key used by one step never counts again (a repeated pitch needs a fresh press), a chain of steps already held moves the red once, and any move the walk did not make uses every held key up. The press of the home line is the consent: opening the page asks the browser nothing, and a later visit opens MIDI silently only when the setting is on and the browser's permission already answers `granted`. The browser's permission is never cleared by Turn off. The byte parser and `MIDIAccess` are one lazy chunk (1.2 KB gzip), fetched only when the reader presses the line or a later visit is already allowed; the store, the walk and the keyboard's bars are small and static. The reader's held keys are a gray bar along each key's front edge in three pure-gray tokens (one for each key it can lie on, a dimmed key's bar drawn outside the dimming), never red. Chrome on Windows lists no Bluetooth MIDI port without `chrome://flags/#use-winrt-midi-api` set to Enabled (measured with a Roland GO:PIANO on Chrome 156), while USB needs nothing, so when no piano is found the line names the flag in one sentence in Chrome and in Edge on Windows (Edge's own flag address; Edge itself is not measured); Safari and iOS have no Web MIDI and show no line.
- **Timing in the waiting walk ([#192](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/192)).** The red moves at the press; no control is added and Play judges nothing. Onsets are the run starts of the steps the walk passed, at the key's event time; each gap is compared with the written gap at the reader's own tempo (a local median ratio), so any speed works and steady latency cancels; a hesitation, and every move the walk did not make, breaks the chain and is not judged. A bar is judged when the walk leaves it: one gray ring before (early) or after (late) its most uneven note; a bar's rings go when it is played again, and all go at Play, a new score, a hands change, Practice mode off or the piano gone. The windows are provisional.
- **The red moves at the press, and nothing waits on a hold ([#204](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/204)).** The hold gate of [#185](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/185) is gone: no wake-up timer, no four-fifths rule, no hint for a longer hold, no deferred hand-over. The time until the next note shows at the red note itself, as a halo that is part of the one red element and that nothing waits on.
- **Piano samples are self-hosted:** they are the release asset `piano-samples-v1` (452 files: the 226 samples in Ogg Opus and in AAC, because smplr skips Ogg on Safari), stored under safe names (`PP C#1` as `PP_Cs1`, mapped by a smplr storage shim that also rejects anything not audio). `scripts/piano-samples.lock.json` is the single pin (tag, size, SHA-256, file list); the Pages workflow unpacks the asset into the site, because release downloads send no CORS header. The repository never contains the samples.
- **The service worker is hand-written** (`apps/web/src/sw/`, no dependency): Workbox's file globs cannot cleanly leave out the thousands of score files and its default 2 MiB file limit drops the Verovio worker. Navigations go to the network first (4 s), then the cached shell; the precache (at most 24 MiB) is filled on install; other hashed files and the piano samples are kept cache first as they are fetched. No automatic `skipWaiting`: a new worker waits until every window has closed, so an open page never loses its chunks; only the reader's Reload on the `A new version is ready.` notice ends the wait ([#156](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/156)). Service workers are blocked in every e2e spec but `pwa`, so `page.route` sees every request. No install button: the browser's own install UI.
- **Rendering:** Verovio keeps the engine's note ids and tags every fingering digit with `data-startid`; one CSS class turns one note or digit red; cursor sync through `getOutputTimestamp()` is within 5 ms.
- **No translucent group of notes in the score (#79):** the hand left out is dimmed shape by shape (fill and stroke opacity), never with `opacity` or a filter on a group that holds transformed shapes, which Chrome composites one layer per group (2,085 on Kuhlau's Rondo, paid by every repaint); a dimmed digit rests at a group opacity, which costs no layer and keeps gray smoothing. A hand switch fades only the systems on screen and never animates `opacity`; the pages off screen skip rendering (`content-visibility: auto`, each keeping its viewBox's aspect ratio). Measurements: `docs/research/hand-switch-performance.md`.

### Scanning

- **Guided multi-page camera (#131, #143, #149):** the camera entry has its own line with the camera symbol, under the file chooser. The viewfinder measures every frame (brightness, a content-independent blur estimate, glare, the page outline) and says what to fix first; the camera's one red element is never a disabled control. A press takes the sharpest frame of a short burst; four views are then taken by themselves whenever the phone pauses, sharp, somewhere new (#149), with no dots to aim at. Frame analysis runs in a worker with pause windows that adapt to the phone's speed. Fusion is per pixel and precision weighted (a static Kalman update): a weighted-median base outvotes glare and shadow, detail comes only from views whose registration a per-tile correlation confirms, a mark seen in one non-reference view is not painted, and a clear reference mark keeps its depth. It is judged by a ground-truth oracle, sixteen safety tests and a homr benchmark against the old rule and PhotoScan's documented soft minimum (`research/omr/camera-fusion.mjs`). Corners are found automatically when the page contrasts with its background and stay adjustable in the review. Calibrated 3D geometry and full-resolution stills are not used.

- **Capture progress is five dots, a flash and a pop, never marks on the page ([#156](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/156)).** The camera's header shows five dots under `Photo n of 5`, one per photo, filled white as each is taken. Every photo flashes the picture, and each of the first four pops the circle and says `Photo n of 5 taken.` for about a second before the sweep sentence returns; the fifth fills the fifth dot, and the dots stay until the review opens. The page stays clean, the circle stays the one red element, and reduced motion drops the flash and the pop but keeps the dots.
- **The loop on the PC is the definition of done for camera and scan changes ([#156](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/156)).** Two specs carry it, `scan-grand-staff` in `pnpm verify`'s `e2e` step and `scan-camera-loop` in its own `e2e:loop` step after it: `scan-grand-staff` takes the owner's three recognised pages (one read as a treble and a bass part) through the review and **Use for fingerings** in Chromium and WebKit and requires a grand staff with both hands fingered; `scan-camera-loop` sweeps five photos of a real page through the camera, has local homr read the fused page and the flat photo, compares the fused page's facts with the manifest, then reviews and fingers it. A camera or scan change counts only after both have run, the loop with homr present (`DIGITUS2_OMR_HOMR`); a skipped loop is not a pass, as with the benchmark gates. Details: `docs/scanning.md`.
- **A scan can be handed over whole, and the build is visible ([#156](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/156)).** **Download everything** in the review saves the pages as the scanner received them, every recognition, the corrected copy and `scan.json` (warnings, recognizer, time, corrections, build, browser) as one ZIP, through the share sheet where the browser can share the file. The build, `Build <short commit> · <day>`, ends About and **Recognition details**, and a waiting newer build is offered on the notice line with **Reload**; the default stays that a new worker waits until every window has closed.
- **The review reads as a verdict ([#158](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/158)):** one line of facts under the title (pages, bars, hands, places to check) or one blocking sentence with its action; the red on the single next action; the thumbnails are the page/whole-score switch; no accuracy disclaimers in the app or its docs, guarded by `src/i18n/noDisclaimers.test.ts`.

- **Recognition runs on the project's scan service on Google Cloud Run** (#62; `docs/scan-service.md`, design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-scan-service-design.md`): `europe-west4`, request-based billing, 4 vCPU and 8 GiB, one recognition at a time, 0 to 1 instances, no keep-warm ping and no minimum instance; one warm-up health request when the scan page opens. Every device scans with one **Scan this score**, phones included; the scan page says where the page goes in one sentence, and the press is the consent. The app has no PC worker; the loopback worker is the research server and the service's engine. Recognition in the browser is not built (oemer there took 21 minutes for Twinkle; a homr port is unmeasured), and vendor OMR services are rejected (sign-ups, per-page credits, the score on their servers).
- **The spend is capped at 10 a month:** a budget in the billing account's currency on the service's own project, alerts at 50, 90 and 100 percent, and Google's kill-switch function that disables billing at the budget. Scanning then stays off until the month resets or the owner raises the budget and relinks; nothing resumes automatically.
- **The service keeps nothing:** each page is recognised in memory and deleted with its result when the job ends; one log line per job (kind, bytes, seconds, outcome), never content or addresses; 30 recognitions per caller per hour; no secret ships in the static bundle. PDFs go with their original bytes to Audiveris (measurably better recognition); photos go to a pinned homr (AGPL-3.0, run on the server, never bundled into the MIT app).
- **Scanning ships as experimental:** the owner accepts a useful first release for clear photos of printed scores. Source-versus-recognition review comes before any use for fingerings, the raw MusicXML can be downloaded for correction elsewhere, and the app makes no accuracy claim. No hand-written replacement XML or unreviewed preprocessing ships. Uploaded scores stay in the browser (IndexedDB "My scores"), nothing is published automatically (only **Share** sends a score's MusicXML to the shared library), and private photos and recognition outputs are never committed.
- **A pre-moderated shared library of uploads** ([#132](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/132), superseding #41): readers share a score anonymously from My scores with a one-sentence copyright notice and one tick; nothing is public until the owner approves it in the Firebase console; anyone can report a shared score, and removal deletes it. The store is Firestore in its own Google Cloud project with no billing account, so it cannot cost money; when abuse spends its quota, the shared library goes quiet and scanning is untouched (`docs/shared-library.md`; options weighed in `docs/research/shared-library-options.md`).

## Known limitations and open findings

Engine:

- With open data the engine trails FingerFlow's learned weights clearly and FHMM3 within noise; closing the gap needs PIG-fitted weights (the owner's decision above).
- On PIG validation `learned-open` scores below `parncutt` (66.53 against 69.31) while the test split and the Mutopia half favour it (+1.8 and +4.3). The teacher and UI reviews judge the shipped set.
- The per-annotator calibration definition was chosen after seeing the test ECE under the old definition. On the Mutopia half the ECE is 0.104 (reported, not gated): the editors agree with the engine more often than its confidence says.
- The textbook floors gate `parncutt` only. The app solves at the expert level (#46). Beginner confidences use the expert-level temperature. Five legato reaches of 13 to 14 semitones remain at the beginner level. The trainer's epoch selection tolerates decode errors and uses `solveHand`.
- 28% of expert annotations violate a solver hard rule, mostly legato overlaps of about 50 ms. On PIG piece 029 the hard decode is infeasible (overlaps of 9 to 27 ms) and the solver falls back to its soft decode. On the PIG validation pieces every lint law error of the shipped set lies in a hand with no legal fingering. The experts' own fingerings score 8.00 lint errors per 1,000 notes.
- CLI gaps (#47): `bench` has no `--library-set` flag and `lint` no `--profile`. `research/readability/corpus.py` reads every `.fingered` score in the library rather than core30, so its numbers move as the library grows.
- Open solver leads and the teacher-review hypotheses: #45, #44.

Teacher review: six fixes are due before the next weekly run (#43).

App and tests:

- Memory on long pieces: a piece of six pages or more keeps only the pages near the screen in the DOM (the Scherzo settles at 143 MB, Beethoven's Ninth at 151 MB; [issue 99](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/99)), and Verovio's 128 MB WASM heap stays warm between pieces except after one of 2,000 notes or more, after which both workers are released (`docs/research/ram-usage.md`).
- Imported MIDI loses held notes: one voice per hand shortens them (#34). MusicXML is unaffected.
- Timing in the walk ([#192](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/192)) judges onsets against the reader's own pulse only (not rests, lengths, releases or subdivisions inside a beat), skips hesitations, and its windows are provisional.
- Playing along needs Web MIDI: Safari and iOS have none, and on Windows a Bluetooth piano needs the WinRT MIDI flag in Chrome (measured; the line names Edge's flag too) while USB works as it is; a step's pitches are exact, so a note outside a 61-key piano's range is passed by clicking the next bar (`docs/app.md`, Known limitations).
- Offline, only the pieces opened before open and only the piano samples fetched before play; the installed app's splash screen and status bar stay white in dark mode; installing and playing on Android and iOS are not yet checked on devices (`docs/app.md`, Known limitations).

Library:

- About 1,300 archive pieces have no composer ("Unknown"), the 1,201 pieces of the classical archive collection are not split by composer era, and archive titles need cleaning (#48).
- The grades skew to 3 and 4. Grade 6 holds 696 pieces (34 with Chopin in the composer or title, 8 by Rachmaninoff): 497 more than before the note ceiling was lifted and the Mutopia release, short of the owner's 1,000 more (#36). The Mutopia rule holds back 9 concert editions: 7 beyond the ceiling (Rachmaninoff Op. 23 No. 10 at 41.5 law errors per 1,000 notes), Goldberg 22 for a lost tempo word and one whose source changed since it was pinned. The checker's grace-note fix released 17 held bulk editions, 4 of them at grade 6; the other converter and checker fixes would release few concert works (#49). The validator refuses 96 archive scores the ceiling admitted (93 before the four repairs of issue #80, and seven more refused after the scoped relaxation of issue #80), among them Chopin's Ballade No. 4, Sonata No. 2 and two uploads of the Polonaise Op. 53, Islamey and La Valse; 31 of them only for bars that run over their time signature by more than half a beat (cadenzas, for one) and 11 only by a rounding of the file's divisions (`content/tools/README.md`, MuseScore archive scores).

Scanning:

- Recognition errors remain and are corrected in the review (#40, done): on the acceptance corpus homr misses 13 of 47 ties that carry no mark in its output, loses the grand staff on a shadowed page, and photo beams are inferred from the meter rather than read; the duplicated C5 and wrong-hand E3 of the private test photos are not re-measured. A staff that homr reads only in part, which loses or adds bars, is named in the review's verdict line, and a fourth homr patch recovers the case behind it: a missed clef that cut off the start of a system (issue 129). Details: `docs/scanning.md`, `docs/research/omr.md`; open: #39 (beams).
- Scanning needs a connection, recognises one page at a time for all readers (the others wait, and are told another score is being scanned), and stops for the rest of the month once the 5 spend cap is reached (`docs/scan-service.md`).
- The join declares the second staff of a merged split page in the first opening `<attributes>` of its first measure, because Verovio reads `<staves>` only there while the engine reads it from any; a split first page declared in a later one drew one staff per system with both hands fingered, as the owner's phone showed. Why page 1 of that scan came back split from the re-scan is open: **Download everything** exists to get the archive of such a scan ([#156](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/156)).
