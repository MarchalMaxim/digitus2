## Wider score view on wide windows (issue #14, 2026-10-16)

The user found the keyboard scrolling sideways on a wide screen with a third of the window empty. Cause: every page, the score view included, stopped at 72rem (1152 px), so at a 1716 px window the dock's keyboard got 720 px, and any piece spanning five octaves (35 white keys at the 22 px minimum, 770 px) scrolled. The score view now takes `max-width: clamp(72rem, 100% - 8rem, 120rem)`: the same 4rem side margins it already had at 1280 px, so up to 1280 px nothing moves (the 1280 x 900 visual baselines stay valid), growing with the window up to 120rem so a line of music stays readable on an ultra-wide screen. The music reflows into longer systems (it is laid out to the column's width) and the keyboard shows a five-octave piece whole: K545 at 1716 px has 1156 px for its 770. New browser check in e2e/playback.spec.ts ('the keyboard on a wide window'), failing before the fix and passing after. Files: apps/web/src/design/base.css, e2e/playback.spec.ts, docs/app.md. Claude Code.

## Smoother light and dark switch (issue #11, 2026-10-16)

The user found the theme change blunt: the paper snapped in one frame while the buttons faded at their own 160 ms. A click on the switch now spreads the new theme over the old one as a circle growing from the switch to the farthest corner of the viewport (View Transitions API, a clip-path keyframe on the new picture; theme.ts sets only the centre and radius as --ripple-* custom properties). Browsers without view transitions change as before; a system or other-tab change is not animated. **The verification loop, in plain words.** `pnpm verify` is the definition of done: one command, eleven checks in a row, and a change counts only when all pass. The ordinary ones are typecheck, lint, formatting, unit tests, a production build, a bundle-size budget and the Playwright end-to-end tests that drive the app in a browser at desktop and phone widths. The project-specific ones are a repository hygiene check, a colour guard (black, white, one red and nothing else) and three benchmark gates that score the fingering engine against the floors in `benchmarks/floor.json`: a change that makes the fingerings worse than the recorded floor fails the loop even when every test passes. CI runs the same checks on GitHub. Details and the recovery steps after a restart: docs/verification-loop.md.

**PIG, in plain words.** The Piano Fingering Dataset (Nakamura, Saito and Yoshii, Kyoto University): 150 classical piano pieces where professional pianists wrote the fingering of every note, several pianists per piece; the standard benchmark of the fingering literature. The engine's main scorecard is how often its chosen finger matches the human one on PIG's test pieces, and its learned weights were trained on PIG's training pieces. Its licence allows research use but not redistribution, so it is never committed: download it from the PIG site (search "PIG dataset piano fingering Nakamura"; the verification spec records the URL used) into `research/data/pig/`.
