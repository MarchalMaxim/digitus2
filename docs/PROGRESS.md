**PIG, in plain words.** The Piano Fingering Dataset (Nakamura, Saito and Yoshii, Kyoto University): 150 classical piano excerpts where pianists wrote the fingering of every note, several pianists per piece; the standard benchmark of the fingering literature. The engine's main scorecard is how often its finger matches the human one on PIG's 30 test pieces. Its licence allows non-profit research use but not redistribution, so it is never committed and nothing fitted on it ships. Without it, `pnpm verify` reports the benchmark and hands steps as SKIP ("dataset missing") and the floors go unchecked: a green run without the data is not a full verification.

**The verification loop, in plain words.** `pnpm verify` is the definition of done: one command, fifteen steps in a row, and a change counts only when all pass. The ordinary steps are typecheck, lint, unit tests, a production build, a bundle-size budget and Playwright end-to-end tests at desktop and phone widths. The project-specific ones are a repository-size check, a colour guard (black, white, one red), the scanning harness tests, the scan service's Python tests (`omr:service`, SKIP without Python), the scanning acceptance gate (`omr:acceptance`, SKIP without the recognisers), and three benchmark gates that score the engine against the floors in `benchmarks/floor.json`: a change that makes the fingerings worse than the floor fails even when every test passes. CI runs most of the same checks on GitHub. Details: `docs/verification-loop.md`.

## Standing rules from the owner

`CLAUDE.md` carries the fixed decisions and the full wording of the UI rules.

- **Models.** Opus 5.5 for substantive subagent work (design, implementation, the reviews that gate merges); Sonnet 5.5 for trivial and mechanical work (web searches, `pnpm verify` and Playwright runs that only report results, screenshots); Fable coordinates and plans. Use workflows where parallel work helps. In Codex sessions: gpt-6.1-sol agents on bounded, independent tasks, with independent verification of the actual output.
- **Verification.** `pnpm verify` is the definition of done. Readability for intermediate players is a first-class metric. A qualitative UI review by an Opus subagent (verification spec 3.10) accompanies UI work.
- **UI minimalism.** "Make sure that the UI stays very pristine and not cluttered. I very much like it right now. Do not overclutter it with features and be brutal with this." Controls appear only in the context that needs them (progressive disclosure).
- **Merging.** Never merge into `main` without the owner's explicit confirmation. Work goes on a branch and a pull request; the owner merges.
- **Requests.** Every request or bug report the owner posts becomes a GitHub issue with the owner's words, a judgement, the agreed approach and a checklist. When it is done or closed, it gets one line in `docs/TODO.md`.
- **This file.** Keep it current; it is what a new session reads first.

## Decisions

### Licensing and data

- **PIG never ships.** Its licence permits non-profit academic use only and forbids redistribution. The dataset, PIG-fitted weights (`research/weights/`) and PIG-derived hand statistics stay research-only. Asking the PIG maintainers for permission is the owner's decision; without it the shipped weights cannot reach FingerFlow's 68.6.
- **Shipped weights come from open data only:** the textbook oracle, Beringer (via pydactyl, MIT) and the train half of the Mutopia editorial corpus. Mutopia is split 70/30 by piece (seed 20261013); the test half is a held-out oracle.
- **Datasets whose licence forbids redistribution are never committed** (MIT, non-commercial project). Per-dataset licences are in `research/data/README.md`.

### Engine and weights

- **Training runs in TypeScript** through the engine CLI with the engine's own decoder and term functions, so features exist once; Python keeps data, splits and statistics.
- **The CLI and the app solve with `RECOMMENDED_WEIGHT_SET` (`learned-open`);** the solver's own default stays `parncutt`, which the textbook floors are measured with. The benchmark gate runs the set named in `benchmarks/floor.json` (`weightSet`, `weightSetHash`): another set, or a retrained set whose floors were not re-measured, is a breach.
- **Calibration correctness is per annotator** (soft labels): PIG validation has 1.2 annotators per piece and test 5, and the any-annotator measure did not transfer (ECE 0.063 on validation, 0.243 on test).
- **The consistent-repeats pass is on by default:** identical passages get the same fingering (repeated bars fingered differently was a teacher objection in the first UI review). It was accepted although it changed 25 of the 30 core pieces under `parncutt`.
- **Hand separation priors** come from the library and the Mutopia train half with equal weight per corpus: pooled counts let the larger Mutopia corpus dominate and mis-split Twinkle. A PIG-fitted variant scored 91.76 against the shipped 92.05, so nothing PIG-derived is needed.
- **Ornaments are a practice default, not a historical interpretation:** trills alternate with the upper neighbour, short and long mordents take 3 and 5 attacks within the written duration, respecting explicit markings and the key; the keyboard follows the realised pitches; auxiliary notes get no invented fingering.

### Benchmarks and floors

- **Floors** (`benchmarks/floor.json`): a milestone's measurement minus 0.5, rounded down, raised in the same commit as the improvement with a row in `benchmarks/history.md`. The ECE maximum is the measurement plus 0.02, capped at 0.10. The lint maximum may loosen only on forced-error evidence (extra errors only in hands with no legal fingering). A floor is never lowered without a recorded reason here and in floor.json's note.
- **`pnpm verify` never rewrites a tracked file;** only labelled milestone runs replace `benchmarks/latest*.json` (`docs/verification-loop.md`).
- **The `core30` benchmark set:** `content/manifest.json` entries carry `benchmarkSet` (`core30` on the first 30 pieces, null on later ones; a missing field means core30), selected by `benchmarkEntries()` in `packages/engine/src/library/benchmarkSet.ts`. Every library-based number (hands floor, repeat consistency, the engine's library tests, `make_hand_motion`, teacher-review round-trips) measures core30, so growing the library never moves a floor.
- **The PIG test split (pieces 001–030) is scored only for the record.** Hypotheses are tested on train and validation; the teacher review's hard-error passages come from validation pieces only.
- **The teacher review is never a gate or a tuning target;** its hypotheses go to `docs/experiments.md`.
- **Readability** (`docs/research/readability.md`, verification spec 3.6): teaching editions print 32% of digits, library pieces a median 18%. The binding reduction is a greedy forward pass over the validated player model R0–R5 plus first notes, pins and low confidence, with per-level density targets and never more digits than the editor prints on the same piece. M5 implements it.
- **Metrics are cross-checked:** the Python scorer in `research/fingering_eval/` matches the official C++ evaluator (1,478 pairs), and the TypeScript metrics match the Python scorer.

### Library and content

- **Curated lanes** (the 286 curated pieces): public-domain or traditional compositions; L1 admitted a piece only when every author died in 1955 or earlier and it was published in 1928 or earlier. Engravings and arrangements are public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA; CC BY and CC BY-SA pieces are credited in the app (typesetter, licence, Mutopia id from `content/manifest.json`). Scarborough Fair uses the traditional Dorian tune.
- **Archive lanes (PDMX), the owner's broad copyright reading:** a score is admitted on the uploader's declaration of public domain or CC0 with PDMX's `license_conflict` false; there is no author-date check. The songs lane (pop, rock, soundtrack, jazz, blues) therefore ships modern arrangements in a public repository on that declaration: a stated risk the owner accepted. Only content exclusions apply (junk, Nazi-era and propaganda pieces; `content/tools/pdmx-excluded.json`); any single reviewer's flag removes a candidate.
- **Non-public-domain scores copied from the web are refused** (unauthorised copies, public repository). The legitimate routes are a private local folder and a CC-licensed lane (#37).
- **Gates:** every piece passes the XSD, the span check, notes on both staves and zero lint law errors at the medium and large profiles. The core 30 are exempt from the law check. Level 6 may relax the span and lint gates within a ceiling (40 law errors per 1,000 notes at each profile, 20 unplayable slices per 1,000), with the flags recorded (`relaxedGates` in the catalog, a sentence in the piece's notes, checked by `gates.py`), because the app's product is the fingering. Bulk Mutopia editions take the same relaxed gates and ceiling at grade 6 by a stated rule (`content/tools/mutopia_advanced.py`: concert repertoire) and keep both MIDI checks.
- **Levels:** the frozen heuristic proposes grades 1–5; grade 6 comes from a stated rule (`content/tools/pdmx_advanced.py`), which both archive lanes take, or, for a bulk Mutopia edition released under the relaxed gates, from the Mutopia rule (`content/tools/mutopia_advanced.py`); a grade-6 song stays on its Songs or Modern shelf. The UI shows four levels (grades 1–2, 3, 4, 5–6); the manifest keeps the grade.
- **Scoped relaxation for four composers (issue #80, owner, 2026-10-19):** "relax the ceiling", "loosen the advanced rule, but no more beginner songs". For archive pieces credited to Liszt, Tchaikovsky, Rachmaninoff or Prokofiev, a proposal of 4 is advanced at any score with fast notes from 4 a second, under a ceiling of 60 law errors and 30 slices per 1,000 notes; a proposal of 3 or below is never advanced. Every other composer keeps the archive rule and ceiling. Pieces credited "NA" are not covered (#48).
- **Archive caps and the note ceiling:** the caps by rating (1,300 classical pieces, 1,000 songs) hold grades 1–4 only; a passing piece at grade 5 or 6 always ships, and within a cap a shipped piece keeps its place, so a larger pool never removes one. A score may have up to 25,000 notes (`pdmx_screen.MAX_NOTES`), the longest tested in the app: the complete Chopin Études Op. 25 opens in 16 s at phone width and plays. The 1.5 MB score limit applies to the stored gzip file.
- **Dedupe compares notes, not titles** (`content/tools/dedupe.py`); a curated piece is never removed, and the same tune at another level stays.
- **Batch harvesting and automated checks come before per-piece agent investigation** (the owner's correction on throughput and cost). Downloaded candidates are not library entries until they pass the gates.
- **Storage:** archive scores are gzip (`scores/<slug>.musicxml.gz`, byte-identical on a rebuild). The app fetches the compact `content/library-index.json` before the first render, loads the full manifest and the score-file map (`apps/web/src/library/scoreFiles.ts`) lazily, so the start-up bundle does not grow with the library. `readScoreFile` detects gzip by its signature bytes, because servers may already have decoded a `.gz` file sent with `Content-Encoding: gzip`.
- **Repository budgets** (`scripts/check-repo-size.mjs`): 120 MB of trackable files (100 MB until the note ceiling was lifted), 1 MB per file and 1.5 MB per score, with explicit allowances for the manifest, verification records and archive catalog fragments.

### App and UI

- **GitHub Pages only:** public, static, no VPS, no login. The one server is the scan recognition service on Cloud Run (`docs/scan-service.md`); the app is deployed nowhere else. The auth study stays as reference (`docs/research/auth-options.md`). Mobile is the responsive web app, installable as a PWA; no native app.
- **One red element on screen;** it may scroll out of view. The red travelling between Play and the cursor counts as the one red; the note burst's double-click accent is a deliberate exception.
- **Pause keeps the resume point red;** Play is plain while paused away from the start.
- **Hand letters on the keyboard only in bars where the hands come within a fifth** (a per-onset octave rule would toggle 41 times in the Minuet), with no phone exception.
- **Seeking (T13): the score is the timeline.** No scrub bar or progress line. A click or tap anywhere in a bar, notes and digits included, goes to its start; Left and Right step a bar, Shift four, Home rewinds; seeks stay inside a loop. Stopped, the bar is cued: its first note is red and Play starts there. The red hops to the bar, and while playing the music waits for it to land (220 to 340 ms). When note editing (M5) arrives it must take clicks on notes over from the seek.
- **Disclosure thresholds** live in `apps/web/src/library/disclosure.ts` (level pills and shelves above 40 pieces, search from 100, collection headings above 12, length pills above 40).
- **Motion durations come from the tokens** and reduced motion turns them off. The theme switch spreads the new theme as a circle from the switch (View Transitions API, 400 ms); browsers without view transitions change instantly.
- **Dark mode** follows `prefers-color-scheme` until the switch overrides it. Score ink is `#d4d4d4`: the owner found near-white notes too bright and `#c4c4c4` too grey.
- **Favourites (#78)** are library slugs, newest first, in the settings in `localStorage`; a piece is marked with the heart on its score page (the style shelves' rows have none) and taken off there or with the filled heart on its Favourites row, and the `Favourites` shelf stands on the arrival page only (a search or an opened shelf takes it away). A favourite stays among a shelf's samples too: leaving it out of the draw would change the samples of the shelf the reader came from the moment a heart is pressed. Back restores the list by its anchor: the remembered offset moves by as much as `.library-list` moved, so a change above the list (Favourites, My scores) is compensated, one inside it is not.
- **The score view widens with the window** (`clamp(72rem, 100% - 8rem, 120rem)`): nothing moves up to 1,280 px, and the keyboard fits a five-octave piece on a wide screen.
- **Bundle budget:** the library page's initial JavaScript and CSS stay under 150 KB gzip; Verovio stays a lazy chunk.
- **Piano samples are self-hosted:** they are the release asset `piano-samples-v1` (452 files: the 226 samples in Ogg Opus and in AAC, because smplr skips Ogg on Safari), stored under safe names (`PP C#1` as `PP_Cs1`, mapped by a smplr storage shim that also rejects anything not audio). `scripts/piano-samples.lock.json` is the single pin (tag, size, SHA-256, file list); the Pages workflow unpacks the asset into the site, because release downloads send no CORS header. The repository never contains the samples.
- **The service worker is hand-written** (`apps/web/src/sw/`, no dependency): Workbox's file globs cannot cleanly leave out the thousands of score files and its default 2 MiB file limit drops the Verovio worker. Navigations go to the network first (4 s), then the cached shell; the precache (at most 24 MiB) is filled on install; other hashed files and the piano samples are kept cache first as they are fetched. No `skipWaiting`: a new worker waits until every window has closed, so an open page never loses its chunks. Service workers are blocked in every e2e spec but `pwa`, so `page.route` sees every request. No install button: the browser's own install UI.
- **Rendering:** Verovio keeps the engine's note ids and tags every fingering digit with `data-startid`; one CSS class turns one note or digit red; cursor sync through `getOutputTimestamp()` is within 5 ms.
- **No translucent group of notes in the score (#79):** the hand left out is dimmed shape by shape (fill and stroke opacity), never with `opacity` or a filter on a group that holds transformed shapes, which Chrome composites one layer per group (2,085 on Kuhlau's Rondo, paid by every repaint); a dimmed digit rests at a group opacity, which costs no layer and keeps gray smoothing. A hand switch fades only the systems on screen and never animates `opacity`; the pages off screen skip rendering (`content-visibility: auto`, each keeping its viewBox's aspect ratio). Measurements: `docs/research/hand-switch-performance.md`.

### Scanning

- **Recognition runs on the project's scan service on Google Cloud Run** (#62; `docs/scan-service.md`, design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-scan-service-design.md`): `europe-west4`, request-based billing, 4 vCPU and 8 GiB, one recognition at a time, 0 to 1 instances, no keep-warm ping and no minimum instance; one warm-up health request when the scan page opens. Every device scans with one **Scan this score**, phones included; the scan page says where the page goes in one sentence, and the press is the consent. The app has no PC worker; the loopback worker is the research server and the service's engine. Recognition in the browser is not built (oemer there took 21 minutes for Twinkle; a homr port is unmeasured), and vendor OMR services are rejected (sign-ups, per-page credits, the score on their servers).
- **The spend is capped at 5 a month:** a budget in the billing account's currency on the service's own project, alerts at 50, 90 and 100 percent, and Google's kill-switch function that disables billing at the budget. Scanning then stays off until the month resets or the owner raises the budget and relinks; nothing resumes automatically.
- **The service keeps nothing:** each page is recognised in memory and deleted with its result when the job ends; one log line per job (kind, bytes, seconds, outcome), never content or addresses; 30 recognitions per caller per hour; no secret ships in the static bundle. PDFs go with their original bytes to Audiveris (measurably better recognition); photos go to a pinned homr (AGPL-3.0, run on the server, never bundled into the MIT app).
- **Scanning ships as experimental:** the owner accepts a useful first release for clear photos of printed scores. Source-versus-recognition review comes before any use for fingerings, the raw MusicXML can be downloaded for correction elsewhere, and the app makes no accuracy claim. No hand-written replacement XML or unreviewed preprocessing ships. Uploaded scores stay in the browser (IndexedDB "My scores"), nothing is published automatically, and private photos and recognition outputs are never committed.
- **No shared library of uploads:** readers share files themselves and the library stays curated (#41 closed; options in `docs/research/shared-library-options.md`).

## Known limitations and open findings

Engine:

- With open data the engine trails FingerFlow's learned weights clearly and FHMM3 within noise; closing the gap needs PIG-fitted weights (the owner's decision above).
- On PIG validation `learned-open` scores below `parncutt` (66.53 against 69.31) while the test split and the Mutopia half favour it (+1.8 and +4.3). The teacher and UI reviews judge the shipped set.
- The per-annotator calibration definition was chosen after seeing the test ECE under the old definition. On the Mutopia half the ECE is 0.104 (reported, not gated): the editors agree with the engine more often than its confidence says.
- The textbook floors gate `parncutt` only. The app solves at the expert level (#46). Beginner confidences use the expert-level temperature. Five legato reaches of 13 to 14 semitones remain at the beginner level. The trainer's epoch selection tolerates decode errors and uses `solveHand`.
- 28% of expert annotations violate a solver hard rule, mostly legato overlaps of about 50 ms. On PIG piece 029 the hard decode is infeasible (overlaps of 9 to 27 ms) and the solver falls back to its soft decode. On the PIG validation pieces every lint law error of the shipped set lies in a hand with no legal fingering. The experts' own fingerings score 8.00 lint errors per 1,000 notes.
- CLI gaps (#47): `bench` has no `--library-set` flag and `lint` no `--profile`. `research/readability/corpus.py` reads every `.fingered` score in the library rather than core30, so its numbers move as the library grows.
- Open solver leads and the teacher-review hypotheses: #45, #44.

Teacher review: six fixes are due before the next weekly run (#43).

App and tests:

- Verovio's fixed 128 MB WASM heap dominates the tab's memory; it is left as is by the owner's decision (`docs/app.md`).
- Imported MIDI loses held notes: one voice per hand shortens them (#34). MusicXML is unaffected.
- Repeats are not expanded in playback (#50).
- Offline, only the pieces opened before open and only the piano samples fetched before play; the installed app's splash screen and status bar stay white in dark mode; installing and playing on Android and iOS are not yet checked on devices (`docs/app.md`, Known limitations).

Library:

- About 1,300 archive pieces have no composer ("Unknown"), the 1,201 pieces of the classical archive collection are not split by composer era, and archive titles need cleaning (#48).
- The grades skew to 3 and 4. Grade 6 holds 692 pieces (34 with Chopin in the composer or title, 8 by Rachmaninoff): 493 more than before the note ceiling was lifted and the Mutopia release, short of the owner's 1,000 more (#36). The Mutopia rule holds back 9 concert editions: 7 beyond the ceiling (Rachmaninoff Op. 23 No. 10 at 41.5 law errors per 1,000 notes), Goldberg 22 for a lost tempo word and one whose source changed since it was pinned. Converter and checker fixes would release few concert works (#49). The validator refuses 96 archive scores the ceiling admitted (93 before the four repairs of issue #80, and seven more refused after the scoped relaxation of issue #80), among them Chopin's Ballade No. 4, Sonata No. 2 and two uploads of the Polonaise Op. 53, Islamey and La Valse; 31 of them only for bars that run over their time signature by more than half a beat (cadenzas, for one) and 11 only by a rounding of the file's divisions (`content/tools/README.md`, MuseScore archive scores).

Scanning:

- Recognition errors remain and are corrected in the review (#40, done): on the acceptance corpus homr misses 13 of 47 ties that carry no mark in its output, loses the grand staff on a shadowed page, and photo beams are inferred from the meter rather than read; the duplicated C5 and wrong-hand E3 of the private test photos are not re-measured. Details: `docs/scanning.md`, `docs/research/omr.md`; open: #39 (beams).
- Scanning needs a connection, recognises one page at a time for all readers (the others are told the scanner is busy), and stops for the rest of the month once the 5 spend cap is reached (`docs/scan-service.md`).
