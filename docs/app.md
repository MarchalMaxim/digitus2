# digitus2 web app

The app in `apps/web` (`@digitus2/web`): static files on GitHub Pages, no server of its own and no login; scanning sends pages to the project's scan service (`docs/scan-service.md`). Product spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-digitus2-design.md` (sections 4, 5 and 9). Verification spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-verification-design.md` (goals "Be fast" and "Honor the design brief", checks 3.9 and 3.10). Library page design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-library-shelves-design.md`. Dock, Practice, the paused state and the hand letters: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-14-ui-batch-design.md` (sections 12 to 15). The technology choices and their measurements come from the rendering spike, `docs/research/rendering-spike.md`. Photo and PDF scanning: `docs/scanning.md`.

## What the app does

A beginner opens one of the built-in pieces from the library, or a MusicXML or MIDI file of their own, and sees the score with a finger number on every note, chosen by the digitus2 engine for the selected hand size. Play sounds the piece on a sampled grand piano while a red cursor marks the notes being heard and an on-screen keyboard shows which finger plays which key. Tempo, one hand or both, metronome, count-in and a bar loop help practice. The fingered score can be exported as MusicXML or printed. An about page lists every licence and attribution.

Everything runs in the browser. The network carries the app's static files, the piano samples (served by the app's own site) and, only when the reader scans, the project's scan service on Google Cloud Run: one health request when the scan page opens, then one request per page on **Scan this score** (`docs/scan-service.md`). The app installs from the browser, and a service worker keeps the app and what the reader has opened, so a piece opened once opens and plays offline.

### Shell, routes and focus

- `App.tsx` renders the store's view. The about and scan views are lazy chunks. The library list is fetched before the first render (`main.tsx`); if that fails the page says so in plain text.
- The URL hash mirrors the view: `#/`, `#/about`, `#/piece/<slug>`, `#/piece/file` (a dropped file) and `#/scan` (a scan under review). The last two cannot be restored after a reload: they fall back to `#/` with the notice "Open it from My scores, or choose the file again." An address that names a piece not in the library falls back to `#/` with the notice "That piece is not in the library." Browser back and forward work.
- A view that replaces another moves the focus to its `h1` (`tabIndex={-1}`, no ring), so the next Tab starts inside the new view and a screen reader announces it. The view the page loads with leaves the focus alone.
- A view that replaces another opens at the top of the page (an instant scroll in a layout effect, so the new view never paints at the old offset): a piece opened from far down the library shows its title, Play and first system on the first screen. The library is the exception. It comes back at the scroll position it had, whether the reader returns with the score's Back button or through the browser's history (`libraryScrollY`; `LibraryView` writes the offset when it unmounts, before the next view scrolls), moved by as much as its list moved when what stands above the list changed meanwhile (`libraryListTop`, see Library, Favourites).
- Every change of view mounts a new view, so no playback (whose cursor scrolls the page) outlives one.
- From 480 px the page keeps its scrollbar gutter on screen (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `html`, issue #107). Without it, a classic scrollbar (Windows, Linux) took 15 px from the page when it appeared: a score arriving after its short loading view moved the side panel and the theme toggle 15 px left while the reader looked on, and a piece too short to scroll laid out 15 px wider than the library beside it. A page that does not scroll shows the empty gutter in the paper colour. Overlay scrollbars (macOS, phones) take no room either way. Below 480 px there is no gutter: phones have overlay scrollbars, and the e2e phone emulation, which hides its scrollbars, would still reserve one; no media feature tells it from a desktop (hover and pointer flip after its first click). The e2e checks measure horizontal overflow against the root's box (`e2e/helpers/overflow.ts`), because headless Chromium leaves the reserved gutter out of `clientWidth`.

### Store

- Only the settings persist, in `localStorage` under `digitus2.settings.v1`. A stored object from an older or newer app is merged field by field with the defaults and unknown fields are ignored. When `localStorage` is blocked the settings live in memory for the visit.
- Settings: `handSize` (large, medium, small, child; default medium), `tempoByPiece`, `hands`, `metronome`, `countIn` (on by default), `showEditorial`, `lastPlayed` (the slug of the library piece played last, set on every Play of a library piece and never for a dropped file; a slug a later library build removed acts as none) `legendSeen` (set by the first Play anywhere), `favourites` (the slugs of the library pieces marked with the score page's heart, newest first; a stored list keeps its unique non-empty strings, and a slug a later library build removed is kept but not shown) and `playAlong` (the reader turned on playing along on their own piano, issue 182; off by default, kept only when it is a boolean; the browser's MIDI permission is separate, see Library, Play along) and `practiceMode` (with a piano connected, the red waits for the reader's piano, issue 191; on by default, kept per browser and not per piece, and read as on when the stored value is not a boolean).
- The tempo factor belongs to the piece, because one factor for every piece made every piece slow once a hard one was slowed. `tempoByPiece` maps a piece's key (`pieceKey`: `piece:<slug>`, or `file:<name>:<fileIdentity>` for a dropped file) to a factor between 50 and 150 %. A piece without an entry plays at 100 %. Only factors other than 100 % are kept, for the 50 pieces changed last (`TEMPO_PIECES_MAX`).
- The theme choice is kept separately, under `digitus2.theme` (see Light and dark).
- Visit state, never persisted: the view, a one-line notice, the offer of a newer build (`updateReady`, below), `libraryScrollY` with `libraryListTop`, the chosen level, the search text, the open shelf and search section, the pages shown, the chosen length, whether the Favourites shelf shows every favourite (`libraryFavouritesOpen`), and `practiceOpen`.

### Engine worker

`src/engine/engine.worker.ts`, `handlers.ts`, `client.ts` and `protocol.ts`. Requests carry ids, so answers can arrive in any order. A worker that fails rejects every pending call and is replaced for the next piece. The app uses only the engine's public API (`@digitus2/engine`, aliased to `packages/engine/src`, plus the `ornaments` and `score-build` subpaths, so the main thread never loads the engine's 1 MB chunk).

- `load` parses MusicXML (and `.mxl`, UTF-16 and Latin-1 files, decoded once). The decoded text's XML declaration is rewritten to say UTF-8, so the export, saved as UTF-8, declares what it is. A `.mid` or `.midi` file is imported with `importMidi` as generated two-staff MusicXML; the import facts travel with the score as `midiInfo`.
- `solve` runs `solveFingering(score, { profile, weightSet: RECOMMENDED_WEIGHT_SET })`: the hand-size profile with the shipped learned weight set (`learned-open`).
- `fingeredXml` writes the fingering into the source MusicXML.
- `embellish` runs `guardEmbellishment` on the plain MusicXML: the light embellisher, the independent check, a solve with `learned-open` and a beginner lint at each of the four hand sizes, the figures of offending bars dropped once, and at least three figures left. It returns the embellished score with a fingering for every hand size, or the reason it is not offered.

### Rendering with Verovio

- Verovio 6.3 (WASM) runs in its own worker (`verovio.worker.ts`, `renderer.ts`) and lays the fingered MusicXML out as SVG pages at a scale chosen from the score column's width (`scaleForWidth`, `render/options.ts`): 40 % below 720 px, so a phone held upright draws as before; 46, 50 and 54 % from 720, 880 and 1040 px; and 56 % from 1200 px. A 1280 px window's column is 832 px, a 1920 px one's 1472 px. From 900 px the side panel narrows the column by 288 px, so windows from 900 to about 1,040 px draw at 40 %, smaller than a 768 px tablet held upright (column 736 px, 46 %). The steps sit on the edges of the 160 px width buckets, so the scale changes only where the score is laid out again. Print keeps 40 %. Only the newest render request counts, so a resize never shows a stale layout. The width is bucketed by 160 px, so small resizes do not lay out again.
- Our note ids are injected into the MusicXML first (`injectIds.ts`), so every SVG note carries the engine's note id, and Verovio writes each digit's note into the SVG (`g.fing[data-startid]`). The main thread only swaps classes (cursor, dimming) and patches digit text (re-solve).
- The pages off screen skip style and paint (`content-visibility: auto` on each page's SVG), so a class change there costs nothing until the page comes near the window; each page keeps its viewBox's aspect ratio meanwhile (`ScoreSheet.tsx`), so no page or scroll position moves when it is drawn. A long piece runs to some 100,000 SVG elements (Chopin's Scherzo No. 2: 780 bars, 32 pages), which a hand switch used to restyle all at once (`docs/research/hand-switch-performance.md`). On a piece of six pages or more only the pages within a window height of the screen are in the DOM (`pageWindow.ts`); the others stand as empty SVGs with their viewBox, so the scroll height never changes, and the page holding the red is never emptied. The scan review keeps every page. Print uses its own engraving and needs none of them.
- The library page warms the engine and Verovio workers up when the browser is idle, so the first piece opens faster.
- A worker that fails (its chunk did not load, it crashed) rejects every pending render. The score then shows an error with a way back instead of "Preparing the score…" for good, and the next piece gets a fresh worker.
- Before layout the MusicXML goes through two display-only steps made for recognised scores: `normalizeVoices.ts` (voice order) and `scanBeams.ts` (conventional beat groups where beams are missing). They change no pitch, timing or id; see `docs/scanning.md`.

Why Verovio (spike sections 1, 3 and 9): it keeps MusicXML note ids; it writes each digit's note into the SVG, so one note or one digit can be made red with CSS; it honours the fingering placement per voice; and it reflows cleanly at 360 px. OpenSheetMusicDisplay, the fallback, is faster and smaller but loses on fingering engraving, per-note styling (grace notes inherit the colour) and stable ids. Verovio's costs are 2.3 MB gzip (kept out of the first load as a lazy worker chunk), about 0.7 s of layout for 120 bars, and the LGPL-3.0-or-later licence (shipped unmodified as its own chunk and credited on the about page). Sources: [Verovio](https://www.verovio.org/), [smplr](https://github.com/danigb/smplr), [`AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp()`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/getOutputTimestamp).

### Open pipeline

`src/score/useScoreDocument.ts` runs load, solve, fingered XML with ids, then render, as a status machine: `loading`, `fingering`, `rendering`, `ready`, `error` (`idle` is reserved and never set).

- The status line paints before the solve starts. An unreadable file ends in a readable error with a way back: one plain sentence, then the engine's message as the detail.
- A hand-size change re-solves and patches only the digits that changed, with no new layout.
- For a MIDI file, a change of the `Hand split` control opens the same bytes again with the chosen split (or none for `Automatic`) through the same statuses. The previous import stays on screen until the new one replaces it.
- The fingered text is kept per fingering, so a new width, or switching back to a fingering shown before, only lays out again. Writing a fingering into the MusicXML takes about 0.1 s for K.545, and exporting pays it once.
- A library piece's editor's fingering file is loaded when the reader first switches the editor's fingering on.
- After the plain score is `ready`, the worker embellishes a library piece at grade 1 or 2, and any dropped MusicXML or MIDI file of at most 900 notes (never a scan import). The embellished score is laid out ahead of time at the current width, so the `Add notes between` switch changes the pages at once. The two versions share their bar starts, so the bar, the loop and the play state carry across a switch. The carry is the state the old score leaves when the new pages land, so a Pause or a seek made while they are laid out holds, and a Play still loading its samples plays on. A play carried across goes on without a count-in, and at once when the piano already holds the new version's pitches and velocities: the loaded samples stay across a new score, and only a score that needs a pitch or a velocity layer they lack loads again. A hand-size change takes the embellished fingering already solved for that size, and the editor's fingering carries onto the original notes by onset, pitch, staff and voice. A piece the guard refuses, or whose embellished layout fails, keeps the plain score and shows no switch.

### Library data

- `content/library-index.json` holds the eight fields the list reads (slug, title, composer, grade, collection, genre, duration) in a compact columnar form with a collection table. It is fetched before the app renders and is not bundled: at 3,189 pieces it is about 93 KB gzip, which would break the start-up budget.
- `content/manifest.json` holds the full entries (sources, licences, file paths; 3.7 MB, about 229 KB gzip). It is fetched as JSON the first time a library piece or the about page needs it, not imported as a module: a 3 MB JSON module keeps its source text and the parsed string alive for the page's lifetime (about 12 MB of heap).
- The map from manifest paths to score files (`import.meta.glob`, one entry per piece) lives in `scoreFiles.ts` and loads when a score opens. In the start-up chunk it made the bundle grow with the library (152.2 KB gzip at 1,682 pieces, over the 150 KB budget).
- MuseScore-archive scores are stored as gzip (`.musicxml.gz`). A server may already have decoded such a file, so `readScoreFile` decides by the gzip signature bytes (`1f 8b`), not by the extension.
- The manifest keeps grades 1 to 6 (`level`), so the engine's benchmarks and the content build are untouched; the page maps them to four levels (`tierOf` in `levels.ts`).

### Audio

- Piano: smplr's Splendid Grand Piano (public-domain samples) with only the piece's pitches and the velocity layers its velocities fall in, about 2.5 MB per layer instead of 19.5 MB for everything for the spike's 8-bar piece. smplr has five layers (1 to 40, 41 to 67, 68 to 84, 85 to 100, 101 to 127) and takes one velocity range, so every layer from the softest note's to the loudest's loads: a piece marked p and f loads three. smplr plays nothing for a velocity outside the loaded layers, and has no per-note gain, so there is no cheaper route. The softest layer is the pp samples through a 1 kHz low-pass filter, which sounds as if played through a towel, so the piano plays nothing below velocity 41 (`PIANO_SOFTEST` in `instrument.ts`): a softer note, from a long diminuendo or a MIDI file, plays at 41 on the pp layer. smplr is imported inside `load()`, which keeps it out of the entry chunk.
- Samples: the app serves the piano samples from its own site, at `<base>piano/` (`/digitus2/piano/` on Pages, `/piano/` in dev, preview and the e2e suite), so playback needs no other host. They are the release asset `piano-samples-v1` of this repository (`splendid-grand-piano-v1.tar`, 452 files: the 226 Splendid Grand Piano samples, public domain, in Ogg Opus and in AAC), pinned by size, SHA-256 and file list in `scripts/piano-samples.lock.json`. The site serves them itself because release downloads send no CORS header. Each file is stored under a safe name, a space as `_` and `#` as `s` (`PP C#1` is `PP_Cs1.ogg`): smplr asks for its own names and the storage it is given (`pianoStorage` in `src/audio/pianoSamples.ts`) fetches the stored file, because the Vite servers keep `%23` encoded and would answer with the app's page. Safari fetches the `.m4a` files, other browsers the `.ogg`. That storage rejects a response that is not audio: a missing sample (the Vite servers answer it with the app's page and status 200), or offline a sample never fetched, makes Play say `The piano sounds could not be loaded. Check the connection and press Play again.` instead of playing silence.
- A new sample set ships as a new release: a new tag and asset name in `scripts/piano-samples-lib.mjs` and `PIANO_CACHE` in `src/sw/policy.ts` (a unit test ties it to the lock's tag, so the service worker starts a fresh piano cache), then `node scripts/build-piano-samples.mjs --write-lock` and the tar published as the new release's asset.
- Dynamics: the MusicXML reader turns the marks ppp to fff into velocities (44, 56, 68, 80, 92, 104, 116, 127), from the mark on; a direction with no mark from that list reads its `<sound dynamics>` (a percentage of velocity 90, moved onto the marks' scale: the velocities notation programs write for the marks, MuseScore's mf 80 and f 96, land on 92 and 104), and an accent such as sfz or fp keeps the level. The marks sit 12 apart because smplr's gain grows with the square of the velocity on top of the louder sample layer: with the MIDI-style spacing (p 49, f 96) a change from p to f was 11.7 dB and sounded like a switch; it is 7.4 dB. mf sits at 92, between the hands' unmarked 96 and 88, so a piece marked mf sounds like one with no mark, and each mark plays smplr's layer nearest its name: mp and p the mp layer, mf the mf layer, f and above the ff layer, pp and ppp the pp layer. With mf at 80 a piece marked mf played on the mp layer and p on the pp layer, softer and duller than an unmarked piece; over the library a quarter of the notes moved off the pp layer. A mark between the staves (below the upper staff, the default, or above the lower one) is for both hands; one above the upper staff or below the lower one is for its hand alone, and the other hand follows it until a mark of its own (a pp above the upper staff alone, as in the Moonlight sonata, is for both hands). MIDI imports keep their own velocities. A note before the first mark plays at velocity 96 in the right hand and 88 in the left (`UNMARKED_VELOCITY` in the engine's `score/build.ts`, read by `noteVelocity` in `instrument.ts`). A score with no marks starts at these velocities; only its hairpins, cresc. or dim. words and `<sound dynamics>` change them.
- Hairpins and cresc.: a hairpin (`<wedge>`, paired with its stop by `number`) and the words cresc., dim., decresc., smorz., perdendosi, calando and morendo (also inside "dim. e poco rit.") change the level gradually, note by note, with the same placement rule as the marks. The level runs linearly from the one at the start to the mark at the end, when that mark lies in the change's direction: for a hairpin, the first mark from its stop to a bar after it; for words, the next mark, at most 4 bars on. With no such mark the change goes one mark's step (12), but never more than a step from the latest mark, so a run of short sighs does not sink to nothing; the level holds there. A hairpin ends at the first stop of its number after it in time, also when another voice writes the stop first. A mark inside a change sets the level and the change carries on from it. A hairpin before the first mark starts from the hand's unmarked velocity. Each note keeps one velocity, so a held note does not swell (smplr has no per-note gain).
- Scheduler: our own look-ahead scheduler (`transport.ts`: 150 ms look-ahead, a 25 ms tick) with tempo, seek, loop, hand muting, metronome and count-in. Grace notes are spread before their principal, 50 ms apart. The note list it plays (graces, rolls, ornaments, the pedal's holds and each note's velocity: `playEvents` in `audio/events.ts`, kept per score by `playEventsFor`) is a static module of its own that imports nothing from `transport.ts`, so the accompaniment of the play-along sounds a note exactly as Play does and the lazy transport chunk stays out of the entry chunk.
- Accompaniment (issue 184): with a piano connected and one hand left out, digitus plays that hand (Score view, Playing along on a piano, The other hand) through an `Accompanist` (`audio/accompanist.ts`, static and small), made on the first `Playback.accompany` over the context and the instrument Play uses. It is a just-in-time queue like the transport's: a note enters the instrument only when it is due within 150 ms, on a 25 ms tick that runs only while the queue holds notes, and each note handed over keeps its own stop (`Instrument.start` returns it, and `createPiano` gives every start a unique smplr `stopId`: `n1`, `n2` and so on). smplr offers nothing simpler. Its stop works by `stopId`, which defaults to the pitch, so cancelling a pending E2 would cut an E2 that is ringing, and an accompaniment repeats its bass pitches all the time. And `piano.stop()`, our `stopAll`, stops voices but not smplr's own queue of the notes more than 200 ms ahead, and it would cut Play's notes and a struck key as well. 150 ms is under smplr's 200 ms, so nothing of ours ever enters that queue. A new stretch clears the queue and cancels the handed notes that have not started (a note that sounds rings for its length); a stop also releases the ones that sound, with smplr's release; `stopAll` is never called.
- Preparing the samples (issue 184): `Playback.prepare()` makes the AudioContext with no gesture and loads the piece's samples under `preparing`, never `loading`: the walk is active only while `!loading`, so a preload that set it would turn the walk off and spend its keys. It uses the same load counter as Play, so a Play or a struck key started meanwhile wins the load; it runs once per score; a failed load shows the `The piano sounds could not be loaded…` notice once and is not tried again for that score (a later Play loads again as before). `accompany` is a no-op until the samples cover the score and the context runs, so nothing is queued to sound all at once later; a context that is held stops what an earlier press left queued (its clock stands still, so those notes would sound out of step when the audio came back).
- Autoplay (issue 184): the browser may hold the context `suspended` until the page has had a click or a key press (Chrome's policy `document-user-activation-required`; a MIDI message is not a user activation). `soundWaits` is true while the context is `suspended` and `navigator.userActivation.hasBeenActive` is not (the second condition keeps the sentence from flashing during the instant a permitted context takes to start), and false for a score whose samples failed to load (a click would sound nothing); it only drives the status line's sentence. Whenever the context is `suspended`, a capture listener on `document` resumes it on the first `pointerdown`, `pointerup` or `keydown`, and goes when the context runs or the view is left. A `resume()` with no gesture stays pending and is never awaited.
- Repeats and jumps: the reader keeps each bar's repeat signs, volta endings and jump marks (`MeasureInfo.form`: `<repeat>`, `<ending>`, the `<sound>` attributes dacapo, dalsegno, segno, coda, tocoda and fine, and the words D.C., D.S., Fine and To Coda), and `performanceOrder` (engine `score/form.ts`) lists the bars in the order heard. A backward repeat returns to the last forward repeat, else to the bar after the previous repeat, else to the top, and plays as many passes as it names (at most 100); a volta bar plays on the passes its numbers name. A D.C. or D.S. is taken once; after it no repeat is taken, the last ending plays, Fine ends the piece and To Coda goes to the coda. The transport plays that order (`performanceOf` in `audio/performance.ts`) while the score stays engraved once, so the red goes back with the music. A step or a click goes to the written bar's next hearing forward and its last hearing back; a seek to the start is the top. A loop plays its bars in a row, one lap of the performance: the stretch where they are heard together, the one the music is in when the loop is set, else the first. A loop over bars 1 to 8 of a piece whose first four bars repeat plays the second hearing of bars 1 to 4 and then bars 5 to 8, and a repeat inside the loop is not played within it. Bars that are never heard in a row run from a hearing of the first bar to the next end of the last. The stretch is chosen when the loop is set and holds until it changes, also before the first Play (`performanceLoop`, `loopStartSec`).
- Rolled chords: a chord marked `<arpeggiate>` sounds from its lowest note up (from the top with `direction="down"`), 40 ms apart in score time, so the roll widens at a slower tempo. The whole roll is held within a quarter of the chord's shortest note. Every note still stops at the written end, and the cursor and keyboard keep the written onset. Each hand rolls on its own chord (`rollDelays` in `audio/performance.ts`), so a roll written in both hands at one tick, as a staff-spanning arpeggio is in MuseScore (424 such ticks in the library), starts in both hands together; the `number` of an arpeggio joins the notes of every hand that carries it, and no library score numbers one. A grace note or an ornamented note does not roll.
- Pedal: `<pedal>` (start, stop, change; line or sign) and `<sound damper-pedal>` hold a note whose written end falls while the pedal is down until the next lift, change or press (a press while the pedal is down is a re-pedal), or until the same key is struck again. A pedal never lifted holds to the end of the piece. Marks on either staff apply to both hands. Grace and ornament notes keep their length. A note that ends exactly as the pedal goes down is not held. Nothing changes on screen: the keyboard and the cursor follow written onsets and ends (`pedalStops` in `audio/performance.ts`).
- Count-in: it keeps the bar grid, so the music enters on its written beat (`countInBar` in `beats.ts`). Before a full measure it is one whole bar, the measure's own beats. Before a pickup it is the beats of the pickup's bar that come before it, each shown by its beat in the bar, at the tempo of the start: Happy Birthday's one-beat pickup in 3/4 counts `1`, `2` and enters on 3; an eighth-note pickup in 4/4 counts `1` to `4` and enters on the "and" of 4. When that leaves fewer than two clicks, a whole bar comes first (`1 2 3 4 1` before a three-beat pickup in 4/4). The metronome puts a pickup's beats where they fall in its bar, none of them accented.
- Lead-in: the transport reports the count-in as it is heard (click 0 when Play starts it, each later click when it reaches the speakers, its end) and the lead-in, from Play or a seek while playing until the first note of a hand that is not muted is heard. A loop wrap to a start where the hands played rest starts one too. The cursor asks `leadingIn()` on every frame and keeps its marks until the next tick reports the lead-in, so no note of the new pass turns red early. Switching hands while playing starts no lead-in. It also asks `playingNow()` on every frame and marks nothing new once the transport has stopped: at the end the transport rewinds before `playing` follows, and a frame in between would mark bar 1 and raise the finish there.
- Clock: the cursor is clocked by `audibleTime(ctx)` (`clock.ts`), which maps `AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp()` to what is leaving the speakers now. The cursor stays in sync on devices with output latency (Bluetooth adds 150 to 250 ms).
- `?audio=silent` selects a silent instrument that makes no sound and fetches no samples. `?hooks=1` installs the same test hooks (`__digitus2Keys`, `__digitus2Along`, `__digitus2Timing`) with the real instrument and changes nothing else.

#### Ornament playback

MusicXML trills and upper/lower mordents sound automatically, and the keyboard follows their realised pitches. Short mordents use three attacks; `long="yes"` uses five, then holds the principal. Trills alternate with the upper diatonic neighbour through the first written note segment, at about eight attacks per score second, and default to ending on the principal. Supported explicit `start-note`, integer `beats` (2 to 64), `trill-step` and ornament `accidental-mark` instructions override those defaults. Neighbour pitches otherwise follow the active key and earlier same-measure, staff and octave accidentals, resolved in musical time across voices.

The original MusicXML is unchanged. Fingering export preserves its signs, written notes and principal fingerings; performance attacks are a separate view. The red cursor stays on the written note. Auxiliary keyboard pitches have no invented finger number, and their samples and keyboard range load automatically. No ornament control is added.

These are practice defaults, with no promise of historically authentic Bach execution or instruction for alternating fingers. Unsupported compound, accelerating or microtonal ornaments keep their notation and ordinary held-note playback. Extension lines do not extend realisation beyond the first written segment; ties then hold the principal. Live tempo changes normally wait for a safe attack boundary, bounded to 250 ms including the look-ahead. If interleaved ornaments have no safe boundary in that window, playback resynchronises with the existing 50 ms seek delay and rearticulates sounding notes.

### MIDI input

`src/midi/` connects the reader's own digital piano, USB or Bluetooth, so the red can wait for them (issue 182; the score side is Score view, Playing along on a piano). It is Web MIDI input only: the app opens no output, and MIDI input never sounds the reader's notes: nothing calls `strike`, and `__digitus2Keys.strikes()` stays empty while the reader plays. The other hand is played through `Playback.accompany`, from the walk (Score view, Playing along on a piano, The other hand).

| File                       | Loaded                                                       | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `types.ts`                 | static, types only                                           | The shared types: a note message, the connection's status and snapshot, and the events the lazy module reports.                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `piano.ts`                 | static, small                                                | The feature test (`midiSupported`) and the permission query (`midiPermission`), the one connection of the page with its store (status, the connected inputs' names, the held pitches, the listeners of the notes) and its React hooks, the press, Turn off and the silent resume on a later visit. |
| `access.ts` and `parse.ts` | one lazy chunk, reached only through `import('./access.js')` | `access.ts` calls `requestMIDIAccess({ sysex: false })`, attaches to every connected input and follows hot-plug. `parse.ts` is the pure byte parser and the held-key bookkeeping.                                                                                                                  |

- **Inputs.** Every connected input, all 16 channels, except Linux's loopback port (a name starting `Midi Through`). A pitch is held while any input and channel holds it, so two inputs or two channels on one key give one note-off, when the last lets go. A second note-on without a note-off is reported again.
- **Bytes.** Running status is kept across messages. A note-on at velocity 0 is a note-off. Pedal (CC 64), every other controller, program change, pitch bend and aftertouch are ignored. The realtime bytes 0xF8 to 0xFF (a Roland piano sends active sensing every 300 ms) are dropped wherever they fall, without touching running status or a message in progress. SysEx is skipped whole, also across reads, and ends at its 0xF7 or at any other status byte, so one lost 0xF7 cannot swallow the notes that follow; system common messages are skipped with their data bytes.
- **One connection per page.** `piano.ts` is a singleton: presses made while a request is open share it, a Turn off during a request discards the access when it resolves, and the events of a connection that is no longer the live one are dropped, so a late message never sticks a key. Strict Mode's second mount opens nothing twice.
- **Reading it.** `usePianoSnapshot()` gives the status and the inputs' names (the first use on any view starts the silent resume of a later visit); `useHeldPitches()` gives the held pitches, ascending, the same array until they change; `subscribeNotes()` hears every merged note-on and note-off synchronously, with the held pitches already updated. A MIDI message re-renders only the readers of `useHeldPitches`, which is `PianoKeyboard`; the score view hears notes through a subscription, outside render.
- **The time of a key** (issue 192). `access.ts` reads the event's `timeStamp` once per MIDI message (`eventTimeMs` in `parse.ts`) and keeps it when it is finite, positive, not ahead of `performance.now()` by more than 1 ms and at most `EVENT_MAX_AGE_MS` (1,000 ms) old; otherwise the arrival time stands in. A busy main thread can hold a message for tens to hundreds of milliseconds, which is when the event time matters; a stamp older than a second is another time base (some backends report 0 or an epoch time). Every note the listener reports carries it as `NoteMessage.atMs`, and a note-on also its `velocity` (1 to 127). The offs made when an input goes away carry the time of that moment, and the `lost` offs the store makes carry neither. The walk's timing reads them (Playing along on a piano, Timing in the walk); the walk itself counts holds from the arrival time.
- **Statuses.** `unsupported` (no Web MIDI), `off`, `asking` (the browser's prompt is open), `searching` (access is granted and the request has not answered), `connected`, `none` (access granted, no input; also what access that lists no input yet gives) and `denied`. A failure that is not a refusal (the lazy chunk did not load) goes back to `off` and leaves `settings.playAlong` as it was.

### Offline and installing

- **Installing.** `public/manifest.webmanifest` makes the app installable: name and short name `digitus2`, `display: standalone` (full screen without the browser's bars, the status bar kept), relative `id`, `start_url` and `scope` (`./`), so one file works under `/` and `/digitus2/`, and white `background_color` and `theme_color`. The icons are a white geometric 2 on black: `public/icon.svg` (also the favicon) and PNGs at 192, 512 (also maskable) and 180 px (`apple-touch-icon`) in `public/icons/`, rendered once by `scripts/render-icons.mjs` with Playwright's Chromium and committed. The app adds no install button and does not listen to `beforeinstallprompt`: Chrome on Android shows its own install UI, and iOS installs from Share, Add to Home Screen.
- **Registration.** `src/app/serviceWorker.ts`, called from `main.tsx`, registers `<base>sw.js` with the base as its scope after the page's `load` event, on production builds only (`vite dev` serves no worker), with `updateViaCache: 'none'`. A failed registration (blocked site data, an insecure origin) leaves the app online only, with nothing on screen and one console warning.
- **The worker.** `src/sw/sw.ts`, with its routing as pure, unit-tested functions in `src/sw/policy.ts`. A build-only Vite plugin (`src/sw/vitePlugin.ts`) runs at the end of every `vite build`: it lists the precache from the files written (`src/sw/precache.ts`: everything except source maps, `.vite/`, `sw.js`, `piano/` and the library's score files with their per-score chunks), hashes them into the build's version, and builds `sw.ts` into `dist/sw.js` as one classic script with both filled in. The build fails when the precache passes 24 MiB.
- **Caches.** The worker answers only same-origin GET requests inside its scope; everything else, the cross-origin scan service included, goes to the network untouched.

| Cache                         | Holds                                                                                                                                                                     | Strategy                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `digitus2-precache-<version>` | The shell: `index.html`, the web app manifest and icons, the JavaScript and CSS chunks (the engine, Verovio and PDF.js workers among them), the library list and manifest | Filled on install. Navigations go to the network first and, after 4 s or offline, get the cached `index.html` (the query ignored, so `?audio=silent` reloads offline) |
| `digitus2-assets`             | Other hashed `assets/` files: the score files and their per-score chunks, as pieces open                                                                                  | Cache first; only status 200 responses that are not `text/html`; at most 600 entries, the oldest stored going first                                                   |
| `digitus2-piano-samples-v1`   | The piano samples, as Play fetches them                                                                                                                                   | Cache first; `audio/*` responses only                                                                                                                                 |

- **Updates.** The browser checks `sw.js` for a new build on navigations, past its HTTP cache. Online, a page load always fetches `index.html` from the network, so a reload shows the newest build, whose new hashed files come from the network. The new worker itself installs its precache and then waits until every window of the app has closed, so an open page never loses the lazy chunks it was built with; only the reader's Reload ends the wait (below). On activation it deletes older precaches and an older piano cache, removes from `digitus2-assets` the files its own precache holds, keeps the scores and samples stored so far, and takes control of the open pages (`clients.claim()`), which is how the first visit is controlled.
- **The update notice.** While a newer worker waits, an open page says `A new version is ready.` on the notice line, gray like every notice and never red, with **Reload** and **Dismiss**. `serviceWorker.ts` offers a worker that waits when the page registers, one still installing and one found while the page is open, but not the first install (nothing controls the page yet). A refusal takes the line first, and opening a piece, a file or a scan, which clears the notice, leaves the offer in place (`updateReady` in the store, apart from the notice, which the first navigation would clear). **Reload** posts `skip-waiting` to the waiting worker (`sw.ts` calls `skipWaiting()` on it) and reloads the page once, when the controller changes. Nothing reloads by itself: the first worker's `clients.claim()` and another window's Reload change the controller of this page too. Every build carries its commit (below), so every deploy to Pages, a docs-only one included, is a new worker version and so an offer.
- **What works offline.** The library, and every piece opened before, after a reload or from the home screen; Play plays it with the samples its first Play fetched. A piece never opened, or a sample never fetched (another piece may need other pitches or velocity layers), needs the network; without it the score shows its error, or Play says the piano sounds could not be loaded.

- **Page turns** (issue #5, `cursorScrollDelta` in `useCursor.ts`). The cursor keeps itself readable above the dock and the music to come in view: when it enters a system that is not wholly in the band between the top of the window and the dock, the page scrolls so that the system's top sits 12 px below the top of the band (`SYSTEM_TOP_MARGIN`, air for the digits over the staff, less when the system only just fits), and the systems after it fill the band. While the system is wholly in the band nothing moves, so the page turns once per screenful, when the red reaches the first system that is not fully visible, the one after the last that is. Within a system that fits the band the page never scrolls, so a reader's own wheel or touch scroll during playback is not undone until the next turn. A system taller than the band gets its top at the band's top when its red notes are readable there, and otherwise, or when its red notes leave the band later in it, the red notes are centred. A seek, a cue and the paused marks use the same placement. The scroll is smooth, and instant under `prefers-reduced-motion`. On K.545 at 150 % on the desktop viewport the page turns fewer times than centring every system it entered would (`playback.spec.ts`, a count of turns against the same systems, never a time).
- The marked notes are drawn bold, stems and flags included, because a 9 by 8 px red notehead was easy to lose in a run of sixteenths: the red also fills the stem and the flag, and `base.css` draws the notehead at 1.4 times its size and the flag at 1.3 times (the engraved glyphs scaled about their own boxes, so the red note keeps the note's shape; an outline stroked around glyphs this small rounded them into blobs, issue #92) and the stem 3 px wide with round ends.
- During the lead-in (`playback.waiting`: the count-in and anything before the first note of the hands played) it marks nothing and presses no key. The first notes turn red, and their keys go down, when the transport reports that note heard, within a scheduler tick and a render of it (17 to 24 ms for Twinkle after the count-in, 4 runs at 1280 x 900). So no note is red before it sounds, and Pause stays the screen's one red element until then. The marks are made in a layout effect, so the plain Pause, the first red notes and their pressed keys reach the screen in one paint.
- The keyboard spans the piece's range with a 22 px minimum white key. A pressed key, white or black, is `--key-down` with its finger number.
- **A keyboard wider than its column** scrolls sideways inside itself and chooses its view once a bar (issue #104, `barView` in `Keyboard.tsx`), when the cursor enters it, in a layout effect and in the same frame as the keys change. It works from every key the played hands strike in that bar, one per attack, from the score shown. When they all fit, the view stays where it is if it holds them all, and is centred on them if it does not. When they do not fit, the view centres the stretch that holds the most attacks. It stays where it is instead if it already holds three fifths of that many (`VIEW_STICKS`), so two stretches that are nearly as good do not trade places at every bar line. The jump is instant. Within a bar the view never moves, so a texture wider than a phone's keyboard does not swing the keys from end to end at every note. The e2e spec `keyboard-view.spec.ts` checks that the keyboard moves at most once a bar on Horse Race, a stride bass under chords two octaves up, at 360 px.

  | Horse Race, 360 px, 25 bars                | Keyboard moves | Pressed keys in view |
  | ------------------------------------------ | -------------- | -------------------- |
  | A new view at every bar, nothing kept      | 22             | 59 %                 |
  | Kept at three quarters                     | 13             | 56 %                 |
  | Kept at three fifths (`VIEW_STICKS = 0.6`) | 4              | 55 %                 |
  | Kept at a half                             | 2              | 44 %                 |

  Choosing the view at every onset, as the keyboard used to, moved it 26 times in 9 bars.

- **Hand letters** (`src/score/handLetters.ts`). A pressed key also shows an L or R for the hand, but only in bars where the hands could be confused. An onset is close when both hands sound and the lowest right-hand key minus the highest left-hand key, counting the keys struck there and those still held, is 7 semitones (a perfect fifth) or less, or negative (the hands interleave). A bar with a close onset is lettered, and so is a lone bar between two lettered bars, so the letters do not leave for one bar and come back. This is decided once per piece from the score, both hands, whatever is muted, so it is the same at every width and on every run. While the cursor's group, playing or paused, is in a lettered bar, every pressed and dimmed key shows its L or R; elsewhere none does. The letters keep their size (10 px on white keys, 9 px on black) on a phone too, so a hand scrolled out of the phone's keyboard leaves its visible numbers unlettered. Twinkle shows no letters in bars 1 to 3 and both L and R from bar 4; Amazing Grace never shows one.
- **One hand.** With a hand muted, the keys that hand has down at the time heard (struck at an onset of either hand, or still held) show dimmed to 40 % (`.is-muted`) and never pressed. They follow that hand's own notes, also while the hand played holds one. A dimmed black key has an opaque paper backing, so the white keys' outlines do not run through it. The score dims that hand too (`mutedElements` in `render/dom.ts`, found once per layout for each selection): its notes with their beams and rests, and its digits, at 40 %. The notes are dimmed shape by shape (fill and stroke opacity, inherited), never through a translucent group: Chrome gives a translucent group holding transformed shapes (every notehead is a scaled `use`) a compositor layer of its own, and whatever overlaps it another. A dimmed digit is text alone, so at rest it is drawn through its own group opacity, which keeps its gray smoothing (translucent text gets coloured subpixel fringes on Windows). Staff lines, clefs, slurs, ties and dynamics stay ink. A note of that hand inside the cursor's chord (a chord shared by both hands) is never pale, nor is a cursor note inside a group being dimmed (a switch while playing), and print shows both hands in full ink.
- **Paused.** After a note was heard, Pause keeps the last group heard red and its keys pressed with their fingers (the muted hand's still dimmed). Play is then plain (`resumeShown`) and resumes there. Paused in the lead-in, before any note of the hands played was heard, nothing is marked and Play is red. Rewind while paused, and the end of the piece, clear the marks and the keys (`Playback.atStart`), and Play takes the red again. While paused, a change of hands marks the last group heard of the hands now played (or clears the marks, and Play turns red, when there is none), and a new layout or a re-solve marks the same group again with its new digits. A loop set while paused that leaves the pause position outside it makes Play start at the loop's first bar, so the marks clear and Play is red again.
- **Cued.** Stopped after a seek (below), the first group of the hands played at or after the bar's start is marked, with its keys pressed, wherever the cursor was and whatever was heard before (`Playback.cue`), and the page scrolls to it as the playing cursor would. Play starts there. A pause in the lead-in that follows marks it again; the first note heard ends the cue.
- **The keyboard answers** (issue #96), with no control. Stopped or paused, the key of the note under the mouse pointer goes down in `--key-down` with the note's finger number, like a cue, and lifts when the pointer leaves (`score/useHoveredNote.ts`): a beginner's "which key is this note" is answered without a click. The hit test is the per-page measurement of `score/bars.ts`: the pages are located once per layout, and a page's notes (`g.note` boxes, 3 px of reach around each) are measured the first time the pointer is over that page, never per pointer move (`noteBoxes` in `score/noteKey.ts`). Mouse only: on a touch screen a tap already cues a bar and presses its keys. Playing, the keyboard belongs to the cursor and nothing is hovered. A hovered key the cursor already holds keeps the cursor's finger; a hovered key is never lettered; the bar's tint is unchanged.
- **A key pressed on the keyboard sounds** (`score/useKeyPress.ts`, `Playback.strike`): it goes down as a struck key does (`--dur-press`, and `restrike` when it was already down) with no number, and sounds at its hand's unmarked velocity (the hand that plays that pitch in the piece, else the right hand), held while the pointer is down and two seconds at most (`KEY_HOLD_SEC`). One key at a time, the primary pointer; a touch that turns into a scroll of the keyboard cancels the press; the click is handled, so no notes rise from it. Before the first Play the samples are not loaded: the first key pressed loads them with the status line's `Loading the piano…` (Play busy and unavailable meanwhile, as on a first Play) and sounds when they arrive, or the notice says the piano sounds could not be loaded. It does nothing to the transport: playing goes on, stopped stays stopped, and Play keeps the red. With `?audio=silent` the silent instrument records the strikes (`window.__digitus2Keys.strikes()`, for the e2e spec `keyboard`).
- **The keys take no focus.** They are a pointer affordance: at 22 px a key is far below the 44 px target size a button would need, the SVG stays `role="img"` with its one label, and the piece's own notes sound through Play, which the keyboard reaches. Pressed and hovered keys are gray, never red: the screen keeps exactly one red element in every state of the keyboard.
- **The reader's keys** (issue 182). Each key held on the reader's own piano shows a gray bar along its front edge (`rect.key-held`, `PianoKeyboard.tsx` and `Keyboard.tsx`'s `held`), whether the score is stopped, paused or playing. It sits inside the key's own group, so it goes down and up with the key's travel, over the key and its finger number and under the hand letters, with `pointer-events: none` and `aria-hidden`. On a dimmed key (`.is-muted`, drawn at 40 % opacity as a whole) it sits beside the group instead, at full strength (`is-dimmed`, `data-for-midi`), because inside the group it would be dimmed with the key and fall to 1.5:1; a dimmed key is never pressed, so it has no travel to follow.

  | Key   | x         | y              | Width         | Height | Corner |
  | ----- | --------- | -------------- | ------------- | ------ | ------ |
  | White | key x + 3 | key height − 8 | key width − 6 | 5      | 1      |
  | Black | key x + 2 | key height − 6 | key width − 4 | 4      | 1      |

  The bar is additive: it never changes what is pressed, dimmed, struck or hovered, so a cued key held on the piano shows the cue's fill and finger plus the bar. A black key that is neither down (cursor, hover or pointer) nor dimmed is black, so its bar takes its own gray (`is-on-black`); a dimmed key's bar takes the gray that reads on the key as dimmed (Light and dark). Held pitches outside the drawn range are not drawn, and the keyboard's view never scrolls to follow them (`barKeys` still choose it). The bar is never red; the colours are in Light and dark. `PianoKeyboard` reads the held pitches itself, so a MIDI message re-renders the keyboard and nothing else in the score view. Mouse and touch presses on the keyboard sound as before and never move the red.

- **The keys fill** (issue 185; when a key gets a level and how long it lasts are in Playing along on a piano). A key with an entry in the level store draws a fill of the bar's own gray, `rect.key-level`, that rises from the bar over the note's length. It sits in the key's group after the key and before its number, with the bar's x, width and corner, and the held bar lies over it, so its foot is the bar. Its bottom is the bar's bottom, and it is at least the bar high at any level above 0:

  | Key   | Bottom | Level 0   | Level 1 (y, height) |
  | ----- | ------ | --------- | ------------------- |
  | White | 97     | no height | 3, 94               |
  | Black | 60     | no height | 2, 58               |

  Between, the height is the bar's plus the way to the key's top inset (3 on a white key, 2 on a black one) times the level. A wrong note has no entry and shows its bar alone; a dimmed key (`.is-muted`) never draws a level, even if the store holds one, and its bar is as before. A level is drawn whether or not its key is still held, so a drain shows after the key is up.
  - **The number and the letter on it.** Where the level covers the finger number, the number is drawn again after the original as `text.key-on-level`, in `--key-on-level` and clipped to the level, with the same position and text, `aria-hidden`. The original stays the key's first text, so `querySelector('text')` still finds it. The hand letter gets the same copy, `text.key-hand-on-level` (not of class `key-hand`, so the letter counts stay), in the letters' layer. That layer is not in the keys' groups and does not travel with them, so its clip is the level's rectangle through the pressed key's scale (`KEY_TRAVEL`: 0.976 white, 0.96 black, checked against `base.css` by `Keyboard.test.tsx`): exact at rest, and off by under a unit during the 50 ms press. The copies exist only while the key has a level, and ignore the pointer.
  - **A black key that shows no state** (an early press, or a key held past its note) draws its level with `--key-held-on-black` (`is-on-black`), as its bar does. It shows no number, as a black key shows one only when pressed or dimmed, so it has no copy.
  - **One loop.** `useKeyLevels.ts` writes `y` and `height` of the level rectangles and of their clip rectangles, straight on the SVG: a layout effect writes every level before the paint, then one `requestAnimationFrame` loop runs only while some level moves (rising below full, or draining), and stops when none does, so a full key held costs nothing. Each frame reads the clock and the reduced-motion query once and writes an attribute only when its value changed. The elements are looked up once per change of the levels or of the shape of what is drawn (a number or a letter can appear while the levels are the same), never per frame. A hidden tab, which gets no frames, is right again at the next one, because the geometry is a function of the time. The level is never red.

#### Moving through the piece

The score is the timeline (T13, issue #23): there is no scrub bar or progress line, which would be a permanent control and means little to a beginner who reads bars.

- **A click or tap anywhere in a bar** goes to that bar's start (`score/BarSeek.tsx`, `score/bars.ts`). A bar's box runs between its bar lines and from its upper staff to its lower one, both hands, reaching three quarters of a staff above and below for the digits; notes and digits inside it count too. The boxes are measured a page at a time, the first time a pointer or a step needs that page, so a long piece's pages off screen stay unrendered (measuring all 780 bars of Chopin's Scherzo No. 2 at the first hover took 241 ms). Only a plain click counts: the primary button, no modifier, no drag, no text being selected or deselected (the note burst's own test, `isEmptyClick`). It is handled, so no notes rise from it. With a loop set, only the loop's bars take a click.
- **Keys.** Left and Right go one bar back or forward from the bar being heard (or the bar Play starts in), Shift with them four bars, within the piece and the loop. Home is Rewind (the page also goes to its top, as Home does anywhere, except while a loop is set and the red waits on its first step: then the page stays with the red). The keys stay with a focused field, slider, select, radio, tab or list, and do nothing with Ctrl, Alt or Meta.
- **Stopped**, the bar is cued (above): its first note takes the red and Play starts there. A seek to the very start while stopped is Rewind, and the red flies home.
- **Playing**, the music goes on from the bar's start after a lead-in; no count-in.
- **The red hops** (`hop` in `score/RedFlight.tsx`): from where it is, the cursor's notehead or Play (Pause in a lead-in), to the topmost notehead of the bar's first group, on an arc that rises 12 to 40 px above the higher end (15 % of the distance), stretched a little in the air and squashing as it lands, in 220 to 340 ms with the distance (`HOP_MIN_MS`, `HOP_MAX_MS`). Playing, the music waits for it (`Transport.seek(sec, { leadSec })`) and comes in as it lands. Stopped, the cue is drawn in ink until it lands (`.score-sheet.is-hopping`), then breathes like a paused cursor. It hops only between places on screen that stay put: a seek to a bar out of view, or one made while the red is already travelling, jumps, and the page scrolls. Nothing hops under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Feedback.** The bar under a mouse pointer takes a light tint of the ink (`--bar-tint`, 4 % in light, 8 % in dark) and the pointer cursor; the bar a seek lands on leaves a footprint, the ink at 10 % fading over 640 ms, so a tap on a phone, where nothing hovers, and a step with the keys both show where they went. Neither is ever red.
- **Help.** The Practice panel's last line says how: `Click a bar to go there. ← and → move one bar.`, or on a touch screen (`(hover: none)`) `Tap a bar to go there.`

#### Playing along on a piano

With a piano connected (Library, Play along), Practice mode on and the score stopped, the red marks the next group of notes to play. It is the existing cue: the notes are red, Play is plain and their keys are pressed with their fingers. When the reader has held every pitch of that group for its time, the red moves on to the next one, and the page turns as it does for the playing cursor. The reader's held keys show on the on-screen keyboard and fill over the length of their notes (Cursor and keyboard; issue 185). The app makes no sound for the reader's notes; Play still plays the piece, and with one hand left out digitus plays the other hand between the reader's steps (The other hand, below). The one control is the Practice mode switch in the Practice panel (below). The code is `score/playAlong.ts` (pure, with the times of the steps) and `score/accompaniment.ts` (pure), `score/keyLevels.ts` (pure, the levels of the held keys and the small store that holds them), `score/usePlayAlong.ts` (the glue between the piano store and `Playback`) and seven members of `Playback`: `cueAt` and `finish`, and for the other hand `prepare`, `preparing`, `soundWaits`, `accompany` and `stopAccompaniment`.

**Practice mode** (issue 191). A switch heads the dock's Practice panel while a piano is connected and the setting is on, whatever the walk is doing. On, the walk runs as described here. Off, the score page is as with no piano: Play plays the piece, the red follows the music, nothing waits, and there is no hold cue, no hint and no accompaniment. The piano stays connected either way and the keys the reader presses show on the on-screen keyboard. The switch is one gate: `usePlayAlong` computes `walking = setting && practiceMode && status === 'connected'` and uses it where it used the connection, for the plan of steps, the accompaniment and `active`, so Off is the walk's existing inactive path. The hook also returns `pianoConnected` (the setting is on and the status is `connected`), which decides whether the panel shows the switch. Turning it off ends the walk as an external move does: the accompaniment stops, the levels, the early flag and the wake-up timer clear, the cue stays where the walk left it, Play is plain and starts there, and turning it off while Play plays changes nothing visible. Turning it on again resumes from where the red stands with every held key spent, so a key held through the switch, or pressed while it was off, never counts and a fresh press does. The home line is unchanged: it is about the connection, and the switch is about what the score page does with it.

**Steps.** The red walks the piece in the order it is heard, so a repeat is walked twice and a second ending comes after the first pass.

- A step is one onset of the performance score (`performanceFor(score).score`: repeats, voltas, D.C., D.S., Fine and Coda in the order heard) for the hands played. Its pitches are those of the notes that start there, distinct and ascending.
- A note held from earlier is not asked for again: a bass held under a moving melody is asked for at its own onset only. A unison of both hands, or of two voices, is one pitch. A tied note is asked for once, at its start; its continuation has no onset. A grace note is never asked for, and an onset with nothing to press (graces only, or only the hand left out) is no step. Pitch is exact, with no octave folding.
- A note with an ornament asks for the pitch the cued keyboard shows at the group's onset, so a trill that starts on its upper note asks for the upper note.
- The steps are built only while a piano is connected, so a reader without one pays nothing for them.

**When a step passes** (issue 185). The hook keeps the keys held on the piano, each with whether its press is still fresh, that is, not yet used by a step, and when it went down. The red waits for the length of the note: a step passes once its keys have been held for its time, not at the press.

- A note-on makes the pitch held and fresh and stamps it with `performance.now()`, taken in the note listener as the message arrives; a second note-on without a note-off in between (two inputs, a sloppy keyboard) makes it fresh again and stamps it again. A note-off removes the pitch once no input or channel holds it. Lifting the key is the release: the damper pedal is not read (MIDI input).
- **The run.** A step runs while every one of its pitches is held and fresh. Its run starts at the latest press among them (`runSince`), so a chord is counted from its last key. A wrong note does nothing: the red stays. Extra keys stay as they are. What the hook keeps for what should follow the reader's own press is what it last handed the other hand over for (`handedRef`: the step, the press that started its run, the tempo factor); the accompaniment of [issue 184](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/184) starts when the run differs from it (The other hand).
- **The hold** is how long the run lasts before the step passes. `alongPlan` gives each step its times, with the shortest non-grace note of the hands played starting at its tick (both voices, and both hands of a unison):

  | Step                        | Hold, in performance seconds                                      |
  | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | Any step                    | the gap to the next step, never more than its shortest note       |
  | The piece's last step       | its shortest note                                                 |
  | The last step inside a loop | the loop's end minus its onset, never more than its shortest note |

  A rest after a note is not waited out, and neither is a rest before a loop's end. The hold is never below 0, so a note of no length passes at the press. Real milliseconds are `hold * 1000 / factor` with the factor of the open piece's Practice tempo, so at 150 % every hold is two thirds as long and at 50 % twice as long. The steps follow the performance order, so in a repeat the last step of the first pass holds the gap to the first step of the repeated bar, capped at its shortest note.

- **On its time.** A running step passes when `now - runSince` reaches its hold. Its pitches are then used. A tied note counts whole and a note running past a repeat's end counts to that end, as the performance clips it.
- **A release near the end.** A key of the running step that comes up after four fifths of its hold (`HOLD_TOLERANCE`) passes the step at the release, judged before the key is removed, so a beginner who plays detached is not held up. A key released earlier is removed alone: the other keys stay fresh, the red stays where it is and the status line asks for a longer hold (below). A fresh press of that key restarts the run from the new press. A key released while its step does not run, or one that is not in the running step, is a plain release: no pass, no hint.
- **Chains.** After a pass, the next step is checked at the same moment against its own run, so a key pressed a moment early for it counts from its own press, and a chain covers exactly the steps whose time is up (at most as many passes as there are steps). One `cueAt` (or `finish`) goes to where the chain ends. A release is judged against the first step of the chain whose pitches include the released key, so a timer that fires late gives the same end state as one that fires on time. A key used by one step never counts again, so a repeated pitch (Twinkle's C C) needs a release and a fresh press.
- **The timer.** One `setTimeout`, set after every look at the walk that leaves a step running, wakes the hook when that step is due (`runSince` plus its hold), and every look clears it first. Time is read from timestamps and never counted in ticks, so a throttled timer in a hidden tab, firing late, passes everything that is due. It is cleared when the walk becomes inactive and at unmount.
- **A flight on screen.** While a flight or hop is on screen nothing passes, but presses keep their times, and when the red lands the align settles at that moment, so a step whose time ran out during the flight passes at once. A release during a flight is a plain one and shows no hint: the flight is the app's move, not the reader's.
- A chord is counted from its last key while the others are still held. A key released before the last one is down must be pressed again.
- Keys already held when the score view opens count as used.
- An external move uses up every held key (all become not fresh) and clears the hint and every level. An external move is any change of where Play starts that the walk did not make: a seek, the arrow keys, Rewind, a loop change, a hands change, a new score, Play then Pause, or the piano connecting. The hook recognises its own moves because it remembers the performance second it last left the playback at. It checks on every note and every look at the playback, before the note is applied, so a key pressed during a seek's hop counts for the step the hop lands on.
- A key pressed while a flight or hop is on screen is kept and counts, from its own press, once it lands. A key pressed while the walk is inactive (the transport plays, the samples load, the score is laid out again) is used up when the walk is active again, so a reader who holds a chord through a new layout presses it again.

**The keys fill** (issue 185). A key held on the piano shows how long its note lasts: a level of the bar's own gray rises inside the key over the note's written length at the Practice tempo, and a full key means the note was held long enough (drawing: Cursor and keyboard). The levels live in a small store (`keyLevels.ts`) that `usePlayAlong` writes and `PianoKeyboard` reads; a level rising re-renders nothing in the score view.

- **Which keys, and from when.** After every look at the walk that reaches the settle, each pitch held and fresh that has no level (a draining level counts as none) gets one if it belongs to the step the red waits on or to the step after it in walk order (a key pressed early). It counts from that key's own press, so a level is started at the press, or when the red arrives for a key already held. A wrong note, a pitch of the hand left out and a key held but spent get none. A press during a flight gets its level when the red lands, at the height its press time gives.
- **Each key's own length.** A key's length is the longest note of its pitch among the step's notes: two voices in unison take the longer, an ornamented note gives its required pitch the principal note's length, and a tie counts whole (to the end of the run it lies in, as the hold does). Its length in beats is that note's ticks over the beat of the time signature in force at the step.
- **The bass keeps on.** A level lasts until its key comes up, so a whole note held in the left hand keeps filling after the red has moved on to the melody over it.
- **The drain.** A key let go before it is full drains from the level it had reached to nothing over `--dur-slow`, read from the computed style (`durationMs`); a full key loses its level, with its bar, at once. A press of a draining key starts a new level. A release during a flight drains too.
- **Cleared at once.** An external move clears every level, and so does the walk becoming inactive (Play, loading, the piano going, a new layout, unmount), so no level is ever drawn while the transport plays, while the samples load or without a piano.
- **Reduced motion.** The level steps a beat at a time. The beat is the time signature's own, `divisions * 4 / beatType` ticks, which is the beat the metronome and the count-in click (`beatTicksAt` in `audio/beats.ts`, as `buildBeats`), so the level steps on the clicks a beginner hears: quarters in 4/4 and 3/4, eighths in 6/8 and 3/8, halves in 2/2. A note of `beats` beats shows `floor(f * beats) / beats`, where `f` is the fraction of its real length that has passed, so the stepping needs no tempo and a tempo change in the middle of a note cannot put it out of step. A note of one beat or less shows nothing until its end, then full. The drain is instant, since `--dur-slow` is 0 there.
- **Ties at a repeat.** `performanceOf` clips a note's end at its run's end, so a tie across a repeat's end counts, for the hold and for the level, up to that end.

**The walk** is active when the setting is on and the status is `connected`, the score is ready and playable, it has a step, and it is neither playing nor loading its samples. It moves the red only when no flight or hop is on screen (`red.redAway`), so there are never two reds; it never calls `cueAt` while the transport plays. Its look at the playback is a passive effect declared after the carry of `Add notes between`, so it runs after the layout effects of `RedFlight` and `EndBurst` and after the carry in the same commit. It moves the red only through `Playback.cueAt(perfSec)` and `Playback.finish()`:

- `cueAt` is a seek in performance seconds, stopped only. It sets `cue` to the written second and `atStart` to false, also at second 0, so the cued branch of `useCursor` marks the first group at or after it. It is never `seek`: a written second enters a bar going back at its last hearing, so a walk made of `seek`s would loop a repeat's first pass for ever. Before the first Play the position waits in `pendingPerfRef`, beside `pendingSecRef`, which is unchanged, so a seek made before the first Play with no cue before it behaves as it always did (the first hearing); `rewind` (so `finish` too) and a new score clear it, and the new transport starts at it with `seekPerformance`. A `seek` made while a cue waits moves it to the written second in the hearing the cue is in, as `Transport.seek` does with a transport (`toPerformanceSec` with where Play starts as the reference), so the arrow keys and a bar click in a repeat's second hearing stay in that hearing before any Play. A cue is not made again when the playback already shows it, so a second look changes nothing.
- `finish` is the transport's own end: the position goes to the top, `cue` is null, `atStart` is true and `ended` counts one, which `EndBurst` and the red's flight home answer.

- **Status line.** Two more lines, the lowest in priority and shown only while the walk is active. Under the count-in and above the other, while digitus has a hand to play and the browser holds the sound back: `Click the page once to hear the other hand.` (The other hand). The other: `Play the red note on your piano.`, or `Play the red notes on your piano.` when the step in front of the reader has more than one pitch. It shows until the first step has been played in this score view, then goes and does not come back in this view; no step is announced. After a release that came too early the line reads `Hold the red note a little longer.` (`Hold the red notes a little longer.` for a step of several pitches), in all five languages, and it wins over the first line, being the more specific advice. That hint shows while the red is cued at its step, and goes with the next pass, an external move and the walk becoming inactive; a release that changes nothing re-renders nothing. After a bar with a ring, the first time in the view: `A ring before a note means it came early; after it, late.` (Timing in the walk).
- **No breathing while waiting.** The cue does not breathe while the walk is active (`resting` in `ScoreSheet` is off), or every step would restart the three breaths.
- **The finish and the page being hidden.** The first step is cued again a frame after the position goes home (`requestAnimationFrame`), because the red's flight home is planned in a layout effect of the same commit and only shows in the next render. In a tab that is hidden at that moment the frame comes when the tab is shown again, and the first step is cued then. A key pressed between the finish and that frame is kept for the first step and moves nothing: until the frame the hook still holds the playback of before the finish, and cueing from it would put the red on the first step under the flight home.
- **Rewind after an external seek.** Whether the red waits on the first step is read from the playback in each render (it is cued at the first step's second), so a seek whose hop is still on screen has already left it: Rewind, Home and a click on bar 1 work at once, and the hint names the step the hop lands on only when it lands.

**Timing in the walk** (issue 192). While the reader walks the piece on their piano, digitus compares their rhythm with the written one, against their own pulse, and after each bar it marks the note that came most clearly early or late. The red still waits; no control is added; Play plays the song and judges nothing. The code is `score/walkTiming.ts` (pure: the plan, the judge, the rings' data), `score/useWalkTiming.ts` (the listener), `score/render/timingMarks.ts` (the rings; their shape is `markShape` alone) and the walk's read-only tap (`Settled.passedRuns` in `playAlong.ts`, `onTiming` in `usePlayAlong.ts`).

- **Onsets.** A step's onset is the start of the run that passed it (`runSince`): its last key down, so a rolled chord counts from its last key and a step pressed early from its own press. The time is the decisive key's event time (`NoteMessage.atMs`, MIDI input), found in the listener's own log of note-ons, which subscribes before the walk's; the arrival time stands in when the log does not have it. Releases are not judged.
- **The reader's pulse.** Each gap between consecutive onsets is compared with the written gap in performance seconds, which carry the tempo marks, the rests and the repeats. The tempo is the median ratio of real to written gaps within `TEMPO_WINDOW` gaps, so any speed works and slow drift is followed; a steady latency moves every onset alike and cancels in the gaps.
- **The verdict.** A waiting walk has no clock to return to, so a note is judged by the gap into it: early when that gap is shorter than the written one at the reader's tempo by more than the tolerance, late when longer. A gap that only puts back the one before it (a note moved, then the pulse resumed) is not rung, so one early note gives one ring and a lasting shift gives at most one, on the note after it. The tolerance is the largest of `TOLERANCE_FLOOR_MS`, `TOLERANCE_BEATS` of the felt beat (a dotted quarter in 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8, else the time signature's beat) and `TOLERANCE_GAP` of the written gap, both at the reader's tempo. Dotted and triplet subdivisions inside a beat stay within it and are not judged.
- **Pauses and breaks.** A gap longer than the rhythm asks by more than `PAUSE_FLOOR_MS` plus the smaller of (`PAUSE_RATIO` − 1) times the expected gap and `PAUSE_BEATS` felt beats is a pause: the walk doing its job, not judged. So a search for the next notes after a whole note, or a fermata held as written, is a pause. The chain also breaks at every move the walk did not make (a seek, Rewind, a loop or hands change, Play then Pause), when the walk goes inactive, at a loop's wrap, at the finish and at a release too early (the step is pressed again). The first gap after a break or a pause is never rung, and a stretch of fewer than `MIN_FIT_ONSETS` onsets judges nothing. After a note followed by a rest the red moves on when the note ends, so that gap is neither judged nor counted in the tempo.
- **When.** A bar is judged when the walk leaves it: the red moves into the next bar, the loop wraps, or the piece ends. A visit a seek cuts short is not judged. At the finish only early notes are marked, because a written `rit.` is not in the tempo map.
- **The rings.** At most `MAX_RINGS_PER_BAR` ringed step per bar, the one furthest past its tolerance, with one thin gray ring per staff on its notes, before them when it came early and after them when late, as far as the local spacing says (`ringFor`: at least `RING_MIN_SHIFT` noteheads, at most half the gap to the neighbour). They are keyed by the written bar, so a repeat's second hearing marks the same notes. A bar's rings go at the first step passed when it is played again; the others stay. A new score, a hands change, Practice mode off, the piano gone and Play clear them all; a seek, Rewind or a loop change keep them. Each ring has the radius `RING_SCALE`, 0.6 of a notehead's width, is appended as the last child of its `g.staff`, outside every `g.layer`, `g.note` and `g.chord`, so the dimmed hand and the red never reach it, and is stroked with `--gray-3`, 1.5 px at every width (`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`), with no opacity and no filter; `pointer-events: none`, so hover and bar clicks pass through; hidden in print; drawn with no motion. ScoreView draws them in a layout effect on the pages built and on each page built later (`decorate`), and never builds a page for them.
- **The explanation.** The first time rings appear in the view the status line says `A ring before a note means it came early; after it, late.` (all five languages) until two more bars are judged, a break or a clear, and never again in that view. It ranks under the hold hint and above the play hint.

**The other hand** (issue 184). With a piano connected and Practice mode on, the Practice panel's Hands control also chooses which hand digitus plays; with Practice mode off, Hands keeps only its plain meaning, the hand Play plays. `Right hand`: the reader plays the right hand on their piano and digitus plays the left. `Left hand`: the mirror. `Both hands`, and a piece with notes in one hand only: digitus plays nothing, and nothing is prepared for it. No control is added. The hands digitus plays are the hands the walk leaves out (`mutedHandsFor(handsFor(score, hands))`). It plays them in rhythm between the reader's steps, counted from the press that starts each step running, and then waits for the reader. Play, the transport, is unchanged, and the reader's own notes are never sounded.

- **When it starts.** A step runs when every pitch of it is held and fresh, from the latest press among them (`runSince`; the step passes a note's length later, issue 185). The walk hands the other hand over when a step starts running, so the stretch is counted from the press, and the pass later hands over nothing. What was handed over decides, not what was seen (`handedRef` in `usePlayAlong.ts`, cleared by every move the walk did not make, by the walk going inactive, by the finish and when a look finds no step running): a run that did not change hands nothing over again, so a flight or hop over a run with no move (a click on the bar the red already waits on, Left at a loop's first bar, with a key down), and the landing or the pass that comes after it, change nothing, while a step let go and pressed again under a flight is a new press and is handed over. A step pressed before the red reached it (its keys went down while the step before still ran) is counted from its own press: it is handed over when the red reaches it, with the part of the stretch that has gone by since the press left out (`accompanimentStretches`' `elapsedSec`), so the rest sounds as if it had been handed over at the press and the stretch ends when the step's hold does. A note overdue by 100 ms or less (`LATE_MS`) is struck at once, so a press a few milliseconds early, as legato makes, keeps the step's downbeat; an older one is left out, not struck late. A chain of steps that all pass in one decision without having been seen running (all due at once) plays only from the last step passed, from that pass.
- **Stretches.** From the step that starts running, digitus plays to the next step the walk waits on, in performance seconds (`accompanimentStretches`, from the running step as `last`, and the move the pass will make: `stepAfter`). Every stretch starts at the press. `advance` and `settle` also return the `last` step passed.

| Move                                           | The stretch                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `cue j`, no wrap                               | from step `last` to step `j`                                     |
| `cue j`, a loop wrap (`j` is not after `last`) | from step `last` to the loop's end; then digitus waits           |
| `finish`                                       | from step `last` to the end of the performance: the piece's tail |
| `stay`                                         | none                                                             |

- **A new stretch drops the one before.** What the stretch before left unstarted is dropped, so a step pressed before the notes of the previous stretch have all started starts its stretch at once and the unstarted notes never sound.
- **An early release.** A release before four fifths of the step's hold leaves the red on the step (issue 185) and leaves the other hand playing: the stretch is the piece's rhythm, as when the reader stops after a step, and it plays to the next step and waits. A new press of the step runs it from that press, and its stretch starts again with it, dropping the notes of the first that have not started.
- **Which notes** (`accompanimentNotes`). The note list of Play (`playEventsFor(performanceFor(score).score)`) filtered to the hands digitus plays. The onset decides the stretch, half-open, so a note at the next step belongs to the next stretch; `sec` decides the time. A note sounds `sec - from` after the press, never before it: a rolled note sounds late and a grace note before its principal, so a grace of the principal that opens a stretch sounds with it, at the press. A note rings its own length, pedal included, past the end of the stretch; inside a loop its length is cut at the loop's end, and a note that would only sound at or after that end is left out, as the transport leaves it.
- **Velocities and ornaments** are Play's: each note at `noteVelocity`, through the same floor, with the ornaments realised as the transport realises them. The reader's key velocity does not change the other hand.
- **Unisons.** A note of the other hand with the pitch and the onset of a note of a hand the reader plays is left out: that pitch comes from the reader's piano at that moment.
- **Before the first step: not played.** The other hand's notes before the step the walk waits on, when no step was played before, are not played: the opening of a piece (a pickup, or an introduction in the other hand), the bar entered by a seek, Rewind, a loop or hands change, Play then Pause or the piano connecting, and the opening of a loop on every lap (the wrap stretch ends at the loop's end). There is no press to count them from, and the reader leads. A piece whose other hand begins alone starts silent at the reader's first note; Play plays the introduction.
- **The end and the loop.** After the piece's last step the tail plays while the burst rises and the red flies home. The walk's finish and its cue of step 0 afterwards are its own moves and do not stop it, and a press of step 0 during the tail starts its stretch at once. In a loop digitus plays to the loop's end and waits.
- **Tempo.** Offsets and lengths are divided by the Practice tempo factor read when the stretch is handed over, so a stretch takes twice as long at 50 %. A tempo change in the middle of a run, with the step still held, hands its stretch over again at the new tempo, counted from the press like the hold, which the new tempo moves too (`Stretch.heard`): the notes that already sounded, at any tempo the run had, are not struck again, and one that the new tempo made due and had not sounded is struck at once. A tempo change while the step is not held leaves the stretch playing at the tempo it started at, and applies from the next press.
- **What stops it** (`stopAccompaniment`: idempotent, never `stopAll`, so a struck key and the transport's notes are untouched). The walk calls it on every move it did not make (a seek, the arrow keys, Rewind, a loop change, a hands change, Play then Pause, a new score), when it goes inactive (playing, loading, the piano unplugged, Turn off) and when the view is left; `Playback` calls it at the start of `play()`, in the click, before the transport schedules anything, when a new score or the unmount halts it, and when the audio is held (the context leaves `running`). The walk's own cue and finish do not call it.
- **Samples.** The walk calls `prepare()` in an effect while it is active with a hand left out, before any press and with no gesture (Audio). `Loading the piano…` shows on the status line while the samples load (`loading` or `preparing`) and the walk stays active: the red moves on every press, and nothing sounds until the samples cover the score. A failed load leaves the walk working without sound.
- **The browser's autoplay policy** (Audio). After a click on a piece in the library the page has had a user activation and the context `prepare` makes runs. A fresh load straight onto a score address has none: the context is `suspended`, the walk works fully, nothing sounds and nothing is queued, and the status line asks for one click: `Click the page once to hear the other hand.` (`Tap the page once to hear the other hand.` on a touch screen, `TOUCH_QUERY`). The click may land anywhere (a click on a bar also seeks, which is an ordinary external move), a key press works too, and the sentence goes when the context runs.

### The dock and Practice

Play or Pause, Rewind, the Practice controls and the keyboard sit in a dock (`.dock`) that sticks to the bottom of the window while the score column is on screen, so the music can be stopped wherever the cursor has scrolled the score. At the end of the column it sits in its own place below the last system, so it never hides the music or the side panel for good.

- **Dock height.** The height, measured with a `ResizeObserver`, is published on the root as `--dock-h` and is the page's bottom scroll padding: a focused control scrolled into view stops above the dock, and the cursor's scroll keeps the whole current system above it (`cursorScrollDelta` in `useCursor.ts`). The dock's own controls take that padding back with a negative `scroll-margin-bottom` of the same height, so focusing or clicking Play, Rewind or the keyboard never scrolls the page while the dock is on screen.
- **Layout.** From 480 px the `Practice` toggle and the status line share the first row, and Play and Rewind stand stacked beside the keyboard. Below 480 px the status line has a row of its own above Play, Rewind and Practice in one row, then the keyboard. The toggle is rendered where it stands on screen (`useMediaQuery(NARROW_QUERY)`), so the DOM order, and with it the tab order, is the order on screen at every width: Practice, Play, Rewind from 480 px; Play, Rewind, Practice below (WCAG 2.4.3; no CSS `order`).
- **Play and Rewind.** Play and Pause are one button, `aria-keyshortcuts="Space"`. Space plays and pauses unless the focus is in a control that takes Space itself (a field, a button, a checkbox, a radio, a select or a summary; a focused link takes Enter, so Space plays there too); its page scroll is cancelled. Rewind is unavailable while the score is stopped at the start (a fresh score, after Rewind, at the end) and while the red waits on the first note for the reader's piano: `aria-disabled`, drawn like a disabled button, still focusable, and a click does nothing. A natively disabled button would drop the focus of the reader who pressed Rewind while paused.
- **Status line.** `role="status"`, always in the page as one live region whose text changes and visually hidden while it is empty (screen readers often do not announce a live region that is inserted with its text, which would lose `Loading the piano…` and the first `1`). It says: `Loading the score…`, `Fingering…` or `Preparing the score…` while a score opens; why Play is unavailable (`This piece has no notes for the left hand.`, `…for the right hand.`, `This piece has no notes to play.`); `Loading the piano…` while the first Play of a score fetches the samples (Play stays red, busy and unavailable) and, with a piano connected and a hand left out, while the samples load for the other hand (Play stays plain and the walk goes on); then the count-in beat by beat, `1`, `2`, `3`, `4` (24 px, bold and in ink; the other lines are gray, on the same line box); and, lowest of all, with a piano connected, `Click the page once to hear the other hand.` (`Tap the page once to hear the other hand.` on a touch screen) while the browser holds the sound back, then `Play the red note on your piano.` or, after a release that came too early, `Hold the red note a little longer.` (Playing along on a piano). The first time timing rings appear in the view, `A ring before a note means it came early; after it, late.` takes the line between those two until two more bars are judged (Playing along on a piano, Timing in the walk). The dock keeps its height when the line speaks from 480 px; below 480 px the stuck dock grows upward instead of moving the music.
- **Practice.** The tempo, hands, metronome, count-in and loop controls sit in the dock's `Practice` panel at every width, so nothing stands between the title and the music. `Practice` is a 44 px text button in ink (`.practice-toggle`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`) whose small CSS triangle turns 90° in 160 ms when it opens: a disclosure, never filled and never red. The panel is only `hidden` while closed, so a loop being typed lives on. It fades in over 160 ms with a 4 px rise, and the dock grows upward in one frame, so the music keeps its place. Escape inside the panel closes it and gives the focus back to the toggle. It stays open from piece to piece for the visit and every page load starts closed.
- **Practice mode.** The first row of the panel while a piano is connected (issue 191); there is no row without a piano. It comes first because it decides what Hands means and what the red does with the reader's playing, and the panel opens upward from Play, so a row added at the top when the piano connects moves no other control. It is a real switch: a native checkbox with `role="switch"` inside a `label.check.switch`, so a screen reader says `Practice mode, switch, on` and Space toggles it, and Space does not play (a switch takes Space like a checkbox). It is drawn (`input[role='switch']` in `base.css`): a 36 × 20 px track with a 1 px ink border and a 14 px round thumb that slides over `--dur-fast`, which is 0 under reduced motion. On: an ink track and a paper thumb at the right. Off: a paper track and a gray (`--gray-3`) thumb at the left. The row is 44 px high, the focus ring is the app's own, and it is never red, in either theme. When the piano goes while the switch has the focus, the focus moves to the Tempo slider. The other tick boxes keep the native look.
- **Tempo.** A slider from 50 to 150 % (kept per piece) shows the percentage and the tempo it makes in quarter notes per minute, from the tempo in force where the piece starts: `70 % · ♩ = 70` for Twinkle (written at 100) at 70 %, `100 % · ♩ = 108` for Ode to Joy. The slider's `aria-valuetext` says it in words. The value is one line of fixed width, so the slider never moves while it is dragged.
- **Hands.** `Both hands`, `Right hand`, `Left hand`: a segmented control of native radios with one sliding thumb. The choice is a saved setting, carried from piece to piece. A piece with notes in one hand only (a single-track MIDI melody, a MIDI file split to one side, a single-staff MusicXML of the reader's own) plays with both hands whatever is saved, and the panel leaves the control out (`handsFor`, `oneHanded` in `usePlayback.ts`, issue #112): the saved choice could pick the empty hand and leave Play red with nothing to play. The setting itself is kept, so the next piece with two hands opens with it; a MIDI file split again to two hands brings the control back. With a piano connected and Practice mode on the control also chooses the hand digitus plays: the hand left out is played by digitus between the reader's steps (Playing along on a piano, The other hand). With Practice mode off it only chooses the hand Play plays.
- **Loop.** A checkbox `Loop bars`. Ticking it shows the two fields `Loop from bar` and `Loop to bar`, filled with the cursor's bar and the next, both clamped to the piece (bar 1 when nothing is marked), and the loop applies at once. Unticking hides them and turns the loop off; the bars typed come back when it is ticked again in the same score view. A loop whose "from" is after its "to" as typed is not applied, and a line under the inputs, in ink, says `The loop needs "from" before "to".` Both inputs are then `aria-invalid` and described by it. It is not a live region: the status line stays the page's only one, and an alert on every keystroke of "12" after "6" would interrupt the typing.
- **Add notes between.** A checkbox after the loop, present only on a piece the guard offers and only once the embellished score is ready; it is absent, never disabled, on every other piece. It is unticked on every open and kept for the open piece only. Ticked, the sheet, the red cursor, the keyboard and playback use the embellished score: passing notes that fill a third, and an upper neighbour a whole step above a repeated note, at most one figure per bar and never in neighbouring bars, with every strong beat kept. The level, the title, the facts and `Export MusicXML` stay the plain score's, and `Print` prints the page as shown. Ticking or unticking carries the bar, the loop and the play state across. The decision and the guard: `docs/research/embellishment-spike.md`, section 6.
- **Moving through the piece.** One gray line closes the panel: `Click a bar to go there. ← and → move one bar.` (`Tap a bar to go there.` on a touch screen). There is no control for it (above).

### Export and print

`Export MusicXML` writes the fingering on screen (the engine's, or the editor's when that is shown) into the source MusicXML in the engine worker and saves it as UTF-8 text: `<slug>.fingered.musicxml` for a library piece, `<file name>.fingered.musicxml` for a file (a MIDI source exports the generated MusicXML). A UTF-16 source exports as UTF-8 and says so in its declaration.

`Print` calls the browser's print. The print stylesheet (`base.css`) hides the controls, the dock, the keyboard, the side panel, the notice and the status line, and prints one SVG per laid-out page, never breaking through one. Both hands print in full ink, the red prints as ink, and a print is ink on paper in either theme.

### About

`#/about` explains what digitus2 is, how it chooses fingerings (an exact search over ergonomic rules; the hand size changes the spans it allows; the engine version is shown), who made it, and every licence and attribution: digitus2 (MIT), Verovio (LGPL-3.0-or-later, unmodified), PDF.js (Apache-2.0, for scan previews), midi-file (MIT), and smplr (MIT) playing the Splendid Grand Piano samples (public domain). It lists the curated built-in pieces in a table (piece, composer, engraving credit, licence); the CC BY-SA and CC BY engravings are counted in a sentence and credited there and on their score pages, and the MuseScore-archive pieces (the PDMX dataset, each public domain or CC0 as its uploader declared it) are counted in one sentence rather than listed. The page ends with the build it runs, `Build <short commit> · <day>` (the commit reads `dev` where git is unavailable), so a phone that keeps the installed app open can say which build it has. `vite.config.ts` defines `__DIGITUS2_BUILD__` (the first 7 characters of `GITHUB_SHA`, else `git rev-parse --short=7 HEAD`, else `dev`, and the time of the build); `src/app/build.ts` reads it and writes the day in UTC in the reader's language, so it names the same day in every time zone (`Version` replaces `Build` in French, `Versie` in Dutch, `Versión` in Spanish and `Versão` in Portuguese). A scan's **Recognition details** carries the same line. The button `Back to the library` is the red element.

### Scanning

Experimental PNG, JPEG and PDF scanning goes through the same own-score chooser or drop target and is deliberately separate from MusicXML and MIDI import. Opening the scan page sends one health request to the scan service (`scan/service.ts` holds its address), which wakes it while the reader arranges pages; the pages go only on **Scan this score**, one request per page, and the service (homr for photos, Audiveris for PDFs) answers with the MusicXML, which is shown beside the original, and **Use for fingerings** opens the existing score view. The raw MusicXML can be downloaded even when strict parsing refuses it, and **Download everything** saves the whole scan (the pages as the scanner received them, every recognition, the corrections, the build and the browser) as one ZIP, through the share sheet where the browser has one (`scan/bundle.ts`). Recognition errors remain (missed ties, wrong rhythms, a lost grand staff on a badly lit photo, beam membership inferred from the meter), so scanning is a correction-and-review workflow. Source and recognition stay reachable from the resulting score (`Scan source and recognition`) and are saved with uploads in the browser-local `My scores` list. PDF.js loads only for PDF previews. The scan page shows one primary button on every device, the advice line and one sentence saying where the page goes; the client (`scan/client.ts`) words busy, rate-limited, unreachable, failed and timed-out answers as short sentences and keeps the pages. Where the page goes and limitations: [docs/scanning.md](scanning.md); the service: [docs/scan-service.md](scan-service.md).

- **Correcting** (`scan/corrections.ts`, `notes.ts`, `ScanEditor.tsx`): in the review, and only there, a click or tap on a note selects it (the nearest note within 22 px of a tap) and opens a panel docked at the bottom of the screen; notes in the normal score view keep seeking. Corrections (pitch, length, tie, hand, removal) are a list replayed over the unchanged raw recognition; each is checked before it is recorded, and the corrected copy is rendered and imported again at once. The render copy carries `id="s<ordinal>"` on every note and rest, where the ordinal is the note's position among the raw file's `<note>` elements, so selection works even when the strict import refuses the file. The selected note carries the screen's one red and `Use for fingerings` is plain meanwhile (no colour transition, so two reds never show). Places to check (tie gaps, unmatched ties, hands that disagree) come from rules checked on the file. One engine worker and one renderer serve the whole review, so a correction does not restart either.
- **On a touch-only device** the scan page is the same, with `Scan this score`; the library and the scan page add `Take a photo` and `Take another photo` (camera capture, photos named by the time taken, `scan/phone.ts`), the page tools are symbol buttons and the arrange hint says to select and move rather than drag (`docs/scanning.md`, On a phone or tablet).

## Design system

- `src/design/tokens.css` holds every colour (black, white, grays and the one red, used only by `.is-red`) and the durations; `base.css` holds layout, the focus ring and the print stylesheet. Breakpoints: 400 px (pills), 480 px (dock) and 900 px (side panel).
- Durations: `--dur-press` 50 ms, `--dur-fast` 120 ms, `--dur` 160 ms, `--dur-slow` 200 ms, `--dur-reveal` 400 ms, `--dur-flourish` 520 ms; easing `--ease: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`. All durations are 0 under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The UI stays pristine and uncluttered. A control appears only in the context that needs it: filters and search show only when a list is long enough, the practice controls fold into one toggle, and what only a developer can act on waits behind `Details`.
- Light and dark, the one red and the motion table are below.

## The red element rule

The design brief allows black, white, grays and exactly one red (`#e3000f`), and exactly one red element on screen at a time. The definition every test uses:

- A **red root** is a visible element (not `display: none`, `visibility: hidden` or `opacity: 0`, and not inside such an element) whose own computed `color`, `background-color`, `border-*-color`, `outline-color`, `fill` or `stroke` equals `rgb(227, 0, 15)`, and whose ancestors are not red roots. Descendants of a red root are not inspected (a white label on a red button is one red root).
- A state passes when there is exactly one red root, **or** when every red root is an SVG `g.note` or `g.chord` inside `.score-sheet` and all of them carry the same `data-onset-tick` value (the playback cursor lights every note that starts at that instant, in both hands; that is one cursor).

Which element is red, by state:

| State                                                                                                                                                                 | The red element                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Library: first visit, a level chosen, an open shelf, search results, the empty result                                                                                 | `Start with …`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Library, returning                                                                                                                                                    | `Continue with {title}`                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| A file dragged over the window                                                                                                                                        | the drop frame (`div.drop-frame.is-red`, a 2 px dashed frame 16 px in from the window's edges, its text in ink and gray); the page beneath, the start button included, is `visibility: hidden`, and the frame leaves in one frame on leave or drop |
| Score, opening (loading, fingering, laying out), and a scan recognising its pages                                                                                     | the loader's keys (`g.loader-red.is-red`); Play, or `Cancel scan`, is plain until it goes                                                                                                                                                          |
| Score, stopped, or paused with nothing to resume (before any note was heard, rewound, at the end, or with a loop set since that leaves the pause position outside it) | Play (also while the first Play loads the piano)                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

Never red: the `Practice` toggle (a disclosure), the `Practice mode` switch, `Add notes between`, chosen level and length pills (ink-filled), the notice, the end line, the line for playing along on a piano and its button, the search field and `Clear`, the theme switch, hover and focus states, pressed keys, the reader's held keys and their levels, the hand letters, the finger digits and the timing rings.

- The timing rings of the walk (issue 192) are gray (`--gray-3`) and sit in the `g.staff`, outside every `g.note` and `g.chord`, so the `.is-red` rules (`svg .is-red *`) never reach them; the red stays the only red.

- Between Play and the cursor the red travels instead of jumping (motion study 05, `score/RedFlight.tsx`). Play gives it up and, in the same frame, a shape (`div.red-ghost-shape.is-red`, a plain red root outside the sheet) carries it for up to 200 ms, to the first notehead at the end of a lead-in that Play began, or back to Play after Rewind while stopped and at the end. A seek makes it hop the same way from the cursor or Play to the bar sought, while the cue it lands on stays in ink. On every frame one of the three is red. Nothing travels under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The easter egg's notes are ink, with one deliberate exception, the user's: a double-click on nothing in particular sends one note of its bigger burst up in the accent for about half a second (`app/NoteBurst.tsx`, `note-burst-note is-red`), next to the screen's red element. The finish at the end of a piece (`score/EndBurst.tsx`) is ink only: the red flying home is the one red. With a piano connected, the red goes on from Play to the first note once it has landed.
- The list scrolled past the start button shows no red in view while the DOM still holds exactly one. That is the behaviour, and the red is never made sticky.

Three enforcement points:

1. `scripts/check-colors.mjs` (the `design` step of `pnpm verify`): no colour literal in `apps/web/src` or `index.html` outside `tokens.css`, no second chromatic colour in `tokens.css`, and the red referenced only by `.is-red`.
2. `apps/web/e2e/helpers/red.ts`: `expectOneRed(page)` applies the definition above in the browser. `e2e/states.spec.ts` runs it in every application state on desktop and at 360 px, in light and in dark, and the flow specs run it at their own checkpoints.
3. Testing Library assertions in the component tests: the library and about views render exactly one `.is-red` element (the right button), the transport's Play button carries it while stopped and not while playing, and `Button`, `Keyboard` and the renderer's `dom` helpers have their own checks.

## Light and dark

The page follows the system's colour scheme (`prefers-color-scheme`) until the reader chooses a theme with the switch; the choice is kept per browser. The switch is a sun that becomes a crescent, because a theme switch must read as light and dark at a glance (music-themed designs were tried and rejected).

- **Tokens** (`design/tokens.css`). The dark set redefines paper (`#111111`), ink (`#f2f2f2`) and the three grays for the same contrasts (secondary text 5.6:1), and leaves the one red unchanged. It is written twice, once under `@media screen and (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` for `:root:not([data-theme='light'])` and once for `:root[data-theme='dark']`. `design/tokens.test.ts` keeps the two identical and checks every text and key pair at 4.5:1 (3:1 for the red and the black keys) in both themes. Screens only: a print is ink on paper in either theme. Two tokens are the same in both themes on purpose: `--on-accent` (the white label on a red button, 4.9:1; paper on red would be 4.3:1 in the dark) and `--invert` (the Hands thumb's difference blend needs white). The `--key-*` set keeps a real keyboard's colours rather than inverting: light keys with dark digits and hand letters on them in both themes (in the dark, white keys `#dadada`, pressed keys `#8f8f8f`, black keys the paper). `color-scheme` follows the theme, so scrollbars and native controls match.
- **The reader's held keys** (issue 182) have four tokens of their own, pure grays and never the accent, three for the bar along the front edge of a key held on their piano and one for the number on its level. `--key-held` (`#666666` in light, `#3d3d3d` in dark) is drawn on a white key, on a pressed key and on a dimmed black key; `--key-held-on-black` (`#666666` in both) is drawn on a black key that is neither pressed nor dimmed; `--key-held-on-dim` (`#666666` in light, `#a6a6a6` in dark) is drawn on a dimmed white key. A dimmed key is the pressed gray at 40 % opacity over what lies behind it, the dock's paper for a white key and the white key's fill for a black one, and its bar is drawn outside that dimming, so the pairs for it are measured against the key as dimmed. The fourth, `--key-on-level` (`#ffffff` in light, `#dadada` in dark), is the finger number and the hand letter drawn again over a key's level (Cursor and keyboard). All are written identically in the two dark blocks, and `design/tokens.test.ts` holds each bar pair to 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11, non-text contrast) and the text on the level to 4.5:1, in both themes. A level takes its bar's gray on the same key, so the bar's pairs hold for it too, `--key-held-on-black` on a black key's level (3.7 and 3.3) included:

  | Pair                                           | Light | Dark |
  | ---------------------------------------------- | ----: | ---: |
  | `--key-held` against `--key-white`             |   5.7 |  7.8 |
  | `--key-held` against `--key-down`              |   3.6 |  3.4 |
  | `--key-held-on-black` against `--key-black`    |   3.7 |  3.3 |
  | `--key-held-on-dim` against a dimmed white key |   4.8 |  4.1 |
  | `--key-held` against a dimmed black key        |   4.8 |  5.7 |
  | `--key-on-level` against `--key-held`          |   5.7 |  7.8 |

  A black key that is neither pressed nor dimmed shows no number, so its level needs no text colour; a dimmed key never draws a level.

- **The timing rings** (issue 192) are stroked with `--gray-3`, with no token of their own: `#767676` on the light paper (4.5:1) and `#8c8c8c` on the dark (5.6:1), above the 3:1 a graphic needs. It is darker than the dimmed hand (40 % ink) in light and lighter than it in dark, so a ring never reads as a dimmed note.
- **The score** has its own ink, `--score-ink`: the ink in light, `#d4d4d4` in the dark (13:1), because a page of notes in near-white glared and `#c4c4c4` looked too gray; the white keys are softened with it. Verovio's inner `svg.definition-scale` carries `color="black"` and leaves `fill` at its initial black; `base.css` sets both from `--score-ink`. The red cursor note stands out more against the softer engraving.
- **The switch** (`design/ThemeToggle.tsx`): a round 44 px button with no border at the top right of every view, on the row of the view's first line, after Back in the tab order (first in the header where the heading is the first row, which keeps 3.25 rem clear of it). Named "Dark theme" with `aria-pressed`; a 24-unit icon, the crescent's bite a mask so the hover fill shows nothing of it; never red, not printed.
- **The choice** (`design/theme.ts`): `chooseTheme` sets `data-theme` on the root and keeps it under `digitus2.theme`; choosing the system's own theme removes both, so the page follows the system again. Blocked site data still switches the page for the visit. A choice made in another tab is taken up (`storage` event). The inline script in `index.html` applies a kept choice before the first paint; `design/theme.test.ts` runs that script against fakes so the key and values cannot drift.
- **Tests**: `e2e/theme.spec.ts` (follows the system; the switch overrides, is kept across a reload and views, and gives the page back to the system; a kept theme is on the page with no application JavaScript at all; the engraving and keys in the dark; print in the dark; the ripple; reduced motion; the switch's place at 360 px). `e2e/states.spec.ts` runs every state's one-red, axe and overflow check again with the system in dark, on desktop and at 360 px.

## Languages

The app speaks English, Dutch, French, Spanish and Portuguese (Brazilian). The page speaks the first of the browser's languages (`navigator.languages`) it knows, by primary subtag, and falls back to English. It never guesses from the reader's location: Belgium and Switzerland speak several languages.

- **Choosing** (`i18n/LanguageLine.tsx`): one quiet line at the foot of the library, `English · Nederlands · Français · Español · Português`. Each name is in its own language (with `lang`), so a reader who cannot read the rest finds theirs, and the current one carries `aria-current`. Detection is right for almost everyone, so the line appears nowhere else: not in the headers, never on the score. No flags: they stand for countries, not languages.
- **The pick** (`i18n/i18n.ts`) works like the theme: kept under `digitus2.lang`, forgotten when it matches the browser's language, taken up from another tab (`storage` event), and the browser's language followed again after a `languagechange` while nothing is kept. The inline script in `index.html` sets `<html lang>` before the first paint, and `i18n/i18n.test.ts` runs that script against fakes so it agrees with `matchLocale`. `main.tsx` waits for the language's texts alongside the library list, so a reader in another language never sees English first.
- **Dictionaries** (`i18n/<locale>/<view>.ts`): one plain object per view (`common`, `app`, `about`, `library`, `scan`, `score`, `transport`). English is the source and the `Messages` type; every other language is typed against it, so a missing or extra key fails the typecheck, and `i18n/i18n.test.ts` checks the same shape at run time. A text with values is a function of them; counts use `Intl.PluralRules` and numbers `Intl.NumberFormat` (`i18n/format.ts`). No i18n library. English is bundled; each other language is its own chunk, loaded with `import()` only when needed.
- **In code**: components read `const t = useT()`. Text decided outside a component (a notice in the store, a module constant) is a `Phrase`, a function of the texts written when it is shown, so it follows a later switch. A user-facing error is a `PhraseError`: its `message` stays English for logs and tests, and `messageOf` (`app/errors.ts`) writes it in the language on screen.
- **Music**: hand letters follow the language (L/R in English and Dutch, G/D in French, I/D in Spanish, E/D in Portuguese). Finger numbers are universal. Piece titles, composers and collections keep their original names.
- **Tests**: unit and e2e tests run in English (happy-dom and Playwright report `en-US`), so text assertions stay English. `e2e/i18n.spec.ts` opens the library, a score and the about page in an nl, fr, es and pt browser. It checks `<html lang>`, the marked language and that no English sentence of the dictionary is on screen, and runs axe. It also switches with the line and keeps the pick across a reload.
- **Translations** were written for this release without a native-speaker review. Each language still needs one.

The `.mxl` and UTF-16 files are built inside the tests from a library score (`e2e/helpers/files.ts`, with the engine's own zip library, fflate).

**The fake Web MIDI** (`e2e/helpers/midi.ts`, issue 182). No real piano and no real browser prompt take part in any spec. `installFakeMidi(page, options)` injects, before the page loads, a `navigator.requestMIDIAccess` of the test's own and a `permissions.query({ name: 'midi' })` that follows it. The options are the permission it starts with (`prompt`, `granted` or `denied`), how the prompt is answered (`allow`, `block`, `dismiss`, or `manual`, held until the test answers), the inputs connected at access (default `GO:PIANO MIDI 1`), the platform (`Windows` or `Linux`), no Web MIDI at all (`unsupported`, as in Safari), the `playAlong` setting and any further stored settings before the first load (`settings`: a spec seeds `hands` or a piece's tempo here and never clicks the Practice panel, because a click is a user activation), the Practice tempo of pieces by slug (`tempo`, issue 185), and `hang`, which leaves `requestMIDIAccess` pending whatever the permission says until `resolve` answers it (Chrome's WinRT backend with the piano off). `fakeMidi(page)` counts the requests and their options, plugs and unplugs inputs (an unplugged port stays in the list, disconnected, and comes back as the same port, as in Chrome; `remove` drops it from the list instead), sends raw bytes or notes from an input, and reads the step the red waits on through `__digitus2Along`; its `playRed` presses every pitch of that step, holds them until the red has moved on (a step passes once its keys have been held for its time, issue 185) and lets go, `playRedTimed` also gives the milliseconds that took, `tap` presses and releases pitches within a time given, and its `pressStep` presses them and holds them (the step runs, and the other hand starts, issue 184). Its ports open when `onmidimessage` is assigned, as in Chrome, and `send(bytes, input, atMs)` sets the event's `timeStamp` to `atMs`, as a piano's message carries it (issue 192). `walkRhythm(page, plan)` walks steps on the fake piano in a set rhythm, entirely in the page, never by round trips: it presses each step at a set instant (a signed offset per step, a pause per step carried to the steps after it), stamps its note-ons with that instant as their event time, and holds it past its hold (a step before an early one is let go after the next press, legato); it reports what it sent, with the rings and the status line as they were just before each press. `walkTiming(page)` reads the timing hook. The helper's separate `strikes(page)` reads what the silent instrument was asked to sound, which stays empty while the reader plays; `accompanied(page)`, `accompCancelled(page)` and `audioState(page)` read the other hand's record. `quietly(page)` makes `page.evaluate` run without a user gesture: Playwright evaluates, and traces, with one, which activates the document and so defeats a flow about the autoplay policy. Under it the helpers, and `expectOneRed` and the others that go through `page.evaluate`, read the page through the protocol; only locators, `waitForFunction` and screenshots stay loud.

## Known limitations

- Glissando and slide lines are not played; they are rare in the library (78 glissandos, 68 slides).
- Jump marks act at bar lines: a Fine or To Coda inside a bar takes effect at its end, and a D.C. written in a first ending is not taken (La Styrienne). Some archive scores contradict themselves and leave a bar out of the performance: a volta with no repeat sign, a bracket that comes after its repeat sign, an ending 2 on a repeat bar with no ending 1; the Water Music minuet leaves its bars 62 to 76 unheard because of two D.C. marks, and the D.S. of Tico-tico lands on a Fine and a coda inside a first ending (`packages/engine/src/score/form.library.test.ts` lists them). The `Add notes between` switch carries the written bar, so a switch in a repeat's second pass resumes in its first. A pickup bar that a repeat returns to is counted in and accented as a whole bar on its second hearing.
- MIDI files are imported quantised per beat to 16th notes, 16th-note triplets or 32nd notes, with one voice per hand, so held notes are shortened (#34). The hands are separated automatically by a learned pitch-trajectory DP, which put 92.05 percent of notes in the correct hand on the PIG test split and 85.10 percent on the Mutopia test half (macro means; scorecard `m6-hands` in `benchmarks/history.md`); the `Hand split` control overrides it. Hands crossed at one instant always go by pitch. Performance recordings without a tempo map are placed on a 120 quarter-notes-per-minute grid.
- Not built: editing or pinning fingers, explanations and confidence marks, and the readability reduction, so every note's digit is printed (milestone M5, #42; its plan, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-14-m5-interaction-readability.md`, predates the UI batch and needs a re-plan; its click-a-bar task is done, T13).
- Settings persist across visits; of what is per piece only the tempo factor does (for the 50 pieces changed last). There are no saved edits, loop or position. The library's scroll position lasts only for the visit.
- Two open tabs of the app do not share the settings while open: each keeps the ones it loaded, and the next change in a tab writes all of them, so a favourite marked in one tab is lost when an older tab then changes a setting.
- The tempo control counts quarter notes per minute, as the transport plays (MusicXML's `<sound tempo>` is in quarter notes). A piece that prints its tempo in another unit shows the same speed in quarters: Greensleeves prints `♩. = 64` and the control says `100 % · ♩ = 96`. A piece without a tempo mark plays, and shows, the engine's default, `♩ = 120`. With tempo changes inside the piece, the control shows the tempo where the piece starts.
- A dropped file cannot be restored from the address after a reload (`#/piece/file` falls back to the library); reopen it from `My scores`.
- Offline, only the pieces opened before open, and the piano plays only the samples fetched before: another piece may need other pitches or velocity layers, and then Play says the piano sounds could not be loaded. The worker does not keep the whole library or every sample.
- A new build's service worker takes over only once every window of the app has closed, unless the reader presses **Reload** on the notice that offers it. Online, a reload already shows the new build; offline, the app opens the build of the worker still in control. Reload activates the new worker for every open window, so a second window of the app keeps the build it loaded and can fail to load a lazy chunk of that build until it is reloaded. A page that never navigates learns of a new build only when the browser checks `sw.js` (the app asks for no periodic check).
- The installed app's splash screen and status bar are white in dark mode: the manifest's colours are fixed, and `index.html` has no `theme-color` meta by choice, because one per `prefers-color-scheme` would ignore the in-app theme switch.
- iOS shows no install prompt: a reader installs from Share, Add to Home Screen.
- Not verified on devices: installing and playing on Android Chrome and iOS Safari (standalone mode, AAC decoding, storage quota), and the content types GitHub Pages sends for `.webmanifest`, `.ogg` and `.m4a` (the piano storage rejects a sample whose type is not `audio/*`). The e2e suite covers what Chromium can check.
- A cursor edge: switching hands while playing, to a hand that has not been heard since playback last started (Play, a seek or a loop wrap), marks a note of that hand that was not heard since. It is its last onset before that start or, when it has none, its first onset (the floor in `useCursor.ts`), marked before it sounds, until the hand's next note is heard. Play, a seek, Rewind and a loop wrap all wait for the hands played, but a hand switch starts no lead-in.
- With one hand muted, its dimmed keys follow its own onsets and stay down until its next onset or the played hand's, like the pressed keys: a short note (staccato) shows held until the next onset of either hand. On a phone the keyboard scrolls to follow the pressed keys only, so a dimmed key can sit outside its visible part. In the score, that hand's slurs, ties and dynamics stay in ink (they belong to the bar, not to a note), and a note of it that shares a chord with the hand played is dimmed on its own while the chord's stem stays in ink. Its shapes are dimmed one by one, so where two of them overlap (a stem's end inside its beam, a stem against its notehead) the overlap is a little darker; it shows when zoomed in. Dimming a group whole would cost a compositor layer per group (issue #79). During its fade a digit is translucent text, with the subpixel colour fringes every text has on Windows, and it settles to gray at the end; a jump far through a long piece (End, or a seek that scrolls a long way) builds the pages there and shows a blank window for a frame or two while they are drawn (`docs/reviews/ui-2026-10-20.md`, H2 and H4).
- On a piece of six pages or more the red's flights (`RedFlight.tsx`, `EndBurst.tsx`) start from or land on noteheads that must be in the DOM, and only the pages near the screen are. A reader who has scrolled more than two window heights from the page of the first note and presses Play gets no flight out from Play (the red appears on the first note when the lead-in ends); one who has scrolled that far from where the music stopped gets no flight home on Rewind and no burst at the end. A hop after a seek builds the page it lands on first, so it is not affected. A hand switched in the same task as a far scroll leaves the pages built a frame later dimmed at rest, and switching back fades their digits in from the gray they were at (`e2e/hand-switch.spec.ts`).
- The bold cursor is drawn for the notes as engraved at Verovio's scale, so its size follows the note. At 360 px the box of a stemmed note's red was 5.0 to 8.0 times an engraved notehead's box in the playback test (K.545, bars 30 and 31), and its red pixels 1.8 to 3.1 noteheads' boxes (the test takes the cursor's own enlargement out of the notehead it measures against). A beamed note with a short stem is the smallest mark, and a note with no stem (a whole note) only grows its head.
- Memory: the score layout runs in Verovio, a C++ engraver compiled to WebAssembly, in its own worker. Its WASM heap is 128 MB from the moment the library list warms it up, and Chrome on Windows counts all of it. It stays warm between pieces, so a piece opens without its 0.3 to 0.7 s cold start, except after a long one: once it has laid out a piece of 2,000 notes or more, or its heap has grown past the 128 MB, it is retired, and that piece pays the cold start on its next layout (a new width bucket, the editor's fingering; 535 → 893 ms on Beethoven's Op. 79); the library warms a fresh one up while idle. The engine worker is released 2 s after work on a piece of 2,000 notes or more. A page in the DOM costs about 1.5 KB per SVG element, so a piece of six pages or more builds only the pages near the screen (`score/render/pageWindow.ts`, `pageIndex.ts`) and the rest stand empty. Measurements and the options not taken: `docs/research/ram-usage.md`.
- Library content: many MuseScore-archive pieces sit under Classical by collection although their composers (Chopin, Debussy, Scriabin) belong to Romantic or Modern, many have the composer `Unknown` and raw titles, so the shelves are less truthful than they could be (shelves spec, section 10; #48).
- Playing along on a piano (issue 182) needs Web MIDI. Safari and every browser on an iPhone or iPad have none, so the line is absent there. USB MIDI works as it is. On Windows a Bluetooth piano is not listed by Chrome's default Web MIDI backend (measured on Windows 11 with Chrome 156: no Bluetooth MIDI port is listed); with `chrome://flags/#use-winrt-midi-api` set to Enabled, or Chrome started with `--enable-features=MidiManagerWinrt`, the paired Roland GO:PIANO is listed and its notes arrive. The line gives the flag in its second sentence in Chrome and Edge on Windows, and shows no such sentence in Firefox or on another system.
- The other hand (issue 184) sounds from the reader's press, so what the reader hears lags the press by the MIDI transport (USB about 1 ms, Bluetooth MIDI 10 to 30 ms), the main thread's handling and the audio device's output latency (40 ms on the spike machine, 150 to 250 ms over Bluetooth audio). The notes that start with the reader's step sound that much after it, and the later notes keep their spacing from the press.
- The other hand's notes before the first step the walk waits on are not played: a pickup, an introduction in the other hand, a bar entered by a seek. The reader's first note starts it, and Play plays the introduction. Whether a loop's wrap should go on through the loop's opening, up to its first step, is open on the issue (issue 184).
- A grace note of the other hand at the start of a stretch sounds with its principal, at the press, not before it. A tempo change applies from the next press, not to the stretch that sounds.
- A fresh load straight onto a score address needs one click before digitus is heard: the browser holds the sound back until the page has had a click or a key press, and a MIDI message is not one. The status line asks for it. After a click on a piece in the library there is nothing to click. The audible result is the owner's check in real Chrome (issue 184).
- A step's pitches are exact, so a piece with a note outside a 61-key piano's range cannot be passed on that piano at that step: the reader clicks the next bar or uses the arrow key. Held keys outside the on-screen keyboard's drawn range are not shown. The walk is tested against a fake Web MIDI; a whole piece played on a real piano over Bluetooth is the owner's check on a device (issue 182).
- Timing in the walk (issue 192) judges when keys go down, against the reader's own pulse: rests, note lengths and releases are not judged, nor are dotted and triplet subdivisions inside a beat, gaps the red does not wait out, or a hesitation. Its windows are provisional until they are set from the owner's piano ([issue 192](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/192)).
- Photo and PDF scanning is experimental: see Scanning and `docs/scanning.md`.

## What the UI review should look at

Verification spec 3.10 asks for a periodic qualitative review by an independent subagent, which writes `docs/reviews/ui-YYYY-MM-DD.md` and never edits product code. For this app it should, as a beginner would: open the piece the red button names (`Continue` on a returning visit) and one Harder piece; read the fingering (are the digits legible, next to the right notes, not colliding with other marks); play with the cursor and the keyboard (does the cursor match what is heard, are the pressed keys and their finger numbers readable on a phone); change the hand size and switch the editor's fingering on and off; loop two bars; drop a MusicXML file and a MIDI file; export; print; and read the about page. On every screen: is the next step obvious, is exactly one thing red, is anything cluttered or ambiguous, and what would a piano teacher object to.

It should also check what the automated checks cannot judge, and that earlier fixes stay fixed:

- Pause within reach in the dock wherever the cursor has scrolled the score; a phone opening a score with music on its first screen; the status line saying what Play is doing (the piano loading, the count); the cursor easy to follow; the finger numbers explained; no developer text outside `Details`.
- The level pills at 360 and 1280 px (all four on one row, none cut off; a chosen pill is ink, never red); whether the start row, the pills, the shelves and the rows read as clear units at every level; the search, its empty result and the end line.
- The paused cursor (the last group heard red, its keys down, Play plain) and the `Practice` toggle in the dock.
- The hand letters: they show only in bars where the hands come within a fifth, and on a phone, where the keyboard follows the keys struck at the cursor, a hand scrolled out of view leaves a lone number with no letter (UI batch design, section 15, rule 5). Look at the G major minuet (BWV Anh. 114) and Amazing Grace at 360 px, in bars the rule leaves plain, and say whether a reader is lost there. If so, the fallback is ready in the design: a hand out of view during a bar keeps the letters to the end of that bar, keyed by the cursor group's measure index, with the same one-bar bridge.
- Playing along (issue 182): the piano line under `Open your own score` reads as one quiet row, and a reader who presses it knows what happened; with a piano connected the red waits on the first note with Play plain, the next note is easy to find after each step, and the gray bars of the held keys are visible on a white key, a black key and a cued key; the level that fills a held key reads as the note's length, the number stays legible on it, and `Hold the red note a little longer.` reads as advice (issue 185). With one hand left out, the other hand comes in rhythm after each press and stops when the reader seeks, and the sentence that asks for a click is clear (issue 184).
