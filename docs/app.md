# digitus2 web app

The app in `apps/web` (`@digitus2/web`), built in milestone M4 from the plan `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-13-m4-app-mvp.md`. Product spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-digitus2-design.md` (sections 4, 5 and 9). Verification spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-verification-design.md` (goals "Be fast" and "Honor the design brief", checks 3.9 and 3.10). The technology choices and their measurements come from the rendering spike, `docs/research/rendering-spike.md`.

## What the app does

Book-photo recovery now reaches source/result review, but known recognition errors remain. Photo and PDF scanning is an experimental correction-and-review workflow, not certified transcription.

Experimental PNG/JPEG/PDF scanning is available through the same own-score chooser or drop target. It is deliberately separate from normal MusicXML/MIDI import: no PC request occurs until **Connect to this PC**, original PDF bytes go to the optional loopback worker, and raw recognized notation is displayed beside the original before an explicit comparison and **Use for fingerings**. The raw MusicXML can be downloaded even if strict scan parsing refuses it. Recognition is imperfect; no automatic repair or accuracy guarantee is implied. Source and recognition provenance remain accessible from the resulting score and are saved with uploads in the browser-local IndexedDB collection. The contextual My scores list reopens drafts, recognized scans or direct MusicXML/MIDI imports; storage failures preserve current in-memory work. [Setup, privacy and limitations](scanning.md). PDF.js is loaded only for PDF source previews, with a bundled local worker and Apache-2.0 licence; it does not rasterize the recognition upload.

A beginner opens one of the built-in pieces (253 after the 2026-10-15 bulk expansion) from the library page, or a MusicXML or MIDI file of their own, and sees the score with a finger number on every note, chosen by the digitus2 engine for the selected hand size.

The library page (UI batch, design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-14-ui-batch-design.md`, sections 3 to 7) reads as a few units, in this order: the name `digitus2` and one sentence (`Finger numbers for piano. Pick a piece, press play, and follow the red note.`); the start row, the one red button (`Start with Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star`, or `Continue with {title}` once a library piece has been played) followed by the search field; the level pills (First steps, Easy, Moderate, Intermediate, Challenging, and Advanced; a pill only for a level that holds a piece) with one gray line saying what the chosen level asks of the hands; the list of the chosen level, its rows under collection headings with "Other pieces" last; and the end line `Open your own score` and the footer `About and licences`. A row is the title with its length on the right, and a composer line only when the composer says something the heading does not (not "Traditional…" or "Anonymous", not a composer the heading names for every row of its group, always when two rows in view share a title); under its own heading a title drops the part of a trailing parenthesis the heading already says (`Melody (No. 1)` under Schumann's Album for the Young). What shows depends on the size of the library, through the thresholds in `src/library/disclosure.ts` (design 6.1), each crossed only by a new build of the library, never while the reader looks:

The level shown on arrival is the one chosen earlier in the visit, else the level of the piece played last (if it is still in the library), else the lowest. The search filters from the first character across every level: each word typed must start a word of the title, the composer or the collection, ignoring case, accents and punctuation (`fur elise` finds Für Elise, `minuet` finds 14 pieces); while the field holds text the pills and the level line leave and the results stand under their level names, and Escape, `Clear` or emptying the field brings the same level back. A search that matches nothing says `No piece matches “{query}”.` and, in gray, `Search looks in titles, composers and collections.` A file dragged anywhere over the window shows the red drop frame (`Drop to open this file`, `MusicXML or MIDI`) over the hidden page, and opens when dropped; a drag that carries no file (a text selection, a link) shows nothing. The end line opens the file chooser, and the wrong kind of file, chosen or dropped, ends in the gray notice. A screen reader hears `{level}: {n} pieces`, the search's count or its empty result from a polite live region, 500 ms after the last change.

In the score view, a one-line legend says what the numbers mean (1 is the thumb, 5 the little finger; upper staff right hand, lower staff left), shown once on a screen: before the reader's first Play anywhere, under the score's header below 900 px and at the top of the side panel, beside the score, from 900 px; after it, in the side panel at every width. Play sounds the piece on a sampled grand piano while a red cursor marks the notes being heard and an on-screen keyboard shows which finger plays which key, with an L or R for the hand only in bars where the hands come within a fifth; Play or Pause, Rewind and the keyboard stay in a dock at the bottom of the window while the cursor scrolls the score, and the dock's status line says when the piano is loading and counts the count-in. Paused after a note was heard, the last group heard stays red with its keys pressed, and Play resumes there; a loop set while paused that leaves that place outside it makes Play start at the loop's first bar, so the marks clear and Play is red again. Rewind is unavailable while the score is stopped at the start. Tempo (shown as a percentage and the tempo it makes, kept per piece), one hand or both (the other hand dimmed), metronome, count-in and a bar loop help practice; at every width they fold into one `Practice` toggle in the dock, so nothing stands between the title and the music. Pieces with an editor's fingering can show it instead of the engine's. The fingered score can be exported as MusicXML or printed. An about page lists every licence and attribution. Everything runs in the browser: there is no server, and the only network traffic is the app's static files and the piano samples.

### Ornament playback

MusicXML trills and upper/lower mordents sound automatically, with the keyboard following their realized pitches. Short mordents use three attacks; `long="yes"` uses five, then holds the principal. Trills alternate with the upper diatonic neighbor through the first written note segment, at about eight attacks per score second, and default to ending on the principal. Supported explicit `start-note`, integer `beats` (2–64), `trill-step` and ornament `accidental-mark` instructions override those defaults. Neighbor pitches otherwise follow the active key and earlier same-measure, staff and octave accidentals, resolved in musical time across voices.

The original MusicXML is unchanged. Fingering export preserves its signs, written notes and principal fingerings; performance attacks are a separate view. The red score cursor stays on the written note. Auxiliary keyboard pitches have no invented finger number, and their samples and keyboard range are loaded automatically. No ornament control is added.

These are practice defaults, with no promise of historically authentic Bach execution or instruction for alternating fingers. Unsupported compound, accelerating or microtonal ornaments retain their notation and ordinary held-note playback. Extension lines do not extend realization beyond the first written segment; ties then hold the principal. Live tempo changes normally wait for a safe attack boundary, bounded to 250 ms including the existing lookahead. If interleaved ornaments have no safe boundary in that window, playback resynchronizes with the existing 50 ms seek delay and rearticulates sounding notes. Ordinary principal-hold timing retains the transport's existing limitations. See [scope, review fixes and verification evidence](reviews/ornaments-2026-10-16.md). Codex — Sol 6.1 implementation and independent review; Astra coordination.

## The red element rule

The design brief allows black, white, grays and exactly one red (`#e3000f`), and exactly one red element on screen at a time. The definition every test uses:

- A **red root** is a visible element (not `display: none`, `visibility: hidden` or `opacity: 0`, and not inside such an element) whose own computed `color`, `background-color`, `border-*-color`, `outline-color`, `fill` or `stroke` equals `rgb(227, 0, 15)`, and whose ancestors are not red roots. Descendants of a red root are not inspected (a white label on a red button is one red root).
- A state passes when there is exactly one red root, **or** when every red root is an SVG `g.note` or `g.chord` inside `.score-sheet` and all of them carry the same `data-onset-tick` value (the playback cursor lights every note that starts at that instant, in both hands; that is one cursor).
- Which element is red, by state: library: the "Start with …" button. Score, stopped or paused: the Play button (also while the first Play loads the piano). Score, counting in: the Pause button, until the first note sounds. Score, playing: the cursor notes (drawn bold, notehead, stem and flag). About: the "Back to the library" button. Error states: the "Back to the library" button. UI batch (design section 9): library, first visit, a level chosen, search results and the empty result: the "Start with …" button; returning: "Continue with {title}". While a file is dragged over the window: the drop frame (`div.drop-frame.is-red`, a 2 px dashed frame 16 px in from the window's edges, its text in ink and gray), the third kind of red root the state walk checks (`expectTag: 'frame'`), and the page beneath it, the start button included, is `visibility: hidden`; it leaves in one frame on leave or drop. Score, paused after a note was heard (T10): the cursor's notes of the last group heard, the cursor kind of red root (`score-paused`), and Play is plain; paused before any note was heard (in the lead-in), rewound, at the end, or paused with a loop set since that leaves the pause position outside it (Play would start at the loop's first bar: nothing to resume where the marks were): Play. The Practice toggle is a disclosure and never red; chosen level pills are ink-filled, never red. The list scrolled past the start button shows no red in view while the DOM still holds exactly one: that is the behaviour today (the red is in view for about 2 % of the 149-piece scroll), and the red is never made sticky (decided by the coordinator for the batch, plan open question 1; the sentence goes into the product spec after the batch, and the user is told in the batch report). The notice, the end line, the search field and Clear, the light and dark switch, hover and focus states, pressed keys, the hand letters and finger digits are never red. Between Play and the cursor the red travels instead of jumping (motion study 05, `score/RedFlight.tsx`): Play gives it up and, in the same frame, a shape (`div.red-ghost-shape.is-red`, a plain red root outside the sheet) carries it for up to 200 ms, to the first notehead at the end of a lead-in that Play began, or back to Play after Rewind while stopped and at the end; on every frame one of the three is red. The easter egg's notes are ink, with one deliberate exception, the user's: a double-click on nothing in particular sends one note of its bigger burst up in the accent for about half a second (`app/NoteBurst.tsx`, `note-burst-note is-red`), next to the screen's red element.

Three enforcement points:

1. `scripts/check-colors.mjs` (the `design` step of `pnpm verify`): no color literal in `apps/web/src` or `index.html` outside `tokens.css`, no second chromatic color in `tokens.css`, and the red referenced only by `.is-red`.
2. `apps/web/e2e/helpers/red.ts`: `expectOneRed(page)` applies the definition above in the browser; `e2e/states.spec.ts` runs it in every application state on desktop and at 360 px, and the flow specs run it at their own checkpoints.
3. Testing Library assertions in the component tests: the library and about views render exactly one `.is-red` element (the right button), the transport's Play button carries it while stopped and not while playing, and `Button`, `Keyboard` and the renderer's `dom` helpers have their own checks.

## Light and dark

T7, chosen by the user on 2026-10-16 from two rounds of studies on a design canvas (seven music-themed switches were rejected: a theme switch must read as light/dark at a glance). The page follows the system's color scheme (`prefers-color-scheme`) until the reader chooses a theme with the switch; the choice is kept per browser.

- **Tokens** (`design/tokens.css`). The dark set redefines paper (`#111111`), ink (`#f2f2f2`) and the three grays for the same contrasts (secondary text 5.6:1), and leaves the one red unchanged. It is written twice, once under `@media screen and (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` for `:root:not([data-theme='light'])` and once for `:root[data-theme='dark']`; `design/tokens.test.ts` keeps the two identical and checks every text and key pair at 4.5:1 (3:1 for the red and the black keys) in both themes. Screens only: a print is ink on paper in either theme. Two tokens are the same in both themes on purpose: `--on-accent` (the white label on a red button, 4.9:1; paper on red would be 4.3:1 in the dark) and `--invert` (the Hands thumb's difference blend needs white). The `--key-*` set keeps a real keyboard's colors rather than inverting: light keys with dark digits and hand letters on them in both themes (in the dark white keys `#dadada`, pressed keys `#8f8f8f`, black keys the paper). `color-scheme` follows the theme, so scrollbars and native controls match.
- **The score** has its own ink, `--score-ink`: the ink in light, `#d4d4d4` in the dark (13:1), because a page of notes in near-white glared and `#c4c4c4` looked too gray (the user, 2026-10-16; the white keys were softened with it). Verovio's inner `svg.definition-scale` carries `color="black"` and leaves `fill` at its initial black; base.css sets both from `--score-ink`. The red cursor note stands out more against the softer engraving.
- **The switch** (`design/ThemeToggle.tsx`): a round 44 px button with no border at the top right of every view, on the row of the view's first line, after Back in the tab order (first in the header where the heading is the first row, which keeps 3.25 rem clear of it). Named "Dark theme" with `aria-pressed`; a 24-unit icon, the crescent's bite a mask so the hover fill shows nothing of it; never red, not printed.
- **The choice** (`design/theme.ts`): `chooseTheme` sets `data-theme` on the root and keeps it under `digitus2.theme`; choosing the system's own theme removes both, so the page follows the system again. Blocked site data still switches the page for the visit. A choice made in another tab is taken up (`storage` event). The inline script in `index.html` applies a kept choice before the first paint; `design/theme.test.ts` runs that script against fakes so the key and values cannot drift.
- **Tests**: `e2e/theme.spec.ts` (follows the system, the switch overrides, is kept across a reload and views and gives the page back to the system, a kept theme is on the page with no application JavaScript at all, the engraving and keys in the dark, print in the dark, the switch's place at 360 px); `e2e/states.spec.ts` runs every state's one-red, axe and overflow check again with the system in dark, desktop and 360 px; `e2e/visual.spec.ts` has dark baselines for the library, the score ready and paused, the about page and the lettered keyboard.

## Known limitations in this milestone

- Photo/PDF scanning is not shipped in this app. A separate experimental worktree evaluates on-device browser recognition; reliable recognition and product integration remain unfinished.

- Repeats are not expanded: playback and the cursor follow document order.
- MIDI files are imported (M6): quantized per beat to 16th notes, 16th-note triplets or 32nd notes; one voice per hand; the hands separated automatically by a learned pitch-trajectory DP, which put 92.05 percent of notes in the correct hand on the PIG test split and 85.10 percent on the Mutopia test half (macro means; scorecard m6-hands, `benchmarks/history.md`), with the `Hand split` control as the override; hands crossed at one instant always go by pitch; performance recordings without a tempo map are placed on a 120 quarter-note-per-minute grid.
- Fingerings cannot be edited or pinned yet, and there are no explanations or confidence marks (M5).
- Every note's digit is printed; the readability reduction (print only the fingers a reader needs) is M5.
- Settings persist across visits; of what is per piece only the tempo factor does (M4 polish, Task 6), with the settings, for the 50 pieces changed last (no saved edits, no saved loop or position). The library's scroll position lasts only for the visit.
- The tempo control counts quarter notes per minute, as the transport plays (MusicXML's `<sound tempo>` is in quarter notes): a piece that prints its tempo in another unit shows the same speed in quarters (Greensleeves prints `♩. = 64` and the control says `100 % · ♩ = 96`). A piece without a tempo mark plays, and shows, the engine's default, `♩ = 120`. With tempo changes inside the piece, the control shows the tempo where the piece starts.
- A dropped file cannot be restored after a reload (`#/piece/file` falls back to the library).
- The hand-size profiles use the engine's rule weights; learned weight sets (and the beginner and expert profiles) arrive with M3.
- Piano samples load from smplr's hosted sample site on the first Play of each score (2.35 s in the first UI review; the status line says `Loading the piano…` meanwhile); self-hosting them is a later task (spike section 9).
- A long piece takes a moment to open (K.545: about 0.5 to 0.8 s on the development machine, most of it Verovio's layout; 2.4 s in M4, before the engine's writer fix); the status line shows meanwhile.
- Known edge of the cursor: switching hands while playing, to a hand that has not been heard since playback last started (Play, a seek or a loop wrap), marks a note of that hand that was not heard since: its last onset before that start, or, when it has none, its first onset, before it sounds (the floor in `useCursor.ts`), until its next note is heard. The transport starts no lead-in on a hand switch; Play, a seek, Rewind and a loop wrap all wait for the hands played.
- With one hand muted, its dimmed keys follow its own onsets and stay down until its next onset or the played hand's, like the pressed keys: a short note (staccato) shows held until the next onset of either hand. On a phone the keyboard scrolls to follow the pressed keys only, so a dimmed key can sit outside its visible part. In the score, that hand's slurs, ties and dynamics stay in ink (they belong to the bar, not to a note), and a note of it that shares a chord with the hand played is dimmed on its own while the chord's stem stays in ink.
- The bold cursor is drawn for the notes as engraved at Verovio's fixed scale, so its size follows the note: at 360 px the box of a stemmed note's red was 4.3 to 8.9 times a notehead's box in the playback test (K.545, bars 30 and 31), and its red pixels 1.8 to 4.2 noteheads' boxes, against 0.8 to 0.9 and about 0.6 for the old red notehead. A beamed note with a short stem is the smallest mark (one sampled elsewhere in K.545 came to 3.75 times), and a note with no stem (a whole note) only gains its 2 px outline.
- Genre pills and folded collections are specified (design section 8: the genre row `Kind of music` above 60 pieces in a level, collections folded to one line each above 60 pieces in view, with `hidden="until-found"`) but not built: at 149 pieces no level holds more than 43, so neither would show. The batch ships their data and rules (`genre` on every manifest entry, `GENRES_ABOVE`, `GENRE_MIN`, `FOLD_ABOVE`, `genrePills`); L2 builds the controls when a level first passes 60 pieces.
- The library imports the whole manifest in its entry chunk (120.4 KB gzip at 149 pieces); the slim library index and the lazy manifest wait for L2, with the 1,000-piece performance gates (a level change painted within 100 ms, a search keystroke within 50 ms, under 4x CPU throttling).
- There is no type-to-search: the search field takes text only once it has the focus (Tab from the red button, or a click).
- The list scrolled past the red button shows no red element in view (the DOM still holds exactly one); the red is never made sticky.

## What the daily UI review should look at

Verification spec 3.10 asks for a daily qualitative review by an independent subagent, which writes `docs/reviews/ui-YYYY-MM-DD.md` and never edits product code. For this app it should, as a beginner would: open the piece the red Start button names (Continue on a returning visit) and one level 3 piece, read the fingering (are the digits legible, next to the right notes, not colliding with other marks), play with the cursor and the keyboard (does the cursor match what is heard, are the pressed keys and their finger numbers readable on a phone), change the hand size and switch the editor's fingering on and off, loop two bars, drop a MusicXML file and a MIDI file, export, print, and read the about page. On every screen: is the next step obvious, is exactly one thing red, is anything cluttered or ambiguous, and what would a piano teacher object to. Since the M4 polish (from the first review, `docs/reviews/ui-2026-10-14.md`) it should also check that what was fixed stays fixed: Pause within reach in the dock wherever the cursor has scrolled the score, a phone opening a score with music on its first screen, the status line saying what Play is doing (the piano loading, the count), the cursor easy to follow, the finger numbers explained, and no developer text outside `Details`. The automated checks above cover the one-red rule, accessibility and layout overflow; the review covers what they cannot judge. Since the UI batch it should also look at: the level pills at 360 and 1280 px (wrapped onto a second row at 360, none cut off; the capsule is the only rounded shape on any page, and a chosen pill is ink, never red); the six level lines ("Each hand stays in one place." and the others, `LEVEL_LINES` in `src/library/levels.ts`), which also go through the next teacher review before L2; whether the start row, the pills, the headings and the rows read as clear units at every level, and the search, its empty result and the end line; the paused cursor (the last group heard red, its keys down, Play plain) and the `Practice` toggle in the dock; and the hand letters: they show only in bars where the hands come within a fifth, and on a phone, where the keyboard follows the keys struck at the cursor, a hand scrolled out of view leaves a lone number with no letter (design section 15, rule 5). Look at the G major minuet (BWV Anh. 114) and Amazing Grace at 360 px, in bars the rule leaves plain, and say whether a reader is lost there; if so, the fallback is ready in the design: a hand out of view during a bar keeps the letters to the end of that bar, keyed by the cursor group's measure index, with the same one-bar bridge.
