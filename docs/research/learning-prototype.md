# Learning prototype for M3 (Python, PIG validation only)

Written 2026-10-13/14 by the research lead for the M3 learning plan
(`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-13-m3-learning.md`). Everything below is measured on the 20
**PIG validation** pieces drawn from the official train split, or by 5-fold cross-validation over
all 120 train pieces. The PIG test split (001-030) was never decoded, scored or opened.

Code: `research/learn/` (Python 3.11, numpy; runs in `research/.venv`). Results: JSON under
`research/learn/results/`, tables regenerated by
`research/.venv/Scripts/python.exe -I -m learn.experiments.make_report`
(`results/report_tables.md`, `results/summary.json`).

Two engine details that the plan's `pathFeatures` must reproduce were found this way: the
`template` bonus is applied inside `solveHand`, not by `unaryTerms` (M2 Task 8 landed during this
work; the Parncutt set on disk now has `template: 2`), and the engine returns the penalty *alone*
(no pair or triple terms) for a same-finger slot on a still-sounding note, while a within-slice
order violation costs penalty *plus* the chord terms; only soft decodes see the difference.

## 3. Data facts that affect the plan

* PIG train minus validation: 100 pieces, 200 hands, 29,563 notes; validation: 20 pieces, 40 hands,
  8,936 notes (annotations 1-4 per piece).
* In 24 of the 159 train files an annotator puts some notes in the other hand than the first
  annotation. Such notes are gold `0` for that annotation here (the plan's `toHandExamples` would
  copy the other hand's finger).
* 28.3% of hand-annotations (90 of 318 on the 120 train pieces) contain a fingering the solver
  cannot produce under its hard rules: 20 within-slice order violations, 108 adjacent same-finger
  notes while the first still sounds (overlap median 49 ms; 52% at most 50 ms, 35% over 200 ms),
  363 same-finger reuses 3+ notes later while the earlier note still sounds (`heldConflicts`).
  About 1% of notes are involved, so the ceiling cost is small, but a "skip infeasible targets"
  rule throws away a quarter of the hands.
* Mutopia test half: 62 parts, 8,805 fingered notes (single editor, so M_gen = M_high = M_soft =
  M_rec). The Clementi block (71 parts, one Mutopia id) landed in the train half; the split is
  lumpy by construction (224 vs 62 parts for a 70/30 split of ids).
* Beringer: 83 pieces after excluding the C-sharp minor broken chords, 166 hands, 8,696 notes.
  Textbook: 672 entries, 9,216 notes. Mutopia train: 224 parts, 428 hands with at least one
  fingered note, 129,167 notes, 27,378 fingered.

## 4. Method

* Features: `cost(path) = sum_rule weight[rule] x points[rule]`, points summed over the unary,
  pair and triple terms exactly as `solveHand` charges them (template bonus included, the
  "same finger in a slice of more than five notes" slot free). Feature sets are whole families;
  the plan's Task 1 set is `rules + colour + trans + chord + lattice + timing` = 99 ids.
* Decoder: exact second-order Viterbi (25 states, 125 triple evaluations per note), the engine's
  tie-breaking (lexicographically smallest optimal path).
* Trainer, per hand example and epoch (seeded shuffle): pick the target annotation (`latent` = the
  one agreeing most with the current plain decode; `first` = lowest slot); target path `y+` =
  gold on fingered notes, model's choice elsewhere (pinned soft decode); most violating path
  `y^` = hard decode with `-rho` on every wrong finger of a fingered note; `loss = max(0,
  w.Phi(y+) - w.Phi(y^) + rho x hamming)`; `w -= tau x sample_weight x (Phi(y+) - Phi(y^))` with
  `tau = min(C, loss / |d|^2)` (PA-I) or `eta` (perceptron); penalty rules clamped at 0; running
  average over all steps; the epoch with the best validation M_gen (averaged weights) is kept.
* Scoring: `fingering_eval.metrics` in `official` mode (the paper's Table 2 scorer), macro over
  pieces; gold notes with finger 0 masked; paired bootstrap over pieces for every delta.
* Protocol: hyperparameters on the 20 validation pieces (section 5.1), then every configuration
  question re-asked by 5-fold CV over all 120 train pieces with a fixed 3 epochs and the last
  epoch's averaged weights (section 5.2), because 20 pieces give ±3.5-point intervals.

The effect is monotone in the weight: every non-PIG corpus mixed into the PIG set costs PIG
accuracy, and only at weight 0.02 is the loss inside the noise.

Reading: PIG performers and Mutopia editors disagree systematically (the datasets report found
72.7% agreement on the overlapping pieces, about the inter-pianist level); a linear model with one
weight vector cannot serve both, and the sparse editorial golds (19% of notes fingered) pull the
weights towards the editorial style. The shippable open set learns Mutopia (+8.9 on the held-out
half) but nothing that transfers to PIG. Textbook and Beringer at weight 0.5 also hurt PIG when
mixed in (67.7 vs 70.4), because scale and arpeggio fingerings are far more regular than music.

Each epoch is roughly 2.4 decodes per hand (latent-target decode on multi-annotation hands, the
augmented decode, two path-feature sums) plus the validation decode. The engine, warm, needs 29 us
per note for `solveFingering` (1.6 `solveHand` calls per hand, explanations included; 114 pieces,
32,603 notes, 946 ms, from `engine_reference.json`), i.e. at most 18 us per note per `solveHand`.
Extrapolated: **1.3 s per epoch on PIG train, 2.2 s on the `pig` corpus (12 epochs: 26 s)**, far
inside the 15-minute budget even if the TypeScript trainer recomputes every term on every call.

## 6. Recommended configuration for the M3 trainer

Train the research set on **PIG train minus validation only** (no textbook, no Beringer, no
Mutopia), with the plan's Task 1 feature set (99 ids; the extra families are noise), averaged
PA-I with Hamming-augmented decoding at **rho 1, C 0.2** (C hardly matters between 0.01 and 1),
Parncutt initialisation, penalty projection on, the latent closest-annotator target, no skipping
of infeasible targets (the gold is the target; pins are hard), **12 epochs with the epoch chosen by
validation M_gen** (expect the peak at epoch 2-5), and report the mean of 3 seeds on validation
(sd 0.43 per run). Expected: validation M_gen 69.5 ± 0.4 (+4.8 over the rules), i.e. the M2 +2
acceptance in Task 4 should pass comfortably. For the shippable set, train on textbook + Beringer
+ Mutopia train with the same settings but expect PIG-level accuracy equal to the rules (64.9)
and a large gain on the Mutopia oracle (65.5 vs 56.6); do not promise FingerFlow's 68.6 from it.

## 7. Corrections to the M3 plan

1. **Task 0, `pathFeatures` and the identity test.** M2 Task 8 added a `template` bonus that
   `solveHand` applies outside `unaryTerms` (`-weights.template` per matched note; the Parncutt
   set on disk has `template: 2`). `pathFeatures(seq, ctx, fingers)` must take the template
   matches (or recompute `matchTemplates(seq, detectRuns(seq), TEXTBOOK_TEMPLATES[hand])`) and
   add `template: -1` per matched note, and `train` must pass the same matches to every
   `solveHand` call, or the identity `dot(weights, pathFeatures) == cost` fails on any piece with
   a scale run and the learned `template` weight is garbage. Also mirror the "same finger in a
   slice of more than five notes costs 0" slot (no pair terms) and note that soft decodes charge
   the penalty *instead of* the terms for a same-finger-while-sounding slot, so the identity only
   holds for finite hard paths.
2. **Task 1 definitions.** State that `tr:<g><f>:<dir>` fires for `g === f` too (same-finger
   hops; the learned `tr:11:*`, `tr:22:*` weights are among the largest), that `shiftFast` is a
   different-slice term only, and generate the Parncutt regression fixture after Task 8's
   template bonus (it changes the right-hand output of the sample).
3. **Task 2, hands.** 24 of 159 train files put some notes in the other hand than the first
   annotation; `toHandExamples` must set gold 0 for a note whose annotation-k hand differs from
   the sequence's hand instead of copying the finger. Mutopia split counts for the test: 112/48
   ids, 224/62 parts; Beringer 83 pieces (confirmed); textbook 672 (confirmed).
4. **Task 3, infeasible targets.** Do not skip examples whose pinned target is relaxed: 49 of 200
   training hands (28% of all hand-annotations) are infeasible under the hard rules, skipping them
   costs −0.6 and loses a quarter of the data. Use the pinned soft path as the target (the gold on
   fingered notes) and only count the cases.
5. **Task 3, test 4 (`with several annotators the closest one is the target`).** `updates === 0`
   in epoch 1 only holds with `rho = 0`; with `rho = 1` the augmented decode finds a cheap wrong
   path and the margin loss is positive (1 update, loss 1.0 in the port). Set `rho: 0` in that
   test or assert on the chosen annotation instead.
6. **Task 3, epoch selection.** `evaluateExamples` counts a note correct when it matches *any*
   annotation (M_soft-like). Select the epoch by the official M_gen on the validation pieces (the
   bench scorer already exists) and keep the per-epoch curve in the log; the peak is at epoch 2-5
   and the averaged weights then drift down by about a point.
7. **Task 3 defaults.** `C: 0.2` (anything in 0.01-1 works), `rho: 1`, `epochs: 12`, averaging
   mandatory (the un-averaged curve oscillates between 60 and 68), projection kept (cheap,
   −0.2 to −1.5 without). `repeatedNoteFingerChange` must stay out of `isPenaltyRule` (it goes
   negative: experts change fingers on slow repeated notes). PA-I vs a fixed-step averaged
   perceptron (eta 0.005-0.1) is a wash; keep PA-I.
8. **Task 4, the `pig` configuration.** Drop textbook and Beringer from the research set: PIG +
   textbook + Beringer at the plan's weight 0.5 scores 68.0 on validation against 70.4 for PIG
   alone (−2.4 [−3.9, −0.9]); at weight 0.1 still −1.6 [−3.0, −0.3]; only at 0.02 is the loss
   inside the noise (−0.8 [−2.6, +0.6]), and mixing the Mutopia train half in at 0.1 costs −1.3
   (section 5.4). If the textbook must stay in the research set for the Task 4 textbook gate, use
   weight 0.02 or pin `template` instead. The "+2 over M2 on the test split" acceptance should
   pass with PIG alone (+4.8 validation, +6.8 CV).
9. **Task 4, the `open` configuration and the goals.** The shippable set reaches rule-level
   accuracy on PIG (64.9, +0.2 [−3.2, +3.6]) and +8.9 on the Mutopia test half. The plan and
   `docs/PROGRESS.md` state "M3 target is to beat FingerFlow-learned 68.6 (69), stretch 71": that
   is reachable only with PIG-fitted weights, which cannot ship. Decide now: ask the PIG
   maintainers for permission, or state that the shipped set is scored against the Mutopia oracle
   and the research set against PIG. Task 9's floors should be set per weight set accordingly.
10. **Task 4 sample weights.** They make no measurable difference in the open set (64.88 vs 64.74
    with all weights 1); keep them for the provenance story but do not expect anything from them.
11. **Task 7 hypotheses.** Five candidate families were tried here (rest coupling, Fitts, cm
    geometry, transition x colour, lattice bins): all within noise on PIG; do not spend the three
    iterations on them. Two hypotheses the data support instead: (H1) the still-sounding
    same-finger hard rule is stricter than pianists (52% of the 108 expert overlaps are at most
    50 ms, `HELD_EPS` is 1 ms): make it a learned soft penalty (`heldSameFinger`, points = 1 or the
    overlap in seconds) or raise the epsilon to about 50 ms, which also removes most "relaxed"
    hands; (H2) a `restRelax` variant for `handPositionShift` only (the plan's halving after a
    rest is fixed by hand). The acceptance threshold "+0.3 on validation" is below the seed noise
    (sd 0.43): use 3 seeds or the 5-fold CV from `research/learn` (`grid cv`, 3 minutes) and ask
    for +1.0, or at least a paired interval excluding 0.
12. **Task 4 / Global, speed.** The 15-minute budget is met by more than 20x (section 5.5); a
    `--seeds 3` option costs a minute and gives the variance the report needs.
13. **Task 5 / Task 9, Mutopia oracle expectations.** Rule-level Mutopia test M_gen is 56.6; the
    open set reaches about 65.5 and a PIG-trained set 59.9. The Mutopia floor should be set from
    the open set, not from a PIG-trained set.

## 8. What could not be verified

* The PIG test split was never decoded, so nothing here is a test-split number; the baselines
  report's 68.6 (FingerFlow) is on the test split and is only comparable in spirit.
* Parity with the engine was checked with the Parncutt weights (integer and half-integer costs,
  where tie-breaking is exact). With learned non-integer weights, floating-point summation order
  can break exact ties differently in TypeScript and numpy; expect rare one-note differences.
* The `digitus2 finger` CLI did not exist yet; the reference decode imports the engine sources
  through tsx at the hashes in `results/engine_parity.json`. If `rules.ts`, `solve.ts` or
  `templates.ts` change, re-run `engine_solve.ts` and `compare_engine.py`.
* TypeScript training time is extrapolated from the engine's measured decode throughput, not
  measured.
* Calibration (Task 6), the beginner profile (Task 8) and the textbook exact-rate gate under
  learned weights were not prototyped. The learned `template` weight drops from 2 to 0.4 in the
  PIG-only set, so the textbook gate of Task 4 ("100 on one- and two-octave scales") needs to be
  checked on the real trainer; if it fails, train with the textbook at a small weight or pin
  `template` at its Parncutt value.
* The Mutopia split's lumpiness (Clementi's 71 parts in train) was not corrected; a split by
  composer or a stratified split would be more balanced, but Task 2's specification was followed
  exactly so that the TypeScript side reproduces the same file.
