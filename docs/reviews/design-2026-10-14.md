# Design critique: the library and the score view, 2026-10-14

- Date: 2026-10-14
- Scope: the landing page (the library view) in depth, as a beginner meets it; the score view only for the three small items of the UI batch, T1 (practice controls), T10 (pause) and T11 (hand letters on the keyboard). The brief is the user's words in `docs/TODO.md` T6, T1, T10 and T11, and the minimalism and progressive-disclosure rules of the spec (section 9) and `CLAUDE.md`.
- Builds looked at: `http://localhost:5174` (main after the M4 polish, 30 pieces: the product today) and `http://localhost:5175` (the unmerged L1 library branch, the layout from before the polish, used only for the library's size and metadata). The task brief said 147 pieces on 5175; the L1 manifest (`thingamajig-wt/library-150/content/manifest.json`) and the page both hold **149**.
- Method: the `design:design-critique` framework (first impression, usability, hierarchy, consistency, accessibility). Throwaway Playwright scripts (Chromium) at 360 x 780, 768 x 1024 and 1280 x 800 took the screenshots and read the DOM: texts, bounding boxes, computed styles, the `.is-red` elements. For T10 and T11 the scripts played pieces with `?audio=silent` and recorded every keyboard state with a `MutationObserver` (pressed keys, their hand, the hand letters, the cursor's onset tick and bar) for two whole pieces. No product code was edited.
- Reviewer: design critic subagent for the UI batch.

## Overall impression

The page is calm, black and white, and the red Start button is unmistakably the first thing to do. Two things hold it back. First, the top of the page asks a beginner to read **64 words in 8 separate units** before the first piece, and three of those units belong to other moments (reading a score, opening a file, re-reading the list's own first row). Second, and much bigger, the layout does not scale: with the L1 library the same page is **17,477 px tall at 360 px (22.4 phone screens)**, one flat list under five level headings, while the metadata that could structure it (23 collections) is not shown at all. The redesign is mostly subtraction at the top and one new layer of structure (level first, collections inside a level) that appears only once the list is long.

## 5. What must be kept, because it works

- **The one red button as the first step.** One `.is-red` in the DOM in every state measured, at every width; the first UI review found it the obvious first action, and so does this one.
- **The welcome sentence** "Pick a piece, press play, and follow the red note." It is short and true, and it teaches the red rule in eight words.
- **Level as the main axis**, ordered from easy to hard, with friendly names ("First steps", "Easy").
- **The row as one big button**: whole-row target (75 to 103 px tall), bold title, gray composer, tabular length; `aria-label` "Open <title> by <composer>" described by the length; secondary text turns ink on hover and focus because gray on gray-1 is under AA.
- **Showing the length.** A beginner picks short pieces first, and the level medians make it meaningful (0:19 at level 1, 1:35 at level 5).
- **Typography and color**: one grotesque, 16 px base, 28 px name, 18 px section headings; `--gray-3` (#767676) at 4.54:1 on white; no color outside the tokens.
- **Quiet footer**: "About and licences" as the last line.
- **Performance and behavior**: largest contentful paint about 56 to 60 ms (`docs/app.md`); the list position restored on Back within a visit; no horizontal scroll anywhere.
- **Score view**: the sticky dock with Play or Pause, Rewind, the status line and the keyboard, and Space to play; the phone's closed `Practice` disclosure, which puts the first system at 278 px on a 780 px screen.

## 7. What the facts mean for a filter design

These are constraints, not a design.

1. **Level is the only axis that is small, fixed and ordered**: 5 values now, 6 after L2. Six short pills fit one row at 360 px. It stays the same size at 30, 150 and 1,000 pieces, so it can be the one pill row.
2. **Collections cannot be pills.** There are 23 now (most of them small, names up to 62 characters) and there will be dozens. Inside a chosen level they work as quiet subheadings: Level 3 would read as 10 groups (Burgmüller 15, Anna Magdalena 7, Schumann 5, Sonatinas 5, Little Preludes 3, …) instead of 41 rows. Because 15 collections span several levels, a collection heading appears under each level it touches.
3. **A genre pill row would mostly repeat the level.** All 24 level-1 pieces and 32 of the 43 level-2 pieces are song arrangements; levels 3 to 5 are entirely original piano music. "Songs" with level 3 would be empty. The manifest has no genre or era field. If a genre filter is still wanted, it needs a small curated field (song or piano piece; perhaps era for L2) and pills that hide whenever a combination would be empty. At 149 pieces I would not build it.
4. **Composer pills do not scale** (40 composers, 25 with one piece). Search covers composers and titles.
5. **Thresholds.** At 30 pieces a level holds 3 to 10 rows: no pills, no search, the page as it is minus the clutter. At 149 a level holds 14 to 43 rows: level pills earn their place, and so do collection subheadings. Search earns a permanent field somewhere past roughly 100 pieces; before that it can appear when the reader starts typing.
6. **Rows must keep a disambiguator**: three title pairs collide.
7. **The recommended piece** is the first level-1 piece in manifest order (Twinkle in both libraries).

### 8.3 T11: the L and R letters on the keyboard

**When they appear today**: always (`Keyboard.tsx`, `handMark`). Every pressed key, and every dimmed key of a muted hand, gets a bold L or R of 10 drawing units (9 on a black key; about 9 to 11 px on screen) 18 units above its finger number, in every state, including moments when only one hand plays.

What this says:

1. **An octave is not "close" in this repertoire.** In the minuet the hands sit about an octave apart all the time (median 15 semitones, 46 of 134 two-hand onsets at 12 or less), and they are never confusable there: two groups a fifth or more apart read as left below, right above. A per-onset octave threshold would flick the letters on and off 41 times in 50 s (about every 1.2 s), and hysteresis around an octave still letters half the piece. Both fail the user's "only when the L and R notes are close to each other".
2. **A fifth (7 semitones, about four white keys), decided per bar, matches the user's words**: the minuet gets letters in 9 of 32 bars (1, 7, 8, 12, 15, 25, 26, 28, 32), where the hands really do meet (bar 26: left C4 E4 against right F#4), switching 12 times in 50 s; Amazing Grace never shows a letter. Interleaved hands (none in these two pieces) are always lettered, and both hands are lettered together, never one.
3. **One edge belongs to the phone.** At 360 px the keyboard is wider than the screen (at least 22 px per white key) and scrolls to the struck keys. In 11 of the minuet's 134 two-hand onsets and 3 of Amazing Grace's 31, only one hand is in the visible part, so a single key shows a lone number (`keyboard-far-360-5174.png`: only "R 5" at the right edge, the left hand's G3 out of view). Those are exactly the far-apart moments where the rule removes the letter. A simple way to keep the user's rule and stay unambiguous: count "the other hand is out of view" as close, so the visible hand keeps its letter.
4. **Moments with one hand only** (the pickup of Amazing Grace, bar 28 beat 3 of the minuet) need no letter by the user's rule; the score shows which staff is playing. They are rare in these two pieces (1 group each).

## 9. Hierarchy, consistency, accessibility

- **What draws the eye first** at 360 and 1280: the red button, then the bold name. Correct. Second-strongest is the ink legend, which outranks the gray tagline above it (C1).
- **Reading flow**: name, tagline, legend, file link, card heading, card title, card meta, red button, then the list. The primary action is the eighth thing read.
- **Whitespace**: generous and even (1.75 rem between sections); at 1280 the single 1,120 px column leaves the eye travelling across empty rows (C8).
- **Consistency**: level in three formats (C5); the file link's wording against the zone's (C2); the card's box is the only ink-bordered box on the page, competing with the red button inside it.
- **Contrast**: `--gray-3` #767676 on white is 4.54:1 (passes AA); on the hover gray the secondary text turns ink. Ink on `--gray-2` (pressed keys) passes easily.
- **Touch targets**: rows 75 to 103 px, the file link 44 px, the red button 44 px, the footer button 44 px: all pass.
- **Text size**: the tag is 12 px and the legend 14 px; the keyboard's hand letters are about 9 to 11 px on screen, small for the only cue they give when they are shown.
- **State checks**: one `.is-red` element in all six library states and in the score view's ready, playing, paused, resumed and rewound states; no horizontal scroll; no console errors.

## 10. Priority recommendations

1. **Give the long library one layer of structure, and only when it is long.** One row of level pills (5, later 6; single-select; the active pill ink-filled, never red), shown only when a level holds more pieces than fit a screen or two (not at 30); inside the chosen level, collections as quiet subheadings with their rows; a search field that appears when the library passes about 100 pieces or when the reader starts typing. Level 2 at 360 would go from one 4,608 px block to 14 small named groups behind one pill.
2. **Cut the top to two units.** The name with one sentence, then the one red button ("Start with Twinkle", or "Continue: <last piece>" for a returning reader). Remove the legend (move it to the score), the file link, the card's box, heading and repeated lines. About 64 words become about 20, and the first piece moves from 509 px to roughly 250 px on a phone.
3. **Fold "your own file" into one quiet line at the end**, with a drop anywhere on the window and no extension list.
4. **Make every row two lines**: the title, then "composer · length"; no tags; short titles under a collection; a reading measure at 1280.
5. **Score view**: one `Practice` disclosure at every width with the loop fields behind Loop (T1); the last group heard keeps the red while paused (T10); hand letters only when the hands are within a fifth or interleaved, decided per bar, plus the out-of-view case on a phone (T11).
