# Scanning (experimental)

Scanning turns a photo or PDF of printed piano music into a score with fingerings. Recognition runs on the project's scanner, a small service on Google Cloud: homr for photos and Audiveris for PDFs. It works the same on a PC, a tablet and a phone, with nothing to install and no account. The app shows the original beside the recognised notation, and you decide whether to use the result. Original PDFs and flat, high-resolution scans work best. Handwriting is not supported.

MusicXML, compressed MusicXML (`.mxl`) and MIDI open directly, without the scanner. Why these recognisers were chosen, and what was measured: `docs/research/omr.md`. How the scanner runs, what it costs and how its spend is capped: `docs/scan-service.md`.

## What the app guarantees

- **Uploaded original bytes are kept.** Unrotated images and PDFs go to the scanner byte for byte. A rotated image is sent as a lossless PNG derived from the original, and four turns restore the exact original file. PDFs are never rasterised in the browser. For guided camera capture, the combined, cropped page becomes the retained source; the five intermediate camera frames are temporary and are released after the page is kept.
- **The page goes only where the scan page says** (Where your page goes, below), and only when you press **Scan this score**.
- **Recognition errors stay visible.** The source and the recognised notation sit side by side, and a line under the title says what was read: the pages, the bars, both hands and the places to check, or the one thing that blocks the scan and what to do. Scanned notation goes through a strict import. A tie it cannot join (its continuation starts late or early, it has no matching note, or a grace note sits at one end) is played as separate notes and listed under **Places to check** with its fix, so it does not block **Use for fingerings** ([issue 233](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/233)). Anything else the import refuses (a pitch or duration that cannot be read) keeps **Use for fingerings** disabled; the alert names the bar, **Show bar N** selects its first note, and the result can still be downloaded.
- **Nothing is silently corrected.** The app never applies a repair on its own: a suggestion from homr's other readings of a pitch is applied only when you choose it, as an explicit correction. It never deletes bars or invents notes, meter or tempo. The scanner never picks one of several exported scores or publishes an incomplete export. The scanner changes a recognition only by two lossless rules, described under Known recognition errors and reported under Recognition details.
- **Your corrections are explicit.** Each correction you make in the review is recorded, listed and undoable. The raw recognition is kept unchanged beside the corrected copy, and both can be downloaded.
- **Display preparation never changes the music.** Inferred beams and a voice regrouping for Verovio (below) apply only to the rendered copy. Recognised pitches, timing, note ids, raw downloads and the input to fingering and playback are untouched.

## Where your page goes

The scan page says it in one sentence under the advice: "Recognition runs on the project’s scanner on Google Cloud (Netherlands). Your page is sent there, recognized and then deleted." Pressing **Scan this score** is the act that sends it; there is no separate consent step.

- **What is sent.** Each page's original bytes (or the rotated PNG), one page per request, over HTTPS to the scanner's address in `apps/web/src/scan/service.ts`. Nothing else: no name, no account, no other page. Opening the scan page sends one request that carries no page, to wake the scanner while you arrange the pages. While the scanner is busy with another reader's score, your page is sent again each time it is offered (every 5 s for up to three minutes), because the scanner reads a request before it answers that it is busy.
- **What the scanner keeps.** Nothing. The page is recognised in memory (Cloud Run's file system is memory) and deleted when the job ends, together with its result; there is no job list and no copy. For each page the scanner writes one log line with the file type, the sizes, the seconds taken and the outcome, never the content, the file name or your address. To hold each reader to 30 recognitions an hour it counts your address in memory for up to an hour; the count is gone when the scanner stops. Google Cloud keeps its own request log for the service (time, status and the caller's address), as for any Cloud Run service ([Cloud Run logging](https://cloud.google.com/run/docs/logging)).
- **One page at a time.** The scanner recognises one page at a time, for all readers together. When another reader's page is being recognised, your scan waits for it and starts as soon as it finishes. Only after three minutes of waiting does the app report the scanner busy and keep your pages; in a batch the pages after it are not sent, and **Retry failed pages** sends them.
- **The scanner sleeps when nobody scans.** The first scan after a quiet spell waits a few seconds while it starts; the bar allows for that until the scanner's warm-up has answered.
- **It needs a connection.** Offline, the app says it could not reach the scanner and keeps the pages; the rest of the app works offline as before.
- **Its spend is capped.** The project pays for the scanner and caps it at 10 a month; when the cap is reached, scanning is off until the next month and the app says "The scanner could not answer. Try again later." (`docs/scan-service.md`).

## Scan a score

### Photograph several pages

On a phone or touch tablet, **Photograph a printed score**, on its own line under **Open your own score**, opens the camera. It takes five photos of the page from slightly different spots and combines them into one sharp page. Lay the page flat, ideally on a darker surface so its edges stand out, hold the phone flat above it and tap the round red button. The line under the picture always says what to fix first: too dark, blurry, or nothing, then tap. When it is too dark and the browser can switch the phone's torch (Android Chrome can; Safari on iPhone does not offer it), **Light** turns it on. A tap always answers: it takes the sharpest frame of the next second (when the phone has not finished analysing any of them, the picture the camera shows at that moment), and if even that frame is too blurry to follow it says so and asks for another tap; a second blurred tap keeps that single photo for the review. After the first photo, move the phone slowly over the page, keeping it flat and the music in the picture, and pause now and then. Each time the phone pauses for about a third of a second somewhere new (a fifth of the way from the middle of the picture to its nearest edge away from every photo already taken), sharp, the next photo is taken by itself, in any order and direction. There is nothing to aim at: the red circle in the middle thickens as a pause begins, and nothing is drawn on the page. Progress is five small dots under "Photo n of 5" at the top, one per photo, outlined until the photo is taken and white after. A photo is shown the moment it is taken: a white flash passes over the picture, the circle swells and settles once, and the next dot fills. Each of the first four photos also says "Photo 1 of 5 taken." (then 2, 3 and 4) in the line under the picture for about a second, after which the line says how many photos are left. The fifth photo ends the sweep with the flash, the tick and the fifth dot filling; the count and the five dots stay while the line says "Combining your photos…" and go when the review opens. The circle is gone by then, because Cancel carries the screen's red while the photos combine. With reduced motion the flash and the swell are off and the dots still fill. A normal hand's shake and a slow drift count as a pause, and one shaky moment does not end it. Where the phone allows it, a tick is felt as each photo is taken (Android vibrates; iPhones tick from iOS 18, though iOS 26.5 is reported to have stopped ticks that a page triggers). On a page with blank areas, such as the last page of a piece, sweep over the music: the camera follows the page by its printed content. If no new photo is taken for ten seconds, **Skip the rest** finishes the page with the photos taken so far. The page outline is found automatically when the page stands out from its background, otherwise the photo less a small margin is used; in the review, **Adjust corners** moves the four corners to the paper and prepares the page again.

The camera combines the reference and four overlapping views locally into one cropped page. Check it before keeping it: retake any page with doubled staff lines, lost symbols or unreadable notes. Keep the page, photograph the next one, and finish to send the completed batch to the ordinary page-arrangement screen. You can reorder, rotate, remove and add images there before pressing **Scan this score**. The limit remains 20 pages, 10 MB per page.

Live capture needs a secure browser page and camera permission. **Use device camera** remains available when live capture cannot start. Uploaded images and PDFs still use the ordinary chooser. Camera acquisition and image processing stay in the browser; only **Scan this score** sends the resulting page images to the scanner. Closing the camera releases its video stream and discards pages that were not finished into the batch. The camera says it stopped only when it delivered no new picture during a tap, or the browser ended or muted it (iOS mutes the camera while another app or Control Center holds it); a picture that cannot be kept asks for another tap. Opening the app with `?camera=debug` in the address (before or after the `#`; it then stays on in that tab) shows, under the camera, how many frames arrived and were analysed, how each tap was settled and the last errors, so a phone that cannot be connected to a computer can still report what happened in a screenshot.

This follows Google's documented [PhotoScan capture interaction](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/7177983?hl=en): an initial reference plus four views. PhotoScan guides them with a center reticle and dots on the photographed print; digitus2 drops the dots on the print and takes a view whenever the phone pauses somewhere new, because the combination only needs four sharp, different views and aiming at dots was the hard part. Google's [research explanation](https://research.google/blog/photoscan-taking-glare-free-pictures-of-pictures/) describes global registration followed by local grid alignment, combination and rectification. digitus2 implements independent algorithms for this interaction, local alignment and score-specific page straightening. It does not claim equivalence to Google's closed implementation. Glare is removed where another view shows the page clean; a glare spot that every view shares stays.

### How reconstruction works

The four additional photographs overlap the reference; they are not stitched corner crops. Sparse image features inside the selected page establish a projective mapping into each view. Tracking uses small images for responsiveness. Captured images then receive local normalized patch matching with forward/backward and ambiguity checks. A smooth displacement grid handles spatially varying residual motion on curved paper. Unsupported areas retain the reference. This matters for music: a small alignment error can otherwise create a second stem when images are combined.

Perspective correction maps a rectangle into the adjusted source quadrilateral. When the views provide sufficient perspective information even allowing for corner-selection error, the page proportions come from orthogonal page axes under a centered, square-pixel pinhole-camera model ([Zhang's planar calibration constraints](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/tr98-71.pdf)). Near-frontal, inconsistent or curved views use observed edge proportions as an approximation. The browser does not provide calibrated lens intrinsics, so this is not a guarantee of exact physical dimensions.

For curved scores, the processor detects groups of five staff lines in the rectified reference and constructs a monotone vertical warp that straightens supported staves. The warp preserves line order and is composed with perspective correction before sampling the original pixels. It does not redraw notes. Short inset examples do not determine the page surface. Pages without reliable staff evidence receive perspective correction only. This is staff-based straightening, not calibrated three-dimensional surface recovery: strong folds, missing edges and horizontal foreshortening remain limitations.

The aligned views are combined per pixel with precision weights, the static form of a Kalman update: each photograph is one noisy measurement of the page, weighted by how sharp it is there, how well it is registered and how noisy it is. The page is combined in two bands. The base (the local paper and ink level) is a weighted median of the views, so a glare spot or a hand shadow in one photograph is outvoted by the others; a per-view illumination field first matches each photograph's paper level to the reference's. The detail (the strokes) comes from the photographs that are sharp in that region, and only from a view whose fine detail lines up with the others in that small tile: each view is correlated with the rest at offsets of up to two pixels, and a view that is off, even by one pixel, contributes only its base there. A mark that only one non-reference photograph shows (a hair, a scratch) is not painted, a clear mark in the reference keeps at least half its depth, and plain paper is averaged, which lowers the noise. The staff geometry still comes from the reference, and the page corners remain adjustable in the review.

This is measured, not assumed. A ground-truth test renders a page, photographs it five times with blur in different regions, a glare spot that moves, a hand shadow, noise and a locally misregistered view, and requires the combined page to be closer to the truth than the best single photograph, with no doubled, moved or invented line (`apps/web/src/scan/camera/fusion.oracle.test.ts`, with sixteen further safety cases in `fusion.safety.test.ts`). A recognition benchmark (`research/omr/camera-fusion.mjs`) photographs eight library pages the same way at three severities, combines them with the old rule, with PhotoScan's documented soft minimum and with this fusion, has homr read every result and scores it note by note against the library's MusicXML. Full-note F1 averaged over the eight pages:

| Five photographs per page                      | Moderate | Harsh (glare, shadow, blur) | Misregistered view |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------- | --------------------------- | ------------------ |
| First photograph alone                         | 92.5     | 58.1                        | 92.5               |
| Best single photograph (chosen with hindsight) | 92.5     | 64.7                        | 92.5               |
| Old rule (reference-led average)               | 87.9     | 67.9                        | 92.9               |
| PhotoScan's soft minimum (T = 6)               | 93.3     | 49.3                        | 93.3               |
| This fusion                                    | 93.3     | 73.1                        | 92.7               |

The clean render reads 93.5. The harsh average hides large swings per page: one page loses a barline and one recognition is refused by the strict import for an unrelated tie, while five of eight pages have exactly the right bars against two for the old rule. On the owner's two real photographs of page 25, the combined page keeps every staff line where the earlier version of this fusion doubled the ossia staves. Results are from synthetic captures; real optics remain a device check.

`scripts/check-score-camera-photos.mjs` runs the production reconstruction on a private manifest containing one to five actual views per case. It records input hashes, corners, independent raw-pixel staff traces, selected notation patches, contribution counts and before/after PNGs. Photographs and outputs stay under ignored `build/`. The available real-photo evidence comprises three photographs, including two views of one page; it does not establish five-view handheld performance, autofocus quality or recognition accuracy.

On those photographs, manually checked top-line identities and independent five-ruling traces preserve all 26 main staves in order. The table reports the worst RMS bow among the annotated top staff lines, measured in each image's pixels after removing a best-fit straight-line slope. Output tilt is also checked separately and stays below 0.08 degrees. This measures image geometry, not recognized pitches or rhythms.

| Actual input                        | Main staves retained | Worst RMS bow before | After   |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- | ------- |
| Page 25, photograph A               | 8 / 8                | 5.66 px              | 0.37 px |
| Page 25, photograph B               | 8 / 8                | 7.25 px              | 0.32 px |
| Improvisations, photograph C        | 10 / 10              | 3.01 px              | 0.79 px |

The C bottom staff retains 3.58 px of vertical range over its measured span, and its first upper line's RMS bow slightly worsens (0.45 to 0.55 px). Full-page inspection retains the inset examples and finds no newly missing or doubled symbols; selected-patch comparisons are supporting pixel evidence, not proof of musical accuracy. Strong localized curls, unsupported margins and clipped content remain limits. Source hashes are checked before and after each acceptance run so stale outputs cannot be mistaken for the current implementation.

### The loop on the PC

A change to the camera or to what the app does with a recognition is checked on the PC against the chain a phone runs, before any phone sees it. Two Playwright specs in `apps/web/e2e` do it; the scanner is the only part they mock.

- **`scan-grand-staff.spec.ts`** takes the owner's three recognised pages (`apps/web/fixtures/scan/`: two pages of one two-staff piano part, and a short last page read as a treble part and a bass part, which the join merges into the piano layout) through **Scan this score**, the review and **Use for fingerings**. In the review and again in the score view it reads the Verovio SVG: two staves in every bar, a brace on every system, no bar lost (25 bars, 12 + 10 + 3), no fingering in the review and, in the score view, every digit above the upper staff or below the lower, with both in at least one bar. It runs in Chromium at 360 px and 1280 px and in WebKit.
- **`scan-join-names.spec.ts`** takes the owner's three phone photos of Boëllmann's Improvisation Op. 28 No. 1 as the scanner read them (`apps/web/fixtures/scan/boellmann-op28-no1-page-*.musicxml`; [issue 230](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/230)). Pages 1 and 3 join, and the first-ending bracket the scanner read into bar 2 of page 1 is a place to check with its photo and **Remove the repeat ending**, which Undo takes back; Recognition details shows the scanner's export note in the reader's words and none of its disclaimers. With page 2, read as a piano part of 17 bars plus a second instrument of 19, the verdict line names page 2 and what was read, and the red is on **Replace page 2 with a new photo**.
- **`scan-camera-loop.spec.ts`** runs the camera itself over the committed photo of a printed page (`research/omr/photos/boellmann-op28-page2-flat.jpeg`, filmed through a tilted camera with hand tremor and noise), sweeps five photos and keeps the page. The page it keeps is the file the app would upload. Local homr reads that page and, for comparison, the flat photo the camera filmed. The facts of the fused page are compared with the manifest the scanning gate uses (`research/omr/photos/manifest.json`): exactly its bars, staves, key and time signature, the same parts and staves per part as the flat photo, a strict import that succeeds, and a note count within 10% of the flat photo's (the gate has no notes fact, so the bound is the loop's own: the runs read the same count for both pages). The scan service then answers with that recognition, and the page goes through the review and the score view as a grand staff in the way the first spec checks. It runs in Chromium as a phone window, not in WebKit: the Linux image has no homr, so the loop would only skip there.

| Boëllmann Op. 28 No. I, page 2 | Bars | Staves | Key (fifths) | Time | Notes the strict import keeps |
| ------------------------------ | ---: | -----: | -----------: | ---- | ----------------------------: |
| Manifest                       |   24 |      2 |            1 | 3/4  |                               |
| Fused page, read by homr       |   24 |      2 |            1 | 3/4  |                           203 |
| Flat photo, read by homr       |   24 |      2 |            1 | 3/4  |                           203 |

The review draws all 24 bars in two staves and the score view puts a digit on every one of the 203 notes the import keeps, and the digits above the upper staff and below the lower number the right and the left hand's notes (88 and 115). Which staff holds a fingered note is read two ways, because homr numbers voices across staves and Verovio keeps a cross-staff note inside the staff of its voice: 96 of the 563 notes of the three owner pages are drawn in the second staff but sit in the first staff's group. By the group holding the note, both staves are fingered in 16 of the 25 bars of those pages; by where the digit is drawn, above the upper staff and below the lower, both hands are in all 25. The specs assert both, each in at least one bar, and that every digit is drawn above the upper staff or below the lower; the loop also asserts a digit on every note the import keeps.

Run them after `pnpm build` at the repository root (the loop imports the built engine and fails without it), and stop any preview already listening on the e2e port first (`docs/verification-loop.md`):

```bash
# from apps/web
pnpm exec playwright test scan-grand-staff --project=mobile --project=desktop
DIGITUS2_E2E_LOOP=1 DIGITUS2_OMR_HOMR=<prepared homr folder> pnpm exec playwright test --project=loop

# from the repository root: WebKit, in Playwright's Linux image under Docker
node scripts/e2e-webkit.mjs scan-grand-staff
```

WebKit runs in Docker because Playwright's WebKit for Windows has no video streams. The loop needs a prepared homr (`DIGITUS2_OMR_HOMR`, the folder the scanning gate uses; `DIGITUS2_OMR_PYTHON` names the Python 3.11 command, `python` by default) and takes 40 to 50 seconds, 24 to 31 of them homr's. Where homr or Python 3.11 is missing it skips itself and says why, naming `DIGITUS2_OMR_HOMR` or `DIGITUS2_OMR_PYTHON`; a skipped loop has not run. Its fused page (`fused.jpg`), both recognitions, `loop.json` with every number above and the review and score screenshots are left in Playwright's output folder for the run. `pnpm verify` runs the grand-staff spec in its `e2e` step and the loop alone, after it, as its `e2e:loop` step (the `loop` project, a phone window that exists only when `DIGITUS2_E2E_LOOP` asks for it): homr's threads slow every other spec's timing, so the loop never shares the machine with them. Without a prepared homr that step reports SKIP, which is not a pass.

### Validation cases

Every scan the owner keeps is a validation case ([issue 233](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/233)): a clean photo of a printed page whose notes are read right must reach **Use for fingerings**, and a scan refused must say why in the reader's words, naming the page or the bar, with a fix to press. A case is a folder holding `page-N.jpg` (or `.png`) and `page-N-recognized.musicxml` for N = 1 to k, and optionally the `scan.json` of **Download everything**. The cases are private (the photos are of the owner's own copies) and are never committed: they live in a folder outside the repository that `DIGITUS2_SCAN_CASES` names.

Cases come in two ways, both from `research/omr/diagnostics` (its `README.md` has the details):

- `node research/omr/diagnostics/ingest.mjs [archive or folder ...]` turns a **Download everything** archive into a case named from the archive and the day it was recognized (`photo 22.57.16-scan.zip` becomes `photo-22-57-16-2026-10-28`). Without an argument it takes every `*-scan.zip` in Downloads; a scan already in the store, under any folder name, is recognised by its photos and recognitions and not added twice. Anything that is not such an archive is refused with the reason: no `scan.json`, a PDF page, a page without its recognition.
- `node research/omr/diagnostics/pull.mjs` copies the scans the scan service kept for an opted-in device (bucket `digitus2-scan-cases` of the project `digitus2-scan`, under `cases/`) with the owner's own gcloud login. It only reads; without gcloud, or while the bucket does not exist, it says so and copies nothing.

`pnpm verify scan:cases` (after `pnpm build`) replays every case through the built app with `apps/web/e2e/scan-cases.spec.ts`: the photos are uploaded as the reader's, the scan service answers each page with the recognition the case kept and what the scanner said beside it (the recognizer and warnings `scan.json` kept, else the page's `page-N-recognition.json` as the service kept it), and the review is read once it settles. The refusal is what says **Use for fingerings** is refused: the verdict line when it is said as an alert, and the alert under the buttons. A case is:

| Outcome   | When                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | The gate |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| usable    | **Use for fingerings** is enabled, and the score view draws fingerings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | passes   |
| explained | **Use for fingerings** is refused, the refusal itself names the page or the bar (or **Replace page N with a new photo**, **Photograph page N again**, or in a scan of one page **Replace it with a new photo**, is on screen), and it leads to a fix there: that retake, or at a bar it names, a correction offered beside a place to check, **Fix all** beside such a correction, the place's note, or **Show bar N** | passes   |
| failed    | anything else: a refusal nothing on screen says, one that names no page or bar (a place to check elsewhere does not name it, nor does a "Technical reason" line), one with no fix where it names, a usable score without fingerings, or a review that has not settled in 90 s                                                                                                                                          | fails    |

Each run writes `report.md` and `report.json` into the cases folder (one row per case: outcome and why, verdict, alert, places to check, fingerings drawn, seconds), the explained cases first, because they are the work list; the run before stays as `report-previous.json`. Each case folder gets the review's screenshot, `review.png`, and for a usable case the score view's, `score.png`. `node research/omr/diagnostics/report.mjs` prints the last report, and `--diff` names the cases whose outcome changed since the run before. Without `DIGITUS2_SCAN_CASES`, or with no case in it, the step reports SKIP, which is not a pass. The plain `e2e` step never replays the cases: the spec runs only when the step sets `DIGITUS2_E2E_CASES`.

A change to the scanner (the recogniser, the preparation of the photo, the export rules) is measured on every case before it ships. `node research/omr/diagnostics/recognize.mjs` reads every case's photos again through the scan service's own code, with a prepared homr, into a parallel store: the photos byte for byte, and for each page the MusicXML and the record the live service would answer and keep. `--photos research/omr/photos` adds each committed photo as a one-page case `photo-<id>`, turned upright as **Rotate** turns it, with `expect.json` holding the bars, staves, key and time read off the page. The baseline is recognised again too, not taken from the kept store, whose answers may come from older versions of the service. Then:

```bash
node research/omr/diagnostics/recognize.mjs --homr <toolchain> --cases <cases> --photos research/omr/photos --out <before>
node research/omr/diagnostics/recognize.mjs --homr <toolchain> --cases <cases> --photos research/omr/photos --prepare <module.py> --out <after>
DIGITUS2_SCAN_CASES=<before> pnpm verify scan:cases
DIGITUS2_SCAN_CASES=<after> pnpm verify scan:cases
node research/omr/diagnostics/report.mjs --against <before> <after>
```

`--against` lists every case with its outcome and fingerings on each side and the change: better, worse, same, or new. A case whose outcome drops (usable, explained, failed, then a page the scanner refused), or that the new run lost, is worse, and one worse case fails the comparison with exit 1. A page the scanner refuses is kept in its case as `recognition-failed.json` with the service's answer; the gate cannot replay it yet, and `--against` counts its case below failed. The outcome does not see a case that stays usable with fewer bars or a retake verdict, so the bars of each page (`rerecognition.json` in the store) are read against `expect.json`, and the verdicts in the two `report.md`. A page takes 5 to 12 seconds to recognise, and a case about 6 seconds to replay. The measurement of the photo cleaning is in `docs/research/omr.md` (Photo cleaning on the validation cases).

### Recognize and review

1. At the end of the library, choose **Open your own score**, or drop files onto the library. Choose up to 20 PNG or JPEG page images together (10 MB each), or one PDF (10 MB). Drag the thumbnails into reading order. Selecting a page shows keyboard-accessible move and remove controls and **Rotate 90° clockwise**. Rotate a sideways or upside-down photo before scanning.
2. Press **Scan this score**. Pages are recognised one at a time in the order shown; the status line reads "Page 2 of 5 · Recognizing…" for a batch. **Cancel scan** stops the job, and leaving the page cancels it too. A page that fails keeps the others; **Retry failed pages** resubmits only the failed ones. One black bar runs for the whole batch. It is timed, not reported: each page still to scan counts 35 s for a photo, and a PDF counts 16 s for each of its pages, as the app counts them in the file (the deployed service's warm times in `docs/scan-service.md`), plus 5 s while the scanner may still be starting. The line under it reads "About 70 seconds". The bar eases to about nine tenths by the expected time, creeps after that and fills only when the last page answers. A page that runs far past its own time moves the bar on only within half of what is left, so the pages after it still have room, and the bar never moves back, not even when a busy scanner makes the page wait. When another reader's score is being scanned, the status reads "Another score is being scanned. Yours starts as soon as it finishes." and the app offers the same page again every 5 s for up to three minutes, with **Cancel scan** working throughout. The scanner gives up on a page after three minutes.
3. Compare the original with **Recognized notation**: every page, pitches, rhythms, bar count, clefs, key and time signatures, and hands. The line under the title says what was read, for example "3 pages · 25 bars · both hands · 2 places to check"; when a page was read as one staff, a line was read only in part, a page could not be read clearly, or the pages did not join, it says so in one sentence with what to do, and **Scan again** carries the red. In a batch of photos whose sentence names a page, the red is on **Replace page 2 with a new photo** instead (**Photograph page 2 again** on a phone): the new photo takes that page's place and only it is scanned again (below). For an image batch, select a thumbnail to see that page's recognition alone, and select it again to return to the whole score; **Use for fingerings** always opens the whole score. On a phone the recognition comes before the original. For a PDF, step through the original with Previous and Next; the recognised pages may break differently. Text and tempo may be missing.
4. Correct what differs, note by note (Correct the recognition, below).
5. Press **Use for fingerings** to open the normal score view with fingerings and playback. The button is enabled once the recognised score, with your corrections, passes the strict import and renders. **Scan source and recognition** in the score view returns to the comparison, and so does the browser's Back button, without a new job.

**Download raw MusicXML** saves the recognised file untouched, without fingerings. A batch offers **Download combined MusicXML** and each page's file instead. Once you have corrected notes, **Download corrected MusicXML** saves the corrected copy too. The downloads sit under **Files for a notation editor**. A notation editor remains the tool for what the review cannot correct. The playback tempo of a scan may be the app's default, not a recognised tempo.

**Download everything**, the first of those downloads, saves the whole scan as one ZIP named after the first page (`photo 20.28.01-scan.zip`). Where the browser can share the ZIP, the share sheet opens with it (closing it does nothing); otherwise it downloads. It holds:

| Entry                          | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `page-N.jpg`, `.png` or `.pdf` | Page N exactly as the scanner received it, byte for byte (a rotated page as the rotated PNG)                                                                                                                  |
| `page-N-recognized.musicxml`   | The scanner's raw recognition of page N, when it has one                                                                                                                                                      |
| `combined-recognized.musicxml` | The raw recognition of the whole scan, as **Download raw MusicXML** or **Download combined MusicXML** saves it                                                                                                |
| `corrected.musicxml`           | The corrected copy, when you have made corrections                                                                                                                                                            |
| `scan.json`                    | The recognizer and when it ran, every warning of the review, the corrections, the build of the app, the browser (its user agent), and for each page its file name, type, size, recognizer, warnings and error |

It is the way to hand a scan to whoever maintains the app: what was photographed, what the scanner answered and what the app made of it arrive together, from a phone, in one tap. The build is also on screen, in **Recognition details** and at the foot of About. **Download everything** is offered once the scanner has answered for at least one page; a batch in which every page failed stays on the start screen with its errors and **Retry failed pages**, and offers no archive.

## Correct the recognition

Select a note or rest in **Recognized notation** to correct it: click or tap it (a tap close to a note reaches it), or focus the notation and press Enter for the first note, then the arrow keys. The selected note carries the screen's red and an ink frame (so the selection does not rest on colour alone), and a panel docks at the bottom of the screen with what the note is ("Bar 11 · right hand · A4 quarter note · tied") and what can change:

| Control                     | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Also read as                | When homr thought another pitch likely enough, at most two of its other readings, most probable first, each one tap: an ordinary pitch correction, recorded and undoable like the others. A reading is offered only at a probability of 0.01 or more, which on the confidence benchmark offers the right pitch for 53% of misread pitches and something on 2.3% of right notes (`docs/research/omr.md`, Confidence). Only homr's photos carry its readings. |
| Lower, Higher; ♭ ♮ ♯        | Moves the note one step on the staff, taking the key signature's sharp or flat for the new letter, or sets its accidental. A tied note moves with its whole tie chain, so a tie never joins two pitches, and later notes of that letter in the bar are drawn with the accidental they now need. Up and Down do the same from the keyboard; with Shift, a whole octave (a misread clef or ledger line).                                                      |
| Whole to 16th, Dotted       | Sets the length of the note, or of its whole chord. The rest of its voice moves with it; the other hand and the other voices never move, and a change that would move them is refused, as is a change to a note in a triplet or other tuplet. When the hands then disagree on the bar's length, the panel says so ("Bar 2: right hand 2 beats, left hand 3 beats, in 3/4.").                                                                                |
| Tied to next                | Ties the note to the same pitch starting exactly where it ends, writing both the sounding tie and the drawn one, or removes the tie. A slur is never turned into a tie or back. When nothing of that pitch starts there, the panel says so instead of guessing.                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| To left hand, To right hand | Moves the note to the other staff at the same time: the hand the fingering uses.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Remove                      | A duplicated chord note goes; a grace note goes; a lone note in a hand that is too long for its bar (a duplicate) goes and the rest of its voice moves up, otherwise it becomes a rest of the same length; a stray rest goes and the rest of its voice moves up into its place. A tied note asks for its tie to be removed first.                                                                                                                           |
| Make it a note              | On a rest the recognition put where the page has a note: the rest becomes a note of the same length, at the pitch of the nearest note on its staff, ready to be moved with Lower and Higher.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ‹ ›, Done                   | The previous or next note in reading order; Done or Escape closes the panel.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

When homr read the page, the panel also shows the photo at the note: a crop of that page, four staff spaces high and eight wide, with a thin black frame around the note's head. homr's own position for a note is only about a staff space from its head, so the frame is placed on the notehead found on the photo, at the note's pitch on its staff, nearest homr's position (the crop is centred there too); where no head is found, the crop is centred on homr's position. Each entry of **Places to check** shows the same crop, above its text on a phone and beside it on a PC. On the 16 clean corpus pages, the frame lies within half a staff space of the head for 96.0% of the notes homr read at the right time and pitch, against 32% at homr's own position (`docs/research/omr.md`). Pages read by Audiveris carry no position and show no crop.

The photo and the notation are linked ([issue 130](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/130)). While the whole score is reviewed, every note of **Places to check** carries a thin gray ring in both views: around its notehead in **Recognized notation** and at its head on the photo. With a mouse, pointing at a note in the notation frames its place on the photo in ink, and pointing at the photo frames the nearest note in the notation in gray; a click or tap on the photo selects that note, as a tap on the notation does. The photo shown follows the note in hand, so a note of page 3 shows page 3. The rings are gray and the frames ink: the one red stays on the selected note. Pages read by Audiveris carry no position, so their photo has no rings and pointing at it does nothing.

Every correction is checked before it is recorded, then the notation is rendered and imported again at once, so **Use for fingerings** follows what is on screen. **Undo** (or Ctrl+Z) takes back the last correction and says which, **Reset to recognition** removes them all (Undo brings them back), and **Review corrections** lists them in the order they were made ("Bar 2, right hand: quarter rest removed"). Notes are named by their position in the raw recognition, not by the strict import, so a recognition the import refuses can still be corrected until it passes. Correcting works on the combined score, including when a page's notes do not fit together; only a batch whose pages cannot be combined at all (below) is reviewed page by page and corrected in a notation editor.

**Places to check** appears above the notation when rules checked on the file itself find something: a tied note that ends before or after the next note of its pitch begins (the bar 11 tie of the test page, whose continuation arrived a quarter note late, is one), a tie without a matching note, hands that disagree on a bar's length, a hand longer than its time signature, or a repeat mark the score cannot carry: a repeat ending (volta bracket) in a score with no repeat sign, or a repeat sign that no later repeat sign closes ([issue 230](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/230); a scanner reads such a bracket into a page that has none, and the whole score used to be refused for it). Each entry selects its note, or the first note of its bar. A repeat-mark entry offers **Remove the repeat ending** (or **Remove the repeat sign**), which takes the repeat signs and brackets off that bar in every part and leaves every note where it is; it is a correction like the others: listed under Review corrections ("Bar 2: repeat marks removed"), undone, reset, and carried to the same bar when another page is photographed again. A tie the import could not join is one entry too, saying it is played as two notes: it offers the length that makes the note end exactly where its continuation starts, when a standard one does ("Make the A4 in bar 11 a dotted half so the tie reaches"), and **Remove the tie**; with two or more such offers, **Fix all N** applies the first offer of each place. While the strict import refuses the recognition, the list opens by itself and its first entry carries the screen's red instead of the unusable **Use for fingerings** (with no entry, **Show bar N** or **Scan again** does). They point at a place; they never decide the correction. On 250 of the library's curated engravings (8,101 bars) the tie rules flag nothing and the hand rule flags 12 bars in 7 pieces (0.15%), where the engraving lets one voice end early.

A misread time signature is corrected from **Places to check**, never from a standing control. When two or more bars of a part have hands that agree with each other on one length that runs past the time signature, the first of those entries offers the meter of that length, once per part: **Set 9/8 time from bar 1**. The length decides the meter: whole beats in quarters (4.5 beats of 4/4 gives 9/8, 3 beats gives 3/4), otherwise in eighths, otherwise in sixteenths, which is a guess the reader confirms against the page (six quarters could be 12/8). The change rewrites only `<time>`, in every part: from that bar until the recognition writes a different time signature. A restatement of the replaced meter, such as a joined page's opening, changes with it; no note, duration or bar moves, and a common-time symbol is dropped so the drawing shows the new meter. It is refused for a meter it cannot write (1 to 32 beats of a whole to a 32nd), a bar that is no longer there, or the meter already in force. The strict import reads a time signature without checking bar lengths against it, so a meter change cannot make it fail; a bar that still overruns the new meter stays under **Places to check**. On the owner's flat photo of Boëllmann Op. 28 No. II page 5, homr's 4/4 gave 14 places to check; 9/8 from bar 1 leaves one, bar 14, where the hands really disagree (9 and 6 beats). The torn photo goes from 7 to 1 the same way.

## On a phone or tablet

A phone or tablet scans exactly like a PC: the same **Scan this score**, the same scanner, the same review. What differs is the camera and the touch tools. On a device whose only pointer is a finger, the library offers **Photograph a printed score** on its own line under **Open your own score**, and the scan page offers **Photograph another page** for further pages. Photos are named by the time they were taken. The page tools (rotate, move earlier, move later, remove) are one row of symbol buttons sized for a thumb, and pages are put in order by selecting a thumbnail and moving it, because dragging needs a mouse.

### Rotation

Each rotation is computed from the original image, after its EXIF orientation, rather than from the previous turn. Rotating a page clears its old recognition; other pages and the order are kept. A failed or cancelled rotation leaves the current image usable. Rotation refuses images over 16 megapixels or 12,000 px on a side, and the rotated PNG must still fit the 10 MB limit. PDFs cannot be rotated.

### Image batches

The combined score appends each page's recognised measures in your order, keeping each page's time units, signatures and notation. It needs compatible parts and staves and an explicit starting context on each page. A page that the recogniser read as a treble part and a bass part, where the other pages hold one two-staff piano part, is merged into that layout first: its bass notes go to the lower staff, both parts move to a common time unit, and the part takes the other pages' name and instrument; no pitch, duration or onset changes. When no page holds a two-staff piano part, the same merge applies to a scan whose every page is two one-staff parts with equal bar counts, as long as at least one page opens with a G clef over an F clef; a single page is merged when it opens that way, and is otherwise left as it was. The part is then named Piano. Whenever the join pairs a treble part and a bass part, with or without a piano page among the others, Recognition details says the app paired the staves. Two parts that open with two treble clefs are not merged on their own, because the same layout can be two instruments. `apps/web/src/scan/join.metamorphic.ts`, run by three test files beside it, checks the rule on core library pieces cut into pages: every mix of split and paired pages joins to the same staves, notes and rests per bar, the same number of systems and braces, the same clefs, key signatures and time signatures drawn, and the same notes and fingers as the paired pages. A scan in which no page opens with a G clef over an F clef stays two parts, which it asserts too, naming each such case. The second staff is declared in the first of the opening `<attributes>` of the first measure (homr writes a split page's divisions and time there and its clefs in a second one), because Verovio reads the staff count only from that one and the engine reads it from any; declared later, the page is fingered for both hands and drawn as one staff. Repeat signs, volta brackets and jump marks are kept as read: they survive the join like any notation, the score plays them in performance order, and one the score cannot carry is a place to check with its removal (above). Only a page whose parts or staves cannot be made to fit the others blocks the combined score: a page read as two instruments (a staff not grouped with its system, so the parts hold different numbers of bars), a page with a different part or staff layout, or one without an explicit starting context. The verdict line then names that page and what was read there, in the reader's language, and offers to photograph it again: "Page 2 could not be combined with the other pages: it was read as 2 parts with 17 and 19 bars. Photograph it again, flat and with the whole page in view." Recognition details carries the same reason; page-by-page review and downloads stay available. Notes that do not fit together, such as a broken tie on one page or across a page edge, do not block it: the combined score opens with them under **Places to check**, and a broken tie is played as two notes until it is corrected. Combining never repairs recognition or fills in notes at page boundaries.

Slur and other span numbers are renumbered per page, so a slur never links across pages by accident; check continuations at page edges. MusicXML allows only 16 numbers per span type, so a batch whose spans would need more is refused, even below 20 pages.

### One page again

One bad photo in a batch does not cost the others ([issue 130](https://github.com/MarchalMaxim/digitus2/issues/130)). Select its thumbnail to see that page alone, then **Replace page 2 with a new photo** (on a phone, **Photograph page 2 again** opens the camera for one page). The new photo replaces the page, only that page goes to the scanner, and the pages are joined again. Your corrections on the other pages stay on their notes: each is found again by its page, its part and its place among that page's notes, and kept only when the note still reads the same; the corrections on the replaced page go with it, and so does any correction that no longer applies to the new recognition (a tie into the replaced page, say), each on its own, never the corrections after it. Until the new page is read and joined, what it replaces is kept with the saved scan, so leaving the page, a refusal by a busy scanner or a page that does not join loses nothing; rotating or replacing that page again keeps it too, while reordering, adding or removing pages ends it. When the verdict names a page (read as one staff, a line read only in part, read unclearly), the same action is offered for that page on the verdict line and carries the red.

### A page that could not be read clearly

homr says how sure it was of every note it read (the decoder's own probabilities, kept by a source patch; `research/omr/prototypes/homr-photo/README.md`). When, over the notes of a page, it was on average too unsure, the verdict line says so and what to do: "Page 2 could not be read clearly. Photograph it again: flat, evenly lit, the whole page in the frame." (for a single photo, "The page could not be read clearly"), and the red is on photographing that page again. The page's doubt is the mean, over its notes that are not rests, grace notes or tie continuations, of one minus the probability of the note as read; it is measured only on a page of at least eight such notes. The threshold is the one rule fixed before the measurements: just above every page as taken that homr read right on the confidence benchmark (`docs/research/omr.md`, Confidence). homr's confidence is not used to mark single notes: on a page it does not tell the misread notes from the right ones well enough. Pages read by Audiveris, and scans saved before homr wrote its confidence, have no doubt and are never named.

### Display of homr results

The pinned homr writes no beams. For display, the app infers conventional beat groups in simple and compound meters, keeps any explicit beams and skips ambiguous cases. These groups are not the source's beams: a run of six eighths can come back as three pairs. Real beam recovery has to link the image's noteheads, stems and beam strokes to the recognised notes, checked separately from pitch and rhythm; a page-specific grouping rule is not a fix. A render-only regrouping of simultaneous notes by voice also works around a Verovio import issue that displaced notes in time and stretched bars.

## My scores

Uploads are saved automatically in the browser's IndexedDB (database `digitus2.my-scores`), with page order, rotation, original and rotated bytes, the raw recognised MusicXML, your list of corrections and the recogniser's name. The raw recognition is never overwritten: the corrected copy is the raw recognition with the saved corrections replayed, and an entry with corrections is listed as **Corrected recognition**. Plain MusicXML and MIDI imports are saved too. **My scores** appears in the library when it has entries. Reopening an entry never contacts the scanner or restarts recognition. A multi-page result that cannot be combined still reopens page by page. Remove deletes an entry. A failed save is reported and the current work stays usable. **Share** on an entry sends its MusicXML (never the photo, the file name or anything else) to the shared library, where nobody else sees it until the owner approves it; **Withdraw** deletes it while it waits (`docs/shared-library.md`). Nothing is shared without pressing Share.

Browser storage belongs to one site and one browser profile, so a local preview and the GitHub Pages site have separate collections. Clearing site data or storage eviction removes it ([IndexedDB](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/IndexedDB_API), [storage limits](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)), so keep your own copies. Nothing is shared or published.

## When scanning does not start

The app keeps your pages in every case below, and **Scan this score** stays available.

- **"The scanner is busy with another score. Try again in a minute."** The app has already waited three minutes for another reader's score. Press it again later. In a batch, the pages after it wait too.
- **"Could not reach the scanner. Check your connection and try again."** The device is offline. Reconnect and retry; nothing was lost.
- **"The scanner could not answer. Try again later."** The device is online, but no answer from the scanner reached the app: the scanner is down, overloaded, or switched off for the rest of the month because its spend cap was reached (`docs/scan-service.md`). In those cases Google answers in front of the scanner, and the browser does not let the page read that answer, so the app cannot say which one it is. A network drop the device has not noticed looks the same.
- **"The scanner could not answer (error 500). Try again later."** The scanner itself answered with an error. The number is its error code.
- **"Recognition timed out. Try a smaller, clearer page."** The scanner gives up on a page after three minutes. A smaller crop, a flatter photo or the original PDF usually helps.
- **"You have scanned a lot in the past hour. Try again later."** Each reader has 30 recognitions an hour; the limit frees up as the hour passes. In a batch, the pages after it wait too.
- **"Cancellation could not be confirmed. The scanner stops by itself within three minutes."** The cancel request was lost; the page stops by the scanner's own time limit, and the scanner may say it is busy until then.

When the scanner answers but cannot read the page:

- **Staff resolution too low.** The message gives the measured staff-line spacing, for example six pixels. Use the full-resolution original, photograph a smaller area more closely, or use the original PDF. Enlarging a compressed image does not restore detail. homr refuses images over 25 megapixels.
- **Multiple scores or incomplete export.** Separate examples, footnotes and a curved page can confuse the recogniser. Try a single main music section or the original PDF.
- **Other recognition failure.** A flatter, sharper source may help.
- **Preview unavailable.** Open the original in another tab. The PDF preview does not run document scripts, and some unusual PDFs or image encodings do not preview.

## Known recognition errors and limits

Measured on the acceptance corpus (16 library pieces rendered as pages, each in four photo-like variants for homr and as a PDF for Audiveris; numbers and method in `docs/research/omr.md`): homr gets 97.0 to 99.5% of pitches and 86.8 to 91.0% of notes fully right (pitch, onset, length, staff and voice), Audiveris on the PDFs 98.7% and 86.7%. The errors to look for:

- **Missed ties**: 13 of the corpus's 47 ties have no tie or slur mark in homr's recognition at all, so the note sounds again instead of being held. Use **Tied to next**.
- **Rhythm**: notes read with the wrong value (sixteenths as 32nds, a stray rest), which **Places to check** often finds as hands that disagree on a bar's length.
- **A lost grand staff**: on a badly lit photo homr can read a piano page as one staff whose clef keeps changing. The verdict line then names the page and says to replace that photo and scan again; the hands are not separated, so use another photo or the PDF.
- **A line of music read only in part**: a staff cut short loses bars, a short stray staff adds some. The verdict line then names the page, says that bars are missing or added, and says to replace that photo and scan again, like the grand-staff one.
- **Beams** on photos are drawn by beat for readability, not read from the source; on PDFs Audiveris reads them.
- **Key and time signature** are inferred when a continuation page does not print them.

The scanner changes a homr export in two lossless ways, and says so under **Recognition details**: a measure that holds no music (homr writes the bracket closing a first ending as a measure of its own, which shifted every later bar) joins its neighbours, and a slur joining one pitch to its continuation is read as a tie even when homr gave the two notes different voice numbers. No pitch, duration or onset changes. **Recognition details** writes these notes in the reader's language, added up over the pages to one line each ("12 slurs between a note and its repetition were read as ties."), together with the small extra staves (ossia) the scanner left out; the scanner's own disclaimers are not shown, since the verdict line and **Places to check** say what was read and where to look.

On fourteen real phone photos (eleven WhatsApp-compressed, of five pages of Boëllmann's Op. 28 and a bound book page, and three Google PhotoScan images of Bach's Invention No. 13; results per photo in `docs/research/omr.md`), all were recognised, 12 pass the strict import and 11 have exactly the page's bars. What they add to the list:

- **Upside-down photos**: rotate them first. As taken, one lost its grand staff and another its key and meter; rotated, both read right.
- **Compound meters**: homr read 4/4 for 9/8 on both photos of one page, so every bar there is listed as longer than its time signature. **Set 9/8 time from bar 1** on the first entry corrects it.
- **Cut-off or torn pages** come back with extra bars.
- **Duplicates and wrong hands**: the book page's first photos showed a duplicated C5 and a bass E3 in the upper hand. Correct such notes with **Remove** and **To left hand**.

Further limits:

- Small inset examples are excluded before recognition and flagged in the result. A legitimate short inset passage, such as an indented coda, can look like an example, so compare the whole original.
- Ornaments can be incomplete and rhythms wrong on any page.
- Audiveris (used for PDFs) fails below about 10 px between staff lines and on skewed or curved pages.
- Text and tempo can be incomplete. A tempo like "= 100" without a recognised beat unit gives no tempo, and an app default tempo is not a recognised one.
- A recognition job finishing does not mean its notation imports as written. A tie whose continuation arrives late (the bar 11 tie of the test page, a quarter note late) is played as two notes and listed under **Places to check** until it is corrected.
- Multi-page books and browsers other than desktop Chrome are untested.

Source beam recovery for photos is an open request (#39).
